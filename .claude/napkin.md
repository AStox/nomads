## Domain Behavior Guardrails
1. **[2026-09-26] No slope may face away from the isometric camera**
   The map draws all terrain first and glyphs on top. That is only correct because ground never rises toward the camera (+x, +y) faster than it drops on screen.
   Do instead: keep the lift limiter in isoView (public/art.js) when changing heights: it scales the island so 99.5% of tiles pass and clamps the rest.
2. **[2026-09-26] The island is 47% sea, and its land is 37% grass, 52% forest, 11% rock**
   Measured over 24 seeds after the relief came down to 250-450 m peaks (median slope 6.5°). Crags now come mostly from hard bedrock bands rather than steepness. stock() sets thing densities per land tile, so they don't depend on how much of the map is sea.
   Do instead: re-measure over ~24 seeds after touching the generator (rock share drives ore and flint), and keep stock() odds per land tile.
3. **[2026-09-26] Water tiles are both sea and lakes**
   Only lakes freeze, and islets offshore or in lakes are cut off from the rest of the land.
   Do instead: use `sea(w)` to tell salt water from lakes, and `mainland(w)` or `landing(w)` to place anyone new.
4. **[2026-09-26] src/terrain and src/sim/world.ts import each other**
   Reading W or H at module top level in src/terrain throws "Cannot access 'W' before initialization".
   Do instead: read W and H only inside functions there.
5. **[2026-09-26] math/noise seeds keep only 16 bits**
   `simplex2d.create(seed)` uses `seed & 0xffff`.
   Do instead: derive sub-seeds with `Math.floor(rand() * 65536)` from the world rng.
6. **[2026-09-30] Every frame is drawn on the GPU from resident chunks; the CPU only draws live tiles**
   gpu.js keeps baked chunks as textures and turns them by depth with the maths of live.js warpOf. That only works while baked ground keeps z = 1.5H(u + v) + lev lp, sprites keep blit's z (anchor cz + rows up + bias, e = bias + foot + lift), and every object pixel's column from its anchor is recorded (the bake's `ax`, blit's B.ax). On the GPU path B is composed lazily in 32 px tiles: a pixel written without `B.need(x0, y0, x1, y1)` for its box first never reaches the screen. Headless fps read 60 while turns stalled for seconds on bakes.
   With WebGL2 the bake bakes neither slope light nor cast shadows (the workers' `realtime`; occlusion only). gpu.js lights every pixel for the hour from live.js's sky (skyFor: sun and moon positions, air mass, twilight, cloud): a row of the light table (16 steps of the key light at the fire's four levels, then the palette as drawn for flames) by its slope toward the sun or moon over the island's heights, less the shadow of the sun map and every shadow-casting sprite's rows (px.js shadowRows, live sprites' listed in compose); slopes turned to the sun or moon also take brighter palette tones (pal.js TONE). Never grade or light by the hour directly: the dark must follow the sun. Never let the exposure or a palette shift follow a light that fades faster than the sky (the sun's direct light, the dusk's glow), or shadows and slopes turned away brighten as it fades; the exposure follows the sun's height (live.js shadowShows). Overlays (labels, icons, smoke) take the open ground's light without shadow; a live sprite that gives light gets `lit[id]` and the palette as drawn, as flames do. Screenshot an hour with `?hour=` or `play.sim.hour`, under clear and cloudy weather (`play.sim.w.weather.sky`), and check dusk by stepping the hour from 19:30 to 23:00 in 5 minute steps: no patch of the frame may brighten.
   A chunk's `obj` is 1 for an object, 2 for ground already shadowed and 4 for still open water, whose tone on the water ramp times 16 sits in `ax` (a turn paints it again on the target grid; anything that recolours water must drop the mark, as the fire glow does). Sprite columns come from blit's B.ax, never an id lookup: ids wrap past 65000 objects a chunk. Tent pixels carry main.js MESH_PX and turn by their own depth. A bake is ~70% groundClass and the shore distance; water is painted once unless its loop moves (main.js WV).
   Ambient occlusion is world-space and live, so it stays the same at every zoom and bearing and follows the sim: gpu.js lightAt returns its step (3 bits) and the trees' share of it (2 bits, the tint) beside the light level, a turned pixel carries them in `b` above kind (bits 0-1) and water (bit 2), and aoMul takes them off the table's colour, scaled by how much of the pixel's light is the sky's (live.js sky A, K; fire lights it back). Its inputs: the ground's fold of the sky (uSun's green, traced once over the heights by setRelief) and the trees' cover (canopy.js: 3 m cells of crown discs, the sim's felled, grown and planted trees taken off and put back in `changed()`, the mean height of trees by uGrad's blue), read at a pixel's foot with a baked sprite's height over it from its depth. With it the bake leaves the hollows out (`aoGpu`); a thing that should shade the forest joins canopy.js, and `?ao=0` draws without it.
   Do instead: route any new depth writer, sprite bias or lift through those formulas (and `add`'s `e` for live sprites), call B.need before any new CPU drawing, and judge changes by `play.metrics.turns`, `live.lastMs` and frames with the CPU throttled 4x, with page screenshots at bearing 0.25, 0.5 and 0.75 (the page canvas does not hold GPU frames).
7. **[2026-09-27] The operator rejects recolored mocks and stepped, blocky terrain**
   Two rounds of looks dressed on one shared heightfield with blob trees read as the same picture recolored. In the isopixel style, terraced cliffs, per-tile rock cubes and tile-column shading read as "too cliffy" or blocks, and raw data rocks drawn one by one read as confetti.
   Do instead: vary the representation itself per mock (terrain as tiers, voxels, hexes, paper, glyphs or paint; models as sprites, cubes or toy pieces; 2D vs 3D; camera and renderer), sharing only demo/styles/world.js. In isopixel light slope tiles per vertex, put real cliffs only on sea headlands, shade rock in a few tones from a normal smoothed over about 3 tiles plus sparse hand texture, merge nearby data rocks into outcrops at far zooms, and check each pass in grayscale and a heavy blur.
8. **[2026-09-28] Every visible object is a sim object, and positions are continuous**
   The island's grown things live in the sim as typed arrays until touched (item 8); about 500 animals and the stocked things have float px, py (x, y stay their floors) and a spatial index; people walk about 2 m per 5-minute tick, so 300 m is half a day and a home 600 m off is out of reach before dark. The fine water field (waterAt) and groundClass() in src/terrain are the one truth for water and ground, shared by sim, renderer and inspector. At 19.2 km the tab holds about 1.05 GB with one bake worker and 270 MB more per worker (each grows its own island and copies the grown arrays), so it runs at most three.
   Do instead: never add decoration the sim does not hold, and route any new water or ground rule through waterAt or groundClass so drawing, walking and inspecting cannot disagree. Draw an object as one drawing at every zoom: trees are sprites.js tree(), anything else small is its real sprite shrunk (things.js object, px.js shrink); a hand-placed mini of its own size and colour is what made objects jump when the zoom brought the real sprite in. Plans must not lean on far places: ctxFor counts a home only within HOME_NEAR (150 m), a new shelter beyond that becomes home, and trips to a fire stop at FIRESIDE (60 m).
9. **[2026-09-28] Draw the generator's heights at 1.5x, and keep the camera off the baked maps**
   True slopes are p50 7, p99 34 degrees; 3.2x turned the eroded hills into needles, and 1x read flat. Camera state that went through each level's baked ground drifted and jumped on level switches.
   Do instead: keep EXAG at 1.5 (?exag= to try others) and every level on point samples of one surface; keep camera state continuous on its own smooth height field (demo/styles/play/camera.js) and derive per-level pixel snapping each frame without writing it back.
10. **[2026-10-01] Light is a derived field of the sim, and the renderer's sky and crowns are its twins**
   src/sim/light.ts works out lux at any point from the sky (src/sim/sky.ts), the skyline of the hills, the crowns of trees overhead and the fires and lamps near, and brightness is its log. People see, walk, work, sleep and hunt by it; `isNight` means the sun more than 6° down. sky.ts copies live.js's sun and moon, and light.ts's crown radii and CORE/EDGE copy canopy.js, so the picture and the people live under one sky. The per-tick memos (lightOn, the fire list) key on w.t, so a test that changes the scene inside one tick reads stale light.
   Do instead: change sunAt/moonAt, the crown shares or CORE/EDGE in both places together; give each scene in a test its own w.t; judge a behaviour change by tallying where agents spend their ticks by hour (rest, range, fire) over a few seeds against a `git worktree` of main, never by one run.
11. **[2026-10-01] The sun turns with the year, and the air is a derived field like light**
   sky.ts's sun takes the day as well as the hour (52° N, a 40-day year, midsummer on day 16, midwinter on day 36), and live.js copies it. src/sim/air.ts works out the air at any point (airAt, airOn) from the island weather (w.weather.temp, .speed, .wind) and the generator's seasonal fields (seasons[s].temp, pool, lee per wind direction), and people lose warmth by its wind chill. The generator's sun, temperature, snow and evaporation fields come from src/terrain/climate.ts, split into stage functions: one long function there ran 5x slower in Bun (JSC stopped optimizing it).
   Do instead: never test against fixed clock hours for light or dark (read them off sunAt for the test's day, as light.test.ts does); read local air through airAt, never w.weather.temp; keep hot generator loops in their own functions and time them.
12. **[2026-10-01] Bedrock, soil and fertility are fields; resources answer to them**
   src/terrain/geology.ts classes each cell's rock from hardness and a chemistry field seeded from hardness's noise (so no island changed shape); ground.ts derives texture, humus, pH, fertility and the soil's water capacity from it. Flint inside boulders, ore and clay follow the rock (world.ts grown, flora.ts scatter, ecology weathering); flint went from 45% of all boulders to 2 to 9% by island, so some islands wake their people 250 m from the nearest. Runtime changes to fertility live in w.fert (soil.ts, 25 m cells, sparse, saved) and fade 3% a day.
   Do instead: give new resources or plant rules a rock or soil property to answer to, never a rock name or a tile class; read fertility through fertilityAt so ash and dung count.
13. **[2026-10-01] Streams have a season: the mask is the bed, the water inside it varies**
   src/terrain/water.ts gives every stream point [x, y, mean m³/s, groundwater share, wettest season's m³/s]; fineGround's stream mask (RIVER class, riverSmooth) is the bed cut to the wettest season's width. The water in it now is flowNow(q, base, quick) (src/sim/streams.ts for the sim with recent rain in w.weather.wet; main.js groundAt for the drawing, by the season), and below TRICKLE the bed is dry. streamAt (flora.ts) finds the nearest stream point; openWater counts running streams. Erosion reads each rock's incise and creep (geology.ts) and the runoff its permeability sheds; changing those reshapes every island.
   Do instead: ask streamNow or flowNow whether water runs at a point, never the mask alone; bump VERSION when erosion or rock properties change.
14. **[2026-10-01] Plants and animals answer to fields and cues, never to a season's name or a tile class**
