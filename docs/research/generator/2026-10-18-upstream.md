# The upstream stages on the GPU (2026-10-18)

The crust, its erosion, the ice's scour and till, the flood and receivers and the hollows ran in `upstream` (`src/terrain/island.ts`, with `grid.ts`) on the CPU. They are now kernels: `src/gpu/wgsl/terrain-upstream.ts` (the WGSL, the units, the laws), `src/gpu/terrain/upstream.ts` (the host: `upstreamGpu(g, rand, watch?)` returns the same `Upstream`), `src/gpu/terrain/upstream.test.ts` (laws, run-to-run identity, one pinned hash a seed) and `src/terrain/island.test.ts` (the lake bound, restaged on the kernels). `generateIsland` calls `upstreamGpu`. The CPU `structure`, `erode`, `scourValley`, `drainedFloor` and `upstream`, and `flood`, `receivers`, `accumulate`, `blur`, `distance`, `sample` and the heap in `grid.ts`, are deleted; `island.ts` keeps the constants (each now exported, with its citation) and the `Island`, `Stage`, `Watch` and `Upstream` types. River polylines stay as they were (list work, in `water.ts`). Nothing of `upstream` stays on the CPU except the generator's first fifteen draws and the step's few numbers, below.

## What runs where

One arena buffer of 34 fields of 65,536 words (8.9 MB), one of atomic words (2 MB), one of parameters; every kernel on one explicit bind-group layout, compiled together once a device (about 0.4 s the first time). A run is two submissions and one read in between.

| stage | kernels | how |
|---|---|---|
| crust | `shape`, `hinge`, `rocks`, `dykes`, `rockfin`, `shore1`, `seafl` | simplex noise of `math/noise` redone in integers; the hinge is the k-th smallest land depth by 32-step bisection on the integer; dykes a thread each; the distance transforms are the CPU's two-pass chamfer run along the wavefront x + 2y, which visits each cell after the four it reads |
| erosion | `hinit`, 60 x (`uplift`, flood, receivers, depth sort, `area`, `implicit`, creep) | below |
| ice | `joints`, `valleys`, `blur0` to `blur5`, `till` | the layout is one thread (it is a prefix sum of draws), each valley is a thread walking its own cells with private arrays, the scour is `atomicMax` over valleys |
| ground it leaves | flood, receivers, depth sort, `hollow`, `sortorder` | |

- **Priority flood.** The flooded surface is the one fixed point of `f = max(h, least neighbour f + eps)` with the map's edge held at its height (eps above nothing makes it unique: a chain of supports cannot loop), so it is found by relaxing down from the edge and comes out the same whatever order threads run in. One round is a dispatch, a thread a cell reading its eight neighbours and `atomicMin`ing itself; the last round of a flood flags if it changed anything, and the host then runs the whole island again with twice the rounds. The first guess is 192 rounds (the floods need about 130 to 220): of seeds 1 to 48, seeds 19 and 47 need the second try, and a first guess of 224 settles all 48 and would cost every island 20 ms. A round is 7.9 us and the floods are about 90 ms of the 210. Tried and dropped: a tile of 16 x 16 cells and a ring of 8 in shared memory, 8 rounds a dispatch (settles within 28 dispatches on all 48 seeds, 45 us a dispatch): 207 ms against 210 for the whole stage, no faster, and more to get wrong.
- **Depth and the walks.** Pointer jumping over `to` (14 rounds of doubling, each a dispatch) gives each cell's depth, a counting sort files the cells by depth, and the drainage area, the response time and the implicit step walk the depths one workgroup of cells at a time, each cell reading only its receiver or the cells that drain into it, so every sum is exact and the order of a level's cells never matters. The first version did the jumping in one workgroup: 2.5 ms a call, 154 ms an island; as dispatches it is 18 calls and about 0.15 ms.
- **The implicit step** is `h' = h_r + (h - h_r) / (1 + f)` with `f = K dt sqrt(A) / far`, receivers first. `f` is held Q16 (K dt per rock Q16, sqrt of the area Q16, the diagonal's 1 / sqrt 2 Q32), and `1 / (1 + f)` as a Q32 quotient (Q8 when `f` passes 65,535, which mudstone does at 7e5): the rounding of `f` is under 1.5e-5 of itself.
- **The response time** (the sum of far / (K sqrt A) up each path, Q6 years in two words) comes back to the host after the first flood; the host makes `dt = 3 tau / 60`, the creep's substeps `ceil(CREEP dt / (0.2 CELL^2))`, the creep's share a substep and each rock's `K dt` from it with sqrt, ceil and the four operations of IEEE arithmetic, and writes them into the parameters. `tau` is 2.6e8 to 3.3e8 years; `dt` 1.3e7 to 1.6e7; 47 to 58 substeps.
- **Creep** is an explicit 5-point stencil, one dispatch a substep, ping-pong between two fields, a nonexistent neighbour (sea, the rim) read as 0 as the CPU's copies were.
- **The hollows** are components of the cells the flood raised by a centimetre or more, four neighbours, labelled by their least cell (minimum-label propagation with a pointer jump), `deepest` an `atomicMax` per label.
- **`order`** (kept in `Upstream`, read by nobody) is every cell once, each after its receiver, by depth and then by index (a bitonic sort of `depth * 65536 + index`); the CPU's was the heap's pop order, lowest flooded surface first.

## Units and scales

Z = Q12.20 metres (heights, depths, scour, till, the flooded surface; checked: nothing reaches 340 m, the blur adds 128 m to nine cells and sums them in a u32; the highest summit is about 190 m); M15 = Q17.15 metres (positions and lengths along a valley or a dyke; 37.5 m is exact); C16 = Q16 cells (the chamfer; 92,682 for a diagonal); noise coordinates Q24 and its terms Q30; the uplift's share Q24; the area whole cells; time Q6 years. Each field's unit is at its declaration (`terrain-upstream.ts`). The measured range on seeds 1 to 3 chose them: depth -20 to 1,730 m (Z holds 2,048), a height 0 to 190 m, an area up to 15,000 cells, `K dt` up to 3,500.

## Randomness, and what was done about the order

The CPU took `rand()` in a fixed order: three noise seeds, the tilt and stretch, the three faults (fifteen draws), then three a dyke (one count from the basement), then each line of each joint set (a draw for the line's step, three a valley). The host takes the first fifteen in that order from the stream it is given (the permutation tables are made by `simplex2d.create`, the integers of `math/noise`) and two more for a Philox key; the kernels read the rest as words `n` of one Philox4x32-10 stream, `philox(n, stream, 0, 0)` keyed by those two, **in the CPU's order** (dykes first, then the lines and their valleys; the layout kernel is the CPU's loops reading words). So which draw belongs to which dyke or valley is fixed by its place in the order, and nothing the threads do changes it; the TypeScript Philox twin is the oracle in the test. The cost is that a seed's dykes and joint valleys are not where the CPU put them, and the wind (the first draw after the stage) is now the eighteenth draw rather than the 1,172nd, so **every seed's island and climate are a new realisation**; the comparison with the CPU below was taken with a scratch seam that wrote the CPU's own draw words into the arena in place of the Philox stream (removed with the CPU code).

## Tolerances against the CPU, seeds 1 to 3

Compared stage by stage while the CPU functions still stood (scratch scripts, not kept):

- **Crust, exact in what is discrete.** Land cells identical (44,202, 46,160, 45,961), rock classes identical in every cell (dykes included, with the CPU's draws). Lift (U / U0) within 2.4e-7, sea floor within 4.2e-5 m, shore distance within 6.3e-3 m (the CPU's float32 chamfer sums). The noise itself, 6,000 random points up to coordinate 33: within 2.8e-7 of `simplex2d.sample` (rms 6e-8); sine and cosine of the azimuths within 1.6e-9.
- **Flood, receivers, area, depth, on the CPU's own fields** (erosion step 20, step 59, the finished ground): receivers identical in every cell, areas identical, flooded surface within 1.2e-6 m (eps 1e-6) and 2e-5 m (eps 1e-4, one float32 step at 190 m).
- **Response time on the same start**: within 1.9e-6 relative.
- **One erosion step from the CPU's state** (seed 1, step 20): height within 2.3e-4 m (mean 9e-5 m), every area identical. At steps 5 and 40 a few hundred and a few dozen receivers differ (near-equal slopes in flats), so the step differs by metres in those places.
- **Ice, till, hollows, on the CPU's own bed and draws**: scour within 1.2e-5 m, height within 1.5e-5 m, `drift` identical in all three seeds, till depth within 7e-6 m, flooded surface within 4.6e-5 m, receivers identical (one cell of 65,536 on seed 2), hollow labels, basins and open water identical.
- **The whole erosion is not comparable cell by cell, and the CPU is no better against itself.** It is a chaotic system: moving the uplift by one part in a million (12 micrometres a step, about the size of float32's own rounding at those heights) changes the bed by a mean of 2.1, 3.1 and 3.2 m (largest 17, 35, 38 m) on seeds 1 to 3, and the same CPU algorithm in float64 differs from its float32 self by a mean of 5.6, 5.0 and 5.4 m (largest 54, 76, 78 m). The kernels differ from the float32 CPU by a mean of 5.8, 7.7 and 5.2 m (largest 58, 79, 61 m) and from the float64 CPU by 5.8, 7.7 and 5.8 m: the same size. The statistics are what hold: highest summit of the bed (kernels 165.7, 163.3 and 145.6 m; float32 CPU 176.4, 158.9 and 163.0; float64 CPU 164.1, 146.0 and 151.3), land mean height (49.1, 46.9, 47.1 against 51.0, 46.3, 47.9; the float64 CPU gives 48.9, 46.0, 47.0), the 10th, 50th and 90th percentile of height, and the median and 90th percentile of slope (2.0 and 3.0 degrees on both). Over 24 seeds the median summit is 153.3 m against the CPU's 155.0, the calibration constant chi 11.4 (min 10.7, max 12.6) against 11.6.

## Anything odd in the CPU code (recorded, not fixed)

1. **The flood's hair is lost above about 32 m.** `flood(h, 1e-6)` stores `max(h, f + 1e-6)` in a `Float32Array`, whose step at 32 m and over is 3.8e-6 m, so a pit above that height is a flat of equal values with no receiver but at its pour point. The kernels hold the hair everywhere (1 unit of Z, 9.5e-7 m, and 105 units over the finished ground for 1e-4). This is most of why the float32 CPU's mean height sits 1 to 2 m above the float64 CPU's and the kernels'.
2. **The starting surface is all ties.** `shore * 1e-3` of a chamfer distance gives equal slopes along orthogonal and diagonal shortest paths, so the CPU's first receivers are decided by float32 rounding (45% diagonal on seed 1). In integers the rounding is systematic and the same field gave 73% diagonal, response times 0 to 18% longer and a wrong first network; the start now carries one unit of counter-based dither (a Philox word a cell, -1, 0 or 1 of Z, [design]), which restores 44.7% and 39.6% diagonal on seeds 1 and 4 against the CPU's 45.0% and 39.8%. The response times then equal the CPU's own on a start dithered the same way (to three digits on seeds 1 and 3 to 7), and are 0 to 13% below the float32 CPU's (median 4%) because that CPU's ties are broken in one direction first.
3. `Math.floor(CRYSTALLINE * (n - 1))` is a float product of 400 / 588; the kernel uses the exact `100 (n - 1) / 147`, which differs by one cell's rank when `n - 1` is a multiple of 147.
4. A valley's sill is the first cell of least `|at - 0.5|`; two cells equally far from the middle tie, and float `at = s / len` breaks the tie either way by rounding. The kernels compare the integers `|2 s - len|` and take the first.
5. The constants inside `structure` (the warp of 1.3 and 0.22, the coast's octaves, seven cells off the edge, a bend of 20 m) and the joint loops (reach 0.75, valleys 2000 + 3000 r, gaps 500 + 1500 r) were literals; the last are now `JOINT_REACH`, `VALLEY_LEN`, `VALLEY_GAP`, with `ERODE` (60 steps, 3 response times, 0.2, 1e-3) and `FLOOD_EPS`, tagged [design] where the CPU's comment did.

## Timings (Apple M3 Pro, one adapter; another agent was using the GPU, so read them as upper bounds)

- Upstream stage: 210 to 230 ms an island, against the CPU's 620 to 700 ms. The first call of a process adds about 0.4 s of pipeline compile.
- Island through `generateIsland`, seeds 1 to 3 warm: 0.98 to 1.01 s against 1.31 to 1.40 s with the CPU upstream, run back to back; `island-gaps --seeds 3` 1.05 s (cold first island included).
- Where the 210 ms goes: the floods about 90 ms (62 of them), the area walk 30 ms (0.49 ms a call), the creep 21 ms (2,820 substeps at 7.3 us), the rest the implicit step, the response time, the depth sorts, the ice and the readbacks.

## Not checked

One adapter, so identity across adapters rests on the integer rule and the unique fixed points (the flood, the labels, the scour maximum, the depth sort are all order-free), not on a second GPU. The browser's WebGPU was not run here; the WGSL uses workgroup-pointer parameters, workgroup uniform loads and an explicit pipeline layout, all core, and compiled under Dawn.
