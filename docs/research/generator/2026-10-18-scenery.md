# The 3 m ground and everything on it as GPU kernels (2026-10-18)

Plan Unit 7 of `docs/plans/2026-10-17-001-refactor-gpu-island-generator-plan.md`: `fineGround`, `scatter` and `clasts` of `src/terrain/flora.ts` are deleted and their work is `src/gpu/terrain/scenery.ts` (host) over `src/gpu/wgsl/terrain-scenery.ts` (the laws). A port, not a model change: the same physics, constants and densities as the CPU functions had (they are the file's own tables and notes), every number an integer between a buffer read and a buffer write, randomness Philox keyed by (seed, layer, quad, object).

## 1. What was ported, and what stayed on the CPU

| CPU function (before) | Now | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `fineGround`: the stream mask and the beds it cuts | a kernel a texel-quad (four texels of a row), strokes binned 16 texels square in segment order | `raster` |
| `fineGround`: heights, wetness, rivers, moisture, six covers (1,021 x 1,021 vertices) | one thread a vertex | `fine` |
| `scatter`: each plant's fit and compensation point at each cell (`envAt`, `fit`, `yearComp`) | one thread a cell | `table` |
| `scatter`: every tree, bush, reed, clay bed, mushroom, herb, fern, flower, tussock, loose stone of ore | one thread a quad (1,020 x 1,020), two passes over the same draws, ranks between | `count`, `rank`, `rows`, `scan`, `write` |
| `clasts`: boulders, stones and pebbles per cell | one thread a cell | `clasts` |

Stayed on the CPU, by design:

- **The river lines** (`fineRivers`): list work placed against earlier lines (water.ts); about 17,500 segments an island. They only carry each point's bankfull width and depth (`bankWidth`, `bankDepth`) on to the raster.
- **The samplers over the arrays** (`fineView`: `bilinear`, `bicubic`, `fine`, `heightAt`, `slopeAt`, `riverAt`, `riverWidth`, `dry`) and every reader (`waterAt`, `streamAt`, `groundClass`, `worldSlope`, `riverSmooth`): code the sim and the page call at a point inside a tick, which a GPU readback could not answer. They are closures over the typed arrays and make nothing.
- **The arrays themselves.** The sim reads `Fine` and `Scatter` on the CPU (people's lookups, `packGround`, `space.ts` stocking a tile, `Flora.attach`), so the kernels write the same typed arrays and the host reads them back. The cost is shown, not assumed: the objects are 13.6 million entries on seed 1 (not the 3.3 million of the napkin item, which is from before the Bornholm covers: hazel and berry alone are 10.5 million), 18 bytes each, 245 MB. Five 54 MB readbacks take 61 ms and turning the integers into float32 another 50 ms, of an island that takes 1.8 s and a stage that takes 0.25 s. The much larger change (readers on the GPU) is not made.

## 2. Units and scales

| field | held as | back to the CPU's unit |
|---|---|---|
| heights, depths, pH, water table, temperature, sun | Q16 i32 | metres, pH, C, MJ/m2 a day |
| covers, moisture, silt, clay share, fits, soil water | Q24 i32 | 0 to 1 |
| the fine field's wetness | Q24, always a multiple of 1/16 (four cells read between) | 0 to 1, exact |
| a compensation point (at most 2) | Q23 | 0 to 2 |
| the stream mask | a byte a texel, two byte planes (coverage, bed depth in cm) | `mask` as it was; `river` is the mask byte over 255 at the vertex's texel (the texel is 8 + 4 u across, exact) |
| a position in a quad | a 16-bit fraction (0.29 mm), as the island's metres in Q16 from its corner for the tile | the game's tile coordinate, an integer of ten-thousandths of a tile, over 10^4 |
| a size | micrometres (u32); a stem's height a whole number of millimetres first (`toMm`) | metres, the same float32 a CPU division stores |
| noise | coordinates Q20, gradients and result Q24 (flora.ts `GX`, `GY` as a table in the island buffer) | |

Heights are the cubic through the cells at quarter steps (Catmull-Rom with t = 0, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, two passes, each step rounded), the swell is flora.ts `noise`/`fbm` in integers (the hash is the same wrapping multiplies; the permutation is the top 12 bits), the beds are the mask byte over 255 times the bed's cm over 100 at the vertex's texel.

## 3. Tolerances against the CPU on seeds 1 to 3 (the deterministic part)

The islands are the ones the CPU functions were run on (dumped before they were deleted).

| field | difference | why |
|---|---|---|
| fine height | mean 7.7e-6 m; at vertices off the streams at most 3.6e-5 m; at most 2.9 mm at six to eight vertices a seed | the output's Q16 step; the stream's bed rounds with the mask |
| wetness | 0 (exact) | multiples of 1/16 |
| covers, moisture | at most 5e-7 | Q24 and one rounding of the noise |
| stream mask | 0.19 to 0.21% of the covered texels (96 to 131 of 51,000 to 63,000) differ by 1 in 255, none by more | distances are Q16 texels (1.5e-5), so a partly covered texel within 0.004 of a rounding edge falls the other side |
| river | at most 1/255 at those texels | the mask |
| a stem's height at the same fit and quantile (`treeHeight`, `bushHeight`) | 88% of 9,600 stems exact, none off by more than 1 mm, tails to a quantile of 2e-10 exact | the answer is rounded once, in base-2 logarithms (a Q16 exponential draw lost the lower tail: a stem of 2.8 m came out as 1 mm) |
| a crown's area (`crownShare`) | 6e-4 of a crown over 0.01 m2 | one rounding of the exponential of the log |
| noise, fbm | 6e-7, 3e-6 | |
| fit, compensation point per cell and plant | 9e-5, and 1.4e-3 of a point over 0.01 | Q24 curves; the point is a ratio of two sums |

## 4. The objects (the part that is not deterministic)

The CPU's `rand` order cannot be reproduced on a GPU where threads run in any order, so the objects are drawn again. The laws are kept exactly: the densities, `draw`, `treeHeight`, `bushHeight`, `crownShare`, the room a quad's stems fill and the lone-tree chance of the last one, the soil, water and stream tests (also at the place the game puts a thing, to a ten-thousandth of a tile), the tile order. What differs is which draw each object takes, and so every object's place, size and seed.

- **Counts by kind and species** against the CPU's, seeds 1 to 3 (seed 1: reeds -0.19%, oak -0.22%, aspen +0.08%, ash +0.41%, hazel +0.04%, berry +0.07%, pine -0.32%, grass +0.02%, bolete +0.63%, chanterelle -0.72%, lady fern -0.41%, clay -1.28%, loose ore +0.33%; the small ones, a few thousand each, within 1 to 2 of the noise of the difference of two draws). Total entries 13,633,859 against 13,629,972, 13,398,194 against 13,400,493, and 14,591,652 against 14,591,522.
- **Per tile (150 m)** the difference of the counts has a variance of 0.2 to 2.1 times the Poisson variance of the two counts together (trees 2.06, because a stand's stems fill a room and so are more clumped than Poisson; bushes 0.69; reeds 0.22; grass 0.13), and per 1.2 km block the same by species (pine 1.7, oak 1.8, ash 0.6, aspen 1.6, berry 1.5, hazel 0.15): no species or place is favoured.
- **Stems' heights** by species, the 1, 10, 50, 90 and 99% quantiles (seed 1), GPU against CPU: pine 6.70 14.58 27.37 41.06 42.00 against 6.71 14.57 27.37 41.13 42.00; oak 8.73 15.25 28.13 38.00 38.00 against 8.73 15.27 28.11 38.00 38.00; ash 10.33 15.05 24.31 32.05 38.96 against 10.27 15.06 24.31 31.99 38.84; aspen 6.03 10.21 20.06 28.19 30.00 against 5.99 10.19 20.04 28.19 30.00. The stand heights are plant-constants.md sec. 11's (del Rio et al., R650; the span by fit [design]; ash and aspen as shares of their maxima [gap]).
- **Where things stand.** No entry lies outside its tile's run; of 257,000 looked at, none under standing water or on a stream by flora.ts `waterAt` and `riverSmooth`, none of the stems off dry ground by `dry`, no tree on ground with no soil; a thing rounded into the next tile east or south (about 1 in 10^5, a position within 0.0075 m of a tile edge) takes its place in that tile's run.
- **The order inside a tile** is the CPU's: quads in raster order, each quad's things in the order the layers lay them (reeds, clay, trees, bushes, mushrooms, herbs, ferns, flowers, tussocks), with the east and south spills of the tile before in their quad's place. The ranks are a prefix over tiles (one thread a tile reads its 81 quads' counts by destination) and each quad's offset within a tile, so no atomics and no order of threads shows: two runs are the same bytes.
- **Quantised sizes.** A reed's, flower's or tussock's size, which the CPU kept at full float precision, is now a whole number of micrometres (1e-6 m); the store held them to a millimetre anyway (`Flora.attach` rounds to mm).

## 5. Clasts

The same law as before (`BOULDERS_HA` the count of boulders of 2 dm and more a ha by drift kind, the smaller classes as many times that as till's N(> d) ~ d^-2.9 has them) and the same contract for `deadwood.ts`: three `Uint32Array(N * N)`, pebble, stone, boulder, now from `await clasts(gpu, isle, seed, BOULDERS_HA, CLAST_DIM, bounds)` (`space.ts resident` and `deadwood.test.ts` await it). A cell's mean runs from 0 to 570,000 (pebbles on bare rock), past what Q16.16 holds, so a count is the sum of independent Poisson draws, one for each whole 8,192 of the mean and one for the rest: exact in distribution. Totals against the CPU's on seeds 1 to 3 within 4 x 10^-4 (pebbles 7.92 x 10^9), the squared deviation over the mean 0.99 to 1.02 over 42,000 to 45,000 cells, none on the sea, a lake or a drift that holds none.

## 6. Time

| | CPU (before) | GPU (now) |
|---|---|---|
| `fineGround` | 454 to 560 ms | 63 ms with its readback of 55 MB |
| `scatter` | 2,184 to 2,480 ms | 0.18 s: table 1 ms, count 30, ranks 3, write 90, readback 61, float conversion 50 |
| `clasts` | 9 to 11 ms | 6 ms |
| `await islandOf(1)` | 4.6 s | 1.77 s (seed 2: 1.35 s); 1.98 s and 1.54 s with the upstream stages as they were when the scenery was ported. `generateIsland` is 1.27 s of it (`scripts/island-gaps.ts --seeds 3`), the scenery stage 0.25 s, and the rest the sim's own pass over the tiles for dry ground (`world.ts`) |

The first run after a change to the kernels compiles them: about 2.5 s here (Metal's compile of the two big scatter modules; cached afterwards by the system), so a cold `islandOf` is no worse than the CPU's.

Memory on the GPU while it runs: the island's packed fields 17 MB, the fine buffer 71 MB, the index buffer 42 MB, and the five output arrays 4 bytes an entry (272 MB on seed 1), all let go when it returns. The write kernel binds exactly the eight storage buffers a stage may (`maxStorageBuffersPerShaderStage` is 8 here and in the browser's defaults): the island, the fine field and the index in three, the outputs in five, which is why the mask planes live in the fine buffer and the ore list in the index buffer.

## 7. Things to know, and odd things in the CPU code (recorded, not fixed)

- Cross-adapter identity is argued by the integer rule and run-to-run identity, not run: this Mac has one adapter.
- `clearAt`'s first line (`i0 < -1 || ... || ci(x0 - 1) < 0 ...`) never returns for any quad of the island, so only its ring of water cells and stream texels decide.
- `draw` returns the first name where a draw is exactly 0 (`x <= 0`) even if its weight is 0, a probability of 2^-32 a draw; `lay` with no room still draws once. Both are left out (a zero room returns at once).
- `tileAt` rounds to float32 before the thing's place is checked again (`back`), a shift under 0.6 mm at the island's far corner: the GPU checks the place of the integer tile coordinate, exact.
- No heather or harebell is ever laid on islands 1 to 3: the pH niche and the water table put them nowhere (not a fault of the port: the CPU laid none either).
- The napkin's "3.3 million things, 870,000 trees" and the stems' "1.36 million, 77 MB" are older islands: seed 1 now holds 2.19 million trees and 10.5 million bushes.
- The tile tests read islands the generator makes now: when the generator's own stages change the islands, `GOLDEN` in `scenery.test.ts` is re-recorded and its comparisons with the CPU numbers are skipped (they are keyed to the island by a hash of its fields).
