I've turned every constant in `ground.ts` and the soil-water parts of `growth.ts` into a row below. Each row has the current rule, a replacement law, a source and a tag. No repository file was changed. Wherever the answer depends on climate, soils or geology I used Bornholm (with Gotland as backup) as you asked. The one big conclusion is that the old pTree, moor and shrub rules don't fit the Baltic setting, so they should be dropped rather than recalibrated.

**Tags.** [cited], [derived], [design], [gap] and UNVERIFIED are as in the brief. **ⁱ** marks a source I saw only as an abstract or search extract; I read every other URL listed.

## Answers to the questions

**Soil depth (P24).** Mass balance is dh/dt = (ρr/ρs) W e^(−h/h\*) + D∇²z.
- On convex ground the steady state has a closed form, (a) in the table, with no time march. If the log's argument is ≤ 1, the cell is bare rock.
- On flat or concave ground there is no finite steady state, so use the time-limited form (b). With h\* = 0.5 m, W = 0.026–0.096 mm/yr, t = 16.6 ka and ρr/ρs ≈ 2 (UNVERIFIED), it gives about 0.5–1.0 m.
- On Bornholm, regolith is mostly glacial drift: thin till over basement, absent on ice-scoured knobs such as Hammeren, and thickest in fracture valleys. A [design] drift-thickness field must be added.
- E19 fitted γ = 2.26 m at an English site, so h\* for NW Europe is uncertain.

**Beach sand (P25).**
- Fetch law (CEM eq. II-2-36): g·Hm0/u\*² = 4.13×10⁻²·(gX/u\*²)^½ and g·Tp/u\* = 0.651·(gX/u\*²)^⅓, with u\*² = C_D·U10² and C_D = 0.001(1.1 + 0.035·U10).
- Worked check: U10 = 10 m/s over X = 100 km gives Hm0 ≈ 1.6 m and Tp ≈ 4.8 s, consistent with Arkona.
- Runup (Stockdon, when ξ ≥ 0.3): R2 = 1.1[0.35β(H0L0)^½ + ½(H0L0(0.563β² + 0.004))^½]. Hs = 4 m, Tp = 8 s and β = 0.08 give about 1.6 m.
- Missing inputs: fetch for each bearing (the generator has no geography outside the island), storm-surge levels, a sand source, and dune width.

**Peat (P26).**
- Clymo: M(t) = (p_c/α_c)(1 − e^(−α_c t)), with p_c ≈ 50 g m⁻² yr⁻¹ and α_c ≈ 10⁻⁴ yr⁻¹. This cross-checks with Finnish raised-bog carbon accumulation of 26.1 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹ (about 52 g of dry mass).
- Ten thousand years gives about 316 kg/m², roughly 3 m at an assumed bulk density of 0.1 g/cm³ (UNVERIFIED).
- Peat depends on the water table, not rainfall, on Bornholm; the bogs are basin mires, mostly in Almindingen.
- Structural change: peat becomes a depth in m, and needs an initiation age as a [design] input.

**Humus and SOC (P27).**
- RothC rate constants k: DPM 10, RPM 0.3, BIO 0.66, HUM 0.02 yr⁻¹.
- Modifiers: temperature a = 47.91/(1 + e^(106.06/(T + 18.27))); moisture b from the topsoil moisture deficit, whose maximum is −(20 + 1.3·clay% − 0.01·clay%²) mm for 0–23 cm; cover c = 0.6 vegetated, 1.0 bare.
- Clay split: x = 1.67(1.85 + 1.60·e^(−0.0786·clay%)), and 1/(x+1) of each decomposition goes to BIO + HUM, split 46:54.
- DPM/RPM input ratio: 1.44 for crops and grass, 0.67 for unimproved grassland and scrub, 0.25 for deciduous woodland.
- Inert pool IOM = 0.049·TOC^1.139.
- Steady state in closed form, with m = mean(abc), e = 1/(1+x) and input I: DPM = f·I/(10m), RPM = (1−f)·I/(0.3m), BIO = 0.46·e·I/((1−e)·0.66m), HUM = 0.54·e·I/((1−e)·0.02m).
- I is the plants' NPP, from growth.ts.

**pH (P28).** Ulrich buffer ranges by pH: carbonate 8.6–6.2, silicate 6.2–5.0, exchanger 5.0–4.2, Al 4.2–3.8, Al–Fe 3.8–3.0.
- Carbonate present (limestone; Gotland, Bornholm's Komstad Limestone): 7.8–8.8 indicates CaCO₃ (SLU).
- Base-rich silicate (dolerite, mudstone, Baltic clay till): silicate range. Bornholm is in BM92's region 2c, where about 80% of soils are luvisols, neutral to slightly acid.
- Base-poor rock (granite, gneiss, sandstone, dune sand): exchanger range in mineral soil, about 5.2–5.4 per SLU. Under mor it falls to about 4: German forest organic layers average pH 4.6 (10th–90th percentile 3.8–5.8, M19); SLU gives about 4 for mor.
- Danish percolation is 150–400 mm/yr, PET about 550 mm (BM92).
- [gap]: CaCO₃ content and base-cation weathering rate per rock, which should replace `base`. German weathering averages about 1.0 keq/ha/yr (M19).

**Fertility and water capacity (P29, P30).** Fertility becomes N supply (see the row for line 88).
- Saxton & Rawls: θ1500t = −0.024S + 0.487C + 0.006OM + 0.005(S·OM) − 0.013(C·OM) + 0.068(S·C) + 0.031, and θ1500 = θ1500t + (0.14·θ1500t − 0.02).
- θ33t = −0.251S + 0.195C + 0.011OM + 0.006(S·OM) − 0.027(C·OM) + 0.452(S·C) + 0.299, and θ33 = θ33t + 1.283·θ33t² − 0.374·θ33t − 0.015.
- S and C are weight fractions; OM is in percent. Peat needs measured retention [gap].
- FAO Table 19 checks: sand 0.05–0.11 m³/m³, sandy loam 0.11–0.15, loam 0.13–0.18.

**Cover (P31).**
- There is no thermal treeline on the island. Paulsen & Körner need a season of ≥ 94 days at daily means ≥ 0.9 °C with a season mean ≥ 6.4 °C; Bornholm's top is 162 m.
- Denmark's natural vegetation is deciduous forest with a beech climax (BM92), so trees should win wherever `keeps()` holds and the niche axes (soil, wet, salt, wind) allow.
- Resolve the forms by light. Order them by `tall`; each form gets light `through(cover above)` and survives if `thrive(light, comp) > 0`. This needs per-species niche fits inside `ground()`, or the cover moved into niche.ts.
- "moor" and "harsh" disappear. Open ground then comes from disturbance (fire, herds) and wet or sand soils.

## Structural changes
1. **New inputs:** drift thickness; deglaciation age; a per-rock soil production rate W (m/yr), CaCO₃ and weathering rate (replacing `hard` ramp, `base` and `feed`); peat start age; fetch or offshore wave climate per bearing, plus surge level; wet days per season (for climate.ts); bankfull Q and width (from water.ts); the Zr and p of each form.
2. **Changed fields:** `soil` becomes drift + regolith; `peat` becomes a depth in m; `humus` becomes SOC (or organic matter %); `fertility` becomes kg N/ha/yr. That last one breaks the 0..1 assumption in sim/soil.ts `feedRate`, herds.ts, fish.ts and niche.ts's `fert` axis.
3. **Removed:** `harsh`, `moor`, pTree, pShrub, pMarsh, pGrass and the cascade. tree, shrub, grass and marsh are derived from niche fits plus light.
4. **`holds()` signature:** gains organic matter and Zr; its caller at sim/soil.ts:72 must change too.
5. **Cycle:** litter → SOC → water capacity → cover → litter. Iterate a few passes, or use the NPP of whichever form wins [design].
6. **Sync with GenWater:** water.ts `BUCKET = 100` competes with `holds()`.
