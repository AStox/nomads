## 2. Where the species' climate limits lie

PLANTATT's Tjan/Tjul are **means over each species' range, not limits**. Every species here except gorse:
- extends from below 45 °N to above 60–65 °N in Europe [PA NEur/SEur], and
- occupies most of the 2,805 British squares.

The island spans about 160 m of relief, so its internal temperature range is about 1 K. That range, and Bornholm's climate as a whole (Jan ≈0, Jul ≈16–17 °C; numbers from GenClimate, here **[UNVERIFIED]**), lie well inside every one of those envelopes.

ESC agrees for the four trees (Fig 9): all are V at an accumulated temperature above 5 °C of 1475–1800 day‑degrees. Bornholm's value is roughly 1600 **[derived** from the monthly means; to be checked against GenClimate**]**.

**Gorse** is the exception. [FEIS] says it does best where every monthly mean is above freezing and the coldest month's mean daily minimum is above 2 °C. It was severely frost‑damaged at −20.8 °C. Bornholm sits in gorse's 55–60 °N limit band, and the species is not native in Denmark **[UNVERIFIED]**. It belongs on this island only marginally, if at all.

## 3. Mapping Ellenberg values onto the code's physical axes

- **R → pH.**
  - Trees: ESC Table 8 gives the topsoil pH (H2O, 0–25 cm) for each SNR class: VP 3.0–4.0, P 3.0–4.0, M 3.0–5.0, R 3.0–5.5, VR 4.5–7.5, Carbonate 7.5–8.5. ESC Fig 14 gives each tree's suitability by class (VP P M R VR Carb):
    - pine: S V V S S U
    - sessile oak: U S V V V U
    - pedunculate oak: U S V V V U
    - ash: U U S V V S
    - aspen: U S V V V U
  - Non‑trees: ESC Table 15 converts R+N into SNR: <5.7 VP, 5.7–7.7 P, 7.7–9.7 M, 9.7–11.7 R, >11.7 VR; Carbonate is R > 6 with N < 4.5. This is **[derived]** and gives:
    - heather 4 → VP
    - bracken 6 → P
    - gorse 8 → M
    - sorrel 9 → M
    - yarrow, buttercup, daisy, lady fern 10–11 → R
    - bramble, hazel, clover, mint, poppy, reeds 12–13 → VR
  - A continuous R→pH regression (Schaffers & Sýkora 2000; Ertsen et al. 1998) is non‑linear [CH]. Its coefficients were not obtained: **UNVERIFIED**.
- **F → water table.** ESC Table 5 gives the mean winter water‑table depth for each SMR class: Fresh 0.8–1.0 m, Moist 0.6–0.8, Very Moist 0.4–0.6, Wet 0.2–0.4, Very Wet <0.2 m. Freely drained classes sit below 1 m.
  - ESC Fig 13 gives each tree's suitability by class (VW W VM M F SD MD VD):
    - pine: U U S V V V V S
    - sessile oak: U U S V V V S U
    - pedunculate oak: U S V V V S U U
    - ash: U S V V V S U U
    - aspen: U S V V V S U U
  - So the replacement for the `wet` axis is a curve over **water‑table depth in m**. For example, ash: unsuitable above 0.2 m, suitable 0.2–0.4 m, best below 0.4 m. **[derived]**
  - ESC itself says F (Hill‑Ellenberg) predicts SMR poorly in woodland (p.24). For the open‑ground herbs, read F against the same depth bands only as a rough guide, **[derived/UNVERIFIED]**: F7 means "constantly moist, not wet", F9 "water‑saturated", F10 "shallow water" [PA Table 10].
- **N → fertility.** N tracks productivity or standing crop rather than any soil chemistry ([PA] p.16 text; Ertsen et al. abstract). On this island, productivity comes out of growth.ts, so using N would be circular.
  - What is needed is an independent nutrient‑supply field, for example N mineralisation in kg N ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹. That is **[gap]** and a structural change.
  - In the meantime, the SNR classes above are the cited ordinal axis.
- **S → salt.** Ellenberg's S classes in % Cl⁻ of the soil solution: S2 0.05–0.3, S3 0.3–0.5, … S9 >2.3 **[UNVERIFIED**, secondary source only**]**.
  - Ertsen et al. 1998 [KfC]: S0–1 species decline a little above 100 mg/l Cl⁻ in groundwater and steeply above 1000 mg/l **[cited]**.
  - Every tree, shrub and herb here is S0 except yarrow (S1), reeds (S2) and *Festuca rubra* (S2).
  - This needs salt in real units (deposition or root‑zone Cl⁻ in mg/l) instead of the 0–1 spray value: **structural change**.
  - No per‑species salt‑**spray** tolerance was found **[gap]**.
- **L → light.** The light scale's definitions [PA Table 9]: L3 means mostly <5% relative illumination; L5 >10%; L8 rarely <40%. For trees the value refers to the sapling stage.
  - Light is no longer a niche axis for green plants (growth.ts `thrive`).
  - Use L as a **validation target**: an L8 species' compensation point from `yearComp` should come out at about 0.4 of open sky, an L5 species' at about 0.1 **[derived]**.

## 5. Structural flags
1. **Remove** the `water` axis for green plants, and remove `evergreen`, `vigor`, `lottery` and the tree `tall` duplicate.
2. **Change** `wet` to water‑table depth in m.
3. **Change** `salt` to Cl⁻ in real units.
4. **Add** a per‑species rooting depth that feeds the soil bucket.
5. **Add** an independent nutrient supply in kg N ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹, or keep `fert` as the ordinal ESC SNR class.
6. **Add** a winter minimum temperature, needed only for gorse.
7. **Fungi** need a host‑tree field: boletes and chanterelles are ectomycorrhizal. **[gap]**, no data gathered.
8. **Bornholm analogue:** gorse is out of place there (§2). *Q. robur* stays the oak (*Q. petraea* figures are in the csv).
