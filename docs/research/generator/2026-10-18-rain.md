# The rain: Smith and Barstad's orographic rain against Bornholm's gauges (the climate gap: rain 850 against 672 mm, interior minus coast +193 against +67)

Chain: fire-constants.md sec. 34 gap 1 ("the orographic rain is far too strong ... the clip ... not run down"; the dome experiment) and the handoff's open gaps. Code: `src/gpu/terrain/rain.ts` (host, the constants below) and `src/gpu/wgsl/terrain-rain.ts` (kernels), tested by `src/gpu/terrain/rain.test.ts`; the derivation of one constant, `scripts/rain-freezing.ts`. The CPU stage `src/terrain/climate.ts` `orographic` is unchanged: it is the unfixed reference (1000 s delays all year) until the cutover. References R1320 to R1329 are this file's; the parent merges pointers into terrain-constants.md and fire-constants.md.

## Finding

1. **The port is faithful: no units, weighting or constant bug in the GPU kernel or in `climate.ts orographic`.** An independent float64 version of the theory, written from the paper (R1320 eqs. 12 and 49) and kept in `rain.test.ts`, reproduces what the paper publishes: the triangle ridge of Fig. 3 (500 m, U 15 m/s, T0 280 K, N_m 0.005, H_w 2500 m, tau 1000 s both) peaks at 2.92 and 2.30 mm an hour for half widths of 15 and 40 km (paper 2.96 and 2.33; raw upslope 14.93, paper 15); Table 5's efficiencies for the Gaussian ridge (H_w 3 km): dynamics 84% (82), and the total for tau of (0, 250), (0, 1000), (0, 2000), (1000, 1000), (2000, 2000) s 79, 56, 40, 43, 26% (79, 55, 39, 42, 25); Tables 1 and 2 for the circular Gaussian hill over the upwind half, in three dimensions (so the lateral waves and both branches of m), at H = N_m H_w / U of 0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, 10: total 1.01, 0.87, 0.63, 0.35, 0.07 (paper the same to the digit), centreline 0.95, 0.65, 0.43, 0.24, 0.05 (paper 0.92, 0.63, 0.41, 0.22, 0.04). On the island's own inputs the CPU function and the GPU kernel both agree with that float version to under 0.004 mm of a season's 80 to 370 mm. The units are right (3600 s for mm an hour; share = wet rose x rain hours, the background P_BG x wet / hours per rain hour; C_w = rho_v Gamma_m / gamma with the vapour density of the layer's air at the season's warmth and measured humidity, 0.0062, 0.0122, 0.0115 and 0.0060 kg/m3 by season, inside the paper's 0.001 to 0.02 range). One thing the checks did find, in my own first draft of the fix below: the kernel's `lag` (1 / (1 + i sigma tau)) holds x = sigma tau only up to 255 in a u32, so delays over about 1260 s on the fastest wind overflowed (7.8 mm of error until `lag` held x at 254, where the factor is under 0.4%). That limit did not bind at the old 1000 s.
2. **The doc's named cause, the clip, is not the cause.** On the real Bornholm (below) the season rain summed with the cut at none and without it is the same: 922 and 922 mm over land, 635 and 634 over the whole grid (the cut changes the grid's mean by under 1 mm, the unclipped mean being the background itself, as the theory's mean perturbation is nothing). The background (634 mm) is larger than the theory's descent nearly everywhere, so the cut hardly acts. The excess is the theory's own prediction: its rain from uplift over land alone is +287 mm on average, and the equal and opposite part falls over the sea.
3. **It is not the island's relief.** Real Bornholm (Copernicus GLO-90 heights, R1326, 583 km2, peak 169 m on the 300 m grid) under the same theory, wind and rose gives, in DMI's 10 km cells (R485, R1328): the nine coastal cells 765 mm (DMI 634), the four interior ones 936 (701), contrast +171 (+67). The generated islands behave like it (758 and 945, +193), a little less orographic than the real one in the 75 m bands (below).
4. **It is not the inputs.** The theory's inputs are the air's own: lapse rate, moist stability, vapour density, wind. From the 1391 radiosondes of Greifswald and Leba in Hammer Odde's wet hours (R495) the environment's lapse rate is 5.18, 5.82, 5.34 and 4.80 K/km by season, the moist adiabat 6.69, 5.34, 5.60, 6.54, so N_m (N_m^2 = g (Gamma_m - gamma) / T, which reproduces the paper's 0.005 from its 5.8 and 6.5 K/km at 280 K [INFERENCE]: the appendix was not read) is 0.0075, about 0.001, 0.0031 and 0.0079 s-1, the vapour weighted wind to 3 km 13.2, 11.0, 11.5, 12.7 m/s against 13.6, 11.9, 12.1, 12.8 at 850 hPa. Using those in place of the paper's single set of numbers (with H_w from the lapse rate by the Clausius-Clapeyron e-fold and C_w's ratio Gamma_m / gamma) leaves Bornholm's cells at 760 and 945 (+185), and N_m alone at 779 and 1007 (+228): the inputs measured give the same rain or more.
5. **It is the cloud delay.** The model's pattern is right and its amplitude is not. Seventeen DMI gauges on Bornholm (R1327; 1991 to 2020, each year relative to the island's municipal total) rise by 106 mm per 100 m of station height (r 0.82). The model at the same places rises by 368 mm per 100 m (r 0.92 with the model's own heights), and its rain at the gauges correlates with the gauges' at r = 0.72 with the model's orographic part 4.5 times too large (slope of observed on model 0.22). Of everything in the theory the cloud delays tau_c and tau_f move that amplitude most: on a 150 m dome of 9 km radius, tau of 500, 1000, 2000, 3000 s (both) gives a hill-minus-ring contrast of 1062, 417, 123 and 57 mm, against N_m 0.002 to 0.015 s-1 (420 to 163), H_w 1500 to 3000 m (518 to 378), the wind at 0.5 (295) and the humidity at 0.5 (208). Barstad and Smith say the same of their own theory: tau is "the primary controller of point precipitation amplitude", and cannot be measured, only inferred from gauges (R1321).
6. **The delays depend on the phase of the water, which the single 1000 s of the paper's mountain example ignores.** Barstad and Smith put it in a sentence about their own fits (R1321 sec. 7): the cold Alpine case (IOP8) "might have a longer drift of hydrometeors, and shorter conversion time, and the IOP2b just the opposite". Roe (R1323): growth by condensation and coalescence alone takes 30 to 60 minutes; condensation accreted onto falling hydrometeors reaches the ground comparatively fast; snow falls at about 1 m/s. Jiang and Smith (R1322): conversion is nonlinear and has a threshold, slow where the condensation is weak (a low hill's is) and fast where it is strong, and the hydrometeor's drift matters most for small hills. So each season takes its own delays from the share of its condensation above the freezing level (the law below), every number from one of those sources. The effect is measured against the gauges, not fitted to them (the delays the gauges alone would pick are longer, below).

## The law

Smith and Barstad's transfer, unchanged (R1320 eq. 49): P^(k, l) = C_w i sigma h^(k, l) / ((1 - i m H_w)(1 + i sigma tau_c)(1 + i sigma tau_f)), sigma = U k + V l, m from eq. 12, the rain of a rain hour p added to the background and cut at none (eq. 40), summed over the wet rose (the season's rain hours x each direction's share). The one change: tau_c and tau_f are each season's own.

- Ice share of the season's condensation, f = mean over the wet-hour soundings of e^(-z_f / H_w), z_f the freezing level above the sea (the theory weights condensation at height z by e^(-z / H_w), R1320 eq. 10, so this is the share of it above z_f; 0 where the sounding stays above freezing; 1 where the air at the ground is below it). `scripts/rain-freezing.ts` computes it.
- tau_c = f TAU_C_ICE + (1 - f) TAU_C_WARM: the cold-cloud conversion time where the condensate is ice, the warm-cloud time where it is liquid.
- tau_f = (1 - f) TAU_F_RAIN + f H_w / V_SNOW: the mean fall time of a particle formed at a height with the condensation's weight, falling as rain below the freezing level and as snow above: H_w (1 - f) / v_rain + H_w f / v_snow, with the paper's 1000 s for the first (H_w at 2.5 m/s).

Integer form: unchanged. The delays only change two of the six words a season's transfer takes (`y0c`, `y0f`, sigma times the delay for each unit of wave index, Q26: 1.5 to 2.4 against 0.97 to 1.1 before), and `lag` holds x = sigma tau under 254 (Q22 in an i32; a u32 holds 1 + x^2 in Q16 to x of 255).

## What is closed, partly closed, open

- **Closed:** the unexplained part of the gap. The kernel is faithful, the clip does nothing, the relief and the measured inputs do not explain it, and the cloud delay does.
- **Partly closed:** the orographic amplitude. On the generated islands inland minus coast falls from 193 to 75 mm (Bornholm 67), the land mean from 850 to 743 (672), coast 758 to 707 (634), inland 945 to 779 (701). The real Bornholm, which is more orographic than the generated islands (a larger plateau), goes from +273 to +120 in the same bands and from 368 to 197 mm per 100 m against the gauges' 106: about half the excess is removed there, not all. The pattern improves (r 0.72 to 0.86).
- **Open:**
  1. **The delays the gauges alone would pick are longer than the cited law's** (equal tau of 2000 to 3000 s, the warm seasons longest, against totals of 3500 to 3600 s now): fitted, they would make the rain 1.0 of the gauges' amplitude, but nothing cited gives a warm-season total of 5600 s. The paper's authors infer tau from gauges, which would be this retrieval; it is not done here because the operator's rule forbids tuning a constant to a failing check, and the state of the question (the threshold of R1322, convective rain in the warm seasons, below) is not settled by a number in a source.
  2. **The threshold.** Jiang and Smith's nonlinear conversion gives no rain at all below a critical condensation rate that rises as the hill falls (about 500 m for a 10 km snow hill in their set-up); a hill of 150 m would be under it for snow. Their collection factor is for cold snow and no rain value is given, so it is not implemented. It points the same way as the delays.
  3. **Showers.** In the warm seasons the soundings' N_m is near zero and 35 to 45% of Hammer Odde's wet hours are coded 80 to 89 (showers or intermittent rain; the automated code does not tell them apart): convective rain is not what a linear stable theory describes (R1321: "no convection or stagnation"). Dropping the orographic term from the share of hours that are showers would remove up to 40% of the warm seasons' term; the code's meaning is ambiguous, so it is not done.
  4. **The background double count.** P_BG is DMI's coastal-cell mean, which already contains the island's own coastal orographic rain; the theory's P_inf is the rain without terrain. With the delays as they are the coast still comes out at 707 against 634 and the land mean at 743 against 672 (about 11%). Setting P_BG to the value at which the model's coast matches DMI's would close both and leave the contrast; that is calibrating an input to the check, so it is left to the operator.
  5. **The target is soft.** DMI's cells are interpolated from at most 17 gauges (R1328), uncorrected for catch; the 10 km "+67" is a handful of stations (the 17 gauges' gradient, 106 mm per 100 m, is larger than the cells', which smooth it). Bergeron's Pluvius in Norway (R1323, second hand) found hills of 50 m with twice the neighbours' rain. A fit to all of that would not be a single number.
  6. The generated islands are less orographic than the real one (193 against 273 before, 75 against 120 after in the 75 m bands): the relief generator's plateau and valley widths, not tuned here.
  7. The cold-season fall time is H_w over a snow speed of 1 m/s for the whole share above the freezing level; aggregates, graupel and the melting layer are one speed here, and the rain's 2.5 m/s is the paper's, not measured for light rain.

## Napkin notes

- The rain kernel's `lag` overflows for sigma tau above 255 (a delay over about 1260 s on the 13.6 m/s wind) unless x is held; it is held at 254. A test that changes an input of a kernel must pass that input to the CPU twin too (`rainGpu(g, z, wetRose, delays)`), or the comparison tests the input.
- Validating the rain needs no new data pull: R1326 (`copernicus-dem-90m.s3.amazonaws.com`, N55 E014 and E015, a COG with one deflate tile and a floating point predictor) and the DMI stationValue API need no key; `python3` on this Mac has no numpy, so use Bun scripts; `curl -L ... -w` failed to connect here, plain `curl -s -o file` worked.
