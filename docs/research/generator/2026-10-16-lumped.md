Cover by seed 1's shares (terrain-constants sec. 12f): trees 51.2% (128 km2), shrubs 6.1% (15.2 km2), open sward 0.8% (2.0 km2), field layer in shade at 10% light 1.7% (4.2 km2), marsh (reed on fen peat) 29.8% (74.5 km2), bare 12.1% (30.2 km2), sand 0% (the generator has no beaches, terrain-constants sec. 12g gap 4).
- Trees by species: the generator's split by area is not recorded; seed 1's stem counts (plant-constants sec. 11d: pine 389,334, oak 500,812, ash 209,628, aspen 539,094) are used as the area split, pine 30.4, oak 39.1, ash 16.4, aspen 42.1 km2 [derived, crude: aspen stems are small].
- Soils (terrain-constants sec. 12a, 12b): meltwater sand, sandy till, clay till, alluvium, fen peat on 26% of land; pH 5.3 base-poor (toward 4 under pine and heather), 5.6 base-rich, 8.3 carbonate (rare).
- Grid: 256 x 256 cells of 75 m (5,625 m2), 44,444 land cells at 250 km2; texels of 18.75 m, 16 a cell. tiers/lumped.ts keys a bin as cell << 16 | state, so a kind has 16 bits of state.
- Time: an island year is 40 game days and biology runs 365.25 / 40 = 9.13 real days a game day (plants.ts YEARS_PACE). A per-real-day hazard r is a game-day probability 1 - exp(-9.13 r) when r is steady over the day.
- A bin costs 16 bytes a table, 32 bytes with the table the step writes (tiers/lumped.ts), for any number of members up to 2^32.

## 1. Grass and herb tillers, and reed shoots

### 1.1 The law (per tiller, per real day)

The only fully specified, field-calibrated per-tiller birth and death law found for a NW European sward is van Loo and Lantinga's (R950, ch. 7, eqns 2a to 2g) [read]. It is a per-parent birth hazard and a per-tiller death hazard driven by temperature, the sward's leaf area index (LAI), time since defoliation and season, and it settles by itself at a constant LAI, which is the self-thinning line:

- Daughters per vegetative parent a day: b = kN Fs LA.
  - Leaf appearance LA = 0.14 clamp((T - c) / (d - c), 0, 1) leaves a tiller a day; c, d = 0, 16 C from 1 March to 10 June and 10, 18 C from 10 June to 1 March, T the day's mean (the fit used soil at 10 cm) (R950 eqn 2d) [read].
  - Site filling Fs = clamp(0.867 - 0.183 LAI, 0, 0.693), and clamp(0.335 - 0.067 LAI, 0, 0.693) in the week after a defoliation; 0.693 = ln 2 is the theoretical most (R950 eqn 2c) [read; R950's Fig. 7.5 caption prints 0.076, the appendix 0.067: 0.067 used].
  - kN: R950's swards had 380 to 500 kg N/ha a year (kN 1). For the never-fertilised island kN = 0.25 (0.15 to 0.35), chosen so summer births match the unfertilised, cattle-grazed Baltic Festuca rubra meadow at Tullgarn (R955 Table 3: births 0.48 %/d in May to July, 0.74 in July to September, 0.21 overwinter; deaths 0.28, 0.39, 0.13) [INFERENCE on R955 read]. R950 states site filling fell with less nitrogen [read].
- Deaths a vegetative tiller a day: m = mu_m + max(0, 0.09 (LAI - 6) / 4), shading deaths from LAI 6 to 0.1 a day at LAI 10 (R950 eqns 2e, 2f) [read]. R950's mu_m 0.01 is a fertilised ryegrass's; the island's mu_m = 0.003 a day in May to October and 0.0015 in November to April [INFERENCE on R955's deaths and R954's winter survival].
- Reproductive tillers: all vegetative tillers alive on 1 March are vernalised; elongation starts between 8 May and 10 June; an elongating tiller has no daughters and dies if its apex is bitten; a flowering tiller dies after seeding (R950 eqns 2b, 2g, 4c) [read].
- Equilibrium: b = m where kN Fs LA = mu, LAI* = (0.867 - mu / (kN LA)) / 0.183. R950's values give 4.3 [read, R950 p. 129]; the island's (kN 0.25, LA 0.0875 at 15 C after 10 June, mu 0.003) give 3.99 [derived], where a sward intercepts 95% of light (LAI 3 to 4, R954 citing Simon and Lemaire) [read].
- The seasonal pattern this gives (R950, R955, R998): births low November to March, rising from March, peaking late summer and autumn in grazed semi-natural swards; flowering deaths June to July; density lowest in spring, highest late summer. Harder summer grazing raises birth and death together without changing density (R998) [abstract].

### 1.2 What the law needs that the sim must supply: lamina per tiller

Births and deaths read LAI, and LAI is lamina mass a m2 times specific leaf area (the sward's 26.39 m2/kg, plant-constants sec. 4b). Tillers therefore carry a lamina class, and the carbon a cell's sward makes is shared among them:

- Lamina classes k = 0 to 7, w_k = 0.25 mg x 2^k (0.25 to 32 mg) [design]. The top class is set from R951's lax sward on the -3/2 line: 5,000 tillers/m2 at 100 mg whole tiller, which at LAI* 4 and SLA 26.39 m2/kg is 4 / (0.02639 x 5,000) = 30 mg of lamina a tiller [derived].
- Growth: the cell's sward makes G g/m2 a day by the forage rule now in use (1.4 g a MJ of the season's sun, intercepted, warm and watered, times fertility; animal-constants sec. 3). G is shared as each tiller's lamina is of the sward's (light is caught by lamina): a tiller in class k moves to k + 1 with hazard g = G / M (M the sward's lamina a m2), which adds (w_{k+1} - w_k) = w_k at that hazard, so the expected gain is exactly G [derived]. The top class does not grow: carbon past the largest tillers is lost.
- Senescence: a green leaf lives 460 degree-days over 4 C (R392, the forage rule's). Lamina is lost at s = (degree-days a day) / 460 of its mass a day; a tiller moves down a class with hazard 2s (a class down loses half), so the expected loss is exactly s M [derived]. A tiller in class 0 that moves down dies: the smallest tillers die first under light stress (Ong 1978, further source) [abstract].
- Defoliation: a tiller is bitten with chance q = I / (s_b B) a day, I the grazers' intake from the layer (g/m2 a day), B its green lamina (g/m2), s_b = 0.5 the share of lamina a bite takes (35% of extended tiller length for dairy cows, 40 to 60% of leaf length, "relatively constant and averages 50%"; continuous grazing defoliates a tiller every 7 to 15 days, R953) [read]. A bite moves a tiller one class down (half its lamina), so the expected lamina removed is exactly I [derived]; a bitten elongating tiller dies (R950 eqn 4c). Check: B 60 g/m2 and I 2 g/m2 a day give q 0.067, a 15-day interval, R953's range [derived].
- Shade: in the field layer the canopy above counts as leaf area in Fs and in the shading deaths, LAI_eff = LAI_sward + L_c, L_c = -ln(f) / 0.8 with f the layer's light (ground.ts fieldLight, 0.10 in gaps) and 0.8 the extinction growth.ts uses for crowns (plant-constants sec. 4: exp(-0.8 x 2.6 x cover)) [INFERENCE: one law for sun and shade]. At f = 0.10, L_c = 2.88 and births stop at a sward LAI of 1.1, 42 g/m2 of lamina [derived]. Kays and Harper found the -3/2 line in full and 70% light and a slope near -1 at 30% (R956) [search excerpt].
- Dead lamina (from senescence, bites past what is eaten, and dead tillers) leaves the tiller kind as standing dead blades of the litter kind (sec. 2), which is the forage rule's dead pool: one mass balance [design].

### 1.3 Self-thinning: the cited line the law must settle on

- Undefoliated or lax swards at their most LAI follow log10 w = log10 K - 1.5 log10 N (w the mean tiller mass): field anchors 20,000 tillers/m2 at 13 mg (close-grazed sheep pasture) and 5,000 at up to 100 mg (lax dairy pasture), slope -1.47 between them (R951) [read; slope derived]. The -3/2 line is a line of constant LAI (R951, R953, after Matthew, Lemaire, Sackville Hamilton and Hernandez-Garay 1995) [read].
- Defoliated swards held below their most LAI are steeper: -2.31 +/- 0.02 in ryegrass mini-swards cut to 40, 80 and 120 mm (R951) [read], close to -5/2 (R952) [abstract]; flatter than -1 from 120 to 160 mm (R952) [abstract].
- The line moved about 0.17 log10 units on the mass axis between seasons (R951) [read].
- Grazing height: mini-swards cut twice weekly at 20, 40, 80, 120, 160 mm held 6,120, 9,450, 7,730, 6,920, 4,710 tillers/m2 of 16.7, 25.4, 40.2, 52.2, 73.6 mg (R951 Table 1) [read]. Continuously stocked swards at 1.1 to 6.4 cm held the most tillers at 2 to 3 cm (R957) [abstract].
- With the law of 1.1 and 1.2, a sward under steady drivers settles at LAI* (births equal deaths) and its tillers at N* = LAI* / (SLA w_mean), on the constant-LAI line; how far along the line is set by tiller size, which bites and the carbon set. The line is not imposed; it is the test (sec. 7.7).

### 1.5 Bin key, state, totals, memory

- State (11 bits): lamina class (3), stage (3: grass juvenile, vegetative, vernalised, elongating, flowering; reed bud, emerging, growing, mature, dead 0, dead 1, dead 2; forb rosette, flowering), species (4: grass, clover, yarrow, sorrel, mint, buttercup, daisy, harebell, poppy, reed, bracken, lady fern), layer (1: open sward or field layer in shade). R950's "bitten within a week" state is shorter than a game day (9.13 real days), so its effect is folded into the day's chances: a tiller bitten at a uniform moment of the day has daughters at the first-week Fs for the rest of the day, up to 7 of its 9.13 days [derived].
- Bin key: the 75 m cell. The law reads per-layer LAI, intake, light and fertility; the sim holds the field layer's share and light per cell (ground.ts field, fieldLight) and the grazers' asks per tile, so every tiller of a layer in a cell shares every input, and the cell is the finest scale the inputs are defined at [derived]. If Unit 12 resolves the field layer's light by texel, the key moves to the texel (sec. 7.2).
- Island totals (members): open sward 3.2 x 10^10, field layer 1.3 x 10^10, live reed shoots 7.5 x 10^9 (summer), standing dead culms 1.9 x 10^10, forbs 1.3 x 10^9: 7.2 x 10^10 [derived]. The plan's table says about 10^11.
- Bins: about 26,700 cells with a field layer or sward (60% of land cells [INFERENCE]) x 30 occupied keys, and 15,600 reed cells x 4: 0.86 M bins, 28 MB at 32 bytes [derived].
- Promotion: a person pulls a tuft, picks a flower (poppy, daisy, harebell, buttercup) or herb (sorrel, mint, yarrow), or cuts reed: the next ranks of the bin become Things carried (an active kind with species, stage and lamina class), with their identity. Grazers never single a tiller out: a bite is a binomial over the bin. A grazer seen eating within people's sight is drawn eating; nothing is promoted.

### 2.2 Decay: Olson's law as a chain of mass classes

- Olson: X / X0 = exp(-k t), k fitted by regressing ln mass remaining on time (R961) [read]. Decay is not one exponential throughout: Scots pine needles lose 30.16% a year while 0 to 20% is gone and 15.76% once over 60% is gone, 26.23% on average over 15 sites from boreal to temperate (R963) [read], so k_A = 0.359 and k_D = 0.171 a year [derived].
- Berg's limit value: L_t = m (1 - exp(-k_A t / m)); Scots pine limit values 71.4 to 90.6%, mean 80.8% (R963) [read]; 78% for pine, spruce and birch, 64% for other deciduous litter (R964) [read].
- First-year k a year, western Polish oak-hornbeam forest, 1 mm mesh bags of 8 g set out 27 October (R961 Table 7, by -ln(1 - loss)) [read; derived]: oak 0.226, hazel 0.360, sycamore 0.483, hornbeam 0.567, ash 1.98. Forest herbs' six-month k: autumn-senescing Urtica 1.1, Stachys 0.9, Mercurialis 2.1, Aegopodium leaves 3.2 (R961) [read].
- Macrofauna: ash leaves lost 88.5% in a year in ash-dominated plots and 66.5% in mixed plots with large invertebrates let in; macrofauna did 96% and 68% of the decomposition there (R962) [read]. Earthworms, millipedes and woodlice are the decomposers (R962) [read].
- Reed: leaf blades on a periodically flooded marsh lost half their ash-free mass in 7 months, k = 0.0035 a day (1.28 a year), 21% left after 11 months (R965) [abstract]; sheaths k = 0.0039 a day (1.42 a year) and stems 0.0026 (0.95) after a 6-month lag with no loss (R966) [abstract].
- Grass blades, heather, aspen, bracken, lady fern: [gap]; grass blades take a slow herb's 0.9 a year [INFERENCE on R961], forbs 1.1 [INFERENCE on R961].
- Temperature: no cited Q10 [gap]. AET explains 54.9% of the variance of early pine needle loss and none of the late (R963) [read]. Half-year seasonal multipliers from R961 (each pair averaging 1): ash 0.57 November to April and 1.43 May to October; oak 1.17 and 0.83; hazel 1.34 and 0.66 [derived; oak's and hazel's is winter leaching].

The chain [derived; the litter pass proposed it, checked here]: a piece of class c in mass class j (0 to 7) weighs m_c r^j, r = 0.8. It moves to j + 1 with hazard h_j = k_j / (1 - r) = 5 k_j, losing m_j (1 - r), so its expected mass loss rate is k_j m_j; in the last class it is removed with hazard k_j, losing m_j, again k_j m_j. So the expected mass of a pool obeys dM/dt = -k M exactly at constant k (Olson), with stage rates k_j (pine 0.359, 0.30, 0.24, 0.171 [INFERENCE between R963's A and D]) giving Berg's curve, and with a limit value the piece leaves to a stable humus pool after J classes (pine J = 7: 1 - 0.8^7 = 79.0% against R963's 80.8% and R964's 78%; deciduous J = 5: 67.2% against R964's 64%). Model check by the litter pass: pine needles keep 72.1% after a year (R963 class A 69.8%) and 19.5% after 5.5 years [read output of their run, not rerun here]. Fauna removal of whole pieces, h_f = 0.87 a year for oak on base-rich soil, gives R962's 66.5% first-year loss [INFERENCE: tuned]; 0 under pine, heather and base-poor soils (pH 4 to 5.3) [INFERENCE].

The step is exact for any step length: within a step the hazards are constant, so the chance of moving from j to j + i is the pure-birth chain's transient probability (a hypoexponential sum over the classes passed), computed by the TypeScript twin in float64 for each litter class, season multiplier and soil class and sent as dyadic outcome intervals; a bin's split over them is then the continuous law's, to the 2^-32 rounding of the intervals [derived]. The litter pass's one-class-a-step rule is exact only as the step shrinks.

### 2.4 Bin key, state, totals, memory

- State (8 bits): litter class (4: pine needle, oak, ash, aspen, hazel, forb, grass blade standing, grass blade lying, reed leaf, reed sheath, reed stem standing, reed stem fallen, fine twig and bud scale, plus attached-to-crown for the leaves of 2.1), mass class (3), reed lag (1, or a class with exit hazard 2 a year [INFERENCE: a mean lag of half a year]).

- Bins: 39,100 cells with litter x 32 occupied keys = 1.25 M bins, 40 MB [derived].
- Promotion: a person gathering leaves, needles or reed litter for bedding or tinder takes counts; a single leaf picked up and looked at is promoted (an active piece with class and mass class). Fire takes counts of what it burns. The moisture law (wgsl/moisture.ts) already holds one water a texel for each class, exactly each member's.

### 3.2 Laws for the lumped kind

Each taxon is a stage chain. A member moves to the next stage with the stage's hazard (degree-days or the calendar triggers above), dies with the stage's survival (where cited: winter moth larvae by mismatch, meadow brown larvae 0.686 over 309 days, so 0.9889 a game day watered and 0.9788 under drought [derived]), and adults bear eggs into the egg stage of the same cell (sec. 7.4: births on the GPU). ## 6. Coupling with the animals and people

- Grazing and predation on lumped prey run on two clocks: the animals step hourly (animal-constants sec. 12), the lumped kinds daily. Each day's census (sec. 7.5) gives each cell's green lamina by layer, dead blades, and each predator class's available prey; each hour the animals ask against what is left of it (as the share pass does now, so order-free), and the day's eaten amount is debited per cell. At the daily step each bin's split carries the day's grazing and predation as outcomes: a tiller bitten with q (sec. 1.2), a prey member eaten with the chance (eaten in its cell and class) over (available there). The expected removal then equals what was eaten; the difference (a binomial's noise) is carried as the cell's credit into the next day's available amount, so over days intake equals what the bins lost [design]. The fate of an eaten member is EATEN by its predator kind (sec. 7.4), and which predator ate it is the split of the cell's eaten members over the predators' asks in slot order.
- The forage rule now in the animals' grow pass (wgsl/animals.ts: fg and fd per tile, 1.4 g a MJ, 300 g/m2 most, 460 degree-day leaves, dead rotting 0.4% a day) becomes the tiller kind's growth G and senescence, and its dead pool the litter kind's standing blades. In expectation the lamina the tillers hold follows the forage rule's green except for three terms the forage rule lacks: daughters' lamina, lamina of tillers dying (van Loo's deaths, and starvation from class 0), and carbon lost above the largest class. These are the model change the plan's integration test records (sec. 7.7).
- People: gather reed (thatch, fuel), herbs and flowers, leaves for bedding, ant pupae (R976), worms for bait, insects; each a take of counts, or a promotion where the person holds the thing.
- Butterflies (src/sim/pools.ts, each tile's count until now) become the meadow brown members of the fauna kind; their adults in a cell within 30 m of someone are drawn from the bins by identity and place, which needs no promotion.

### 7.3 One bin per occupied key (a decision for Main)

tiers/lumped.ts keeps a new bin for each step's entrants, so a long-lived state with daily entrants holds a bin for every step of its members' stay: about ln(n) / p steps, n the entrants and p the chance a step of leaving. With lamina classes moving about 0.4 of tillers a game day that is about 30 bins a key; for a state left at 0.03 a step (a vegetative tiller's deaths without class moves) about 380. That is 10^8 bins, 3 GB, past the budget [derived]. Entry steps are information: knowing which of a key's members entered at which step takes a record per step still holding members, whatever the encoding.

So Unit 12b merges a key's bins after each step (a `merge` option of the tier, off for the seedlings and the tier's tests): the new bin is the key's stayers first, in their rank order, then entrants in key and bin order, then births (LUMP_NEW's order). A stayer's new rank is its old rank less the members that left its bin, from its own bin alone; its identity (key, e, sigma) is renewed each step with that link. What changes against the plan: identity is answered for the present and linked step to step; a fate needs the merged bin's members each step (entrants come from the cell's other states), so the CPU keeps the bins of watched cells (those within people's sight, where anything can be singled out, and any cell asked of), read back with the landing, and replays fates there; birth is not recoverable unless a kind carries it in its state, which none of these laws needs. The alternative, keeping entry steps, costs 1 to 3 GB for these kinds.

### 7.4 Extensions of the tier (Unit 12b's share of the plan's open items)

- GPU inputs with history: the law's inp is written on the GPU each step by the inputs kernel (per land cell: tillers 4 words: layer LAI, intake, relative growth, light; litter 2: season multiplier class, soil class; fauna 6: soil temperature, soil water, organic matter, eaten by predator class; sand 4: u*, wetness, feet volume, swash depth), and a ring of the last 40 steps (an island year) of those words per land cell keeps fates replayable: 44,444 cells x 16 words x 40 x 4 bytes = 114 MB [derived], or 46 MB with a 16-step ring.
- Births on the GPU: a split's outcome may bear: each member taking outcome j bears a number of members into state bs[j] drawn as a further multinomial over 0, 1, 2 ... (daughter tillers Poisson(b dt), cocoons, egg batches), conditioned on the member's own outcome (survivors and those dying in the step have the continuous law's different counts), written as contributions after the departures; and leaves born at leaf-out and grains moved by an event come the same way. The CPU's add() stays for the generator's start.
- Outcomes: a tiller's step has at most 3 class moves x 2 (live, die) x 4 daughter counts, so OUTCOMES rises from 16 to 32 for the tiller kind, or births are drawn in their own multinomial as above (preferred: 16 suffices).
- Takes with a fate: promote() generalised to take(key, e, count, code) with codes PROMOTED, EATEN by predator kind (0xfffffff0 + kind), TAKEN by people, recorded in the log for fates; GPU-requested takes come from the animals' kernels through an appended request buffer read back at the landing into the log.
- 64-bit counts for the sand kind (sec. 5).
- Scratch shared across kinds: today each Lumped names its scratch by kind (store.scratch(`${name}:...`)); the kinds step one after another, so one set sized for the largest kind serves all.
