# flora.ts and land.ts: what each constant is now, and the cited replacement

All sources below were read in this session (`[abs]` means I read the abstract or a search excerpt only). Changing the reference climate to Bornholm/Gotland was applied: where a figure depends on site, the source is Danish or Swedish. Scratch notes and downloaded sources are in /tmp/nm/gen/ (flora_notes.md).

## Five changes that alter the generator's structure
1. **Tree count would rise about 10×.** At full cover the code now lays about 102 stems/ha (3.6 per 351.6 m² quad). The measured carrying capacity at the code's own heights is 1,160 to 1,410 stems/ha. Unthinned stands in Denmark and Sweden hold about 800/ha. Island-wide that is millions of tree objects, so Main has a performance or representation decision to make.
2. **light.ts crown radii are about 2× too wide.** `crownRadius` (oak 0.35 H gives 5.25 m) disagrees with measured crown width: oak at 15 m has a 2.27 m radius, ash 3.17 m against 4.64 m, aspen 1.49 m against 2.86 m. Changing it also moves rain.ts, recruits.ts and the sprites.
3. **TALL duplicates `NICHE.tall` and should be removed.** Height should be computed per site.
4. **Fallen wood is 1 to 3 orders of magnitude too sparse,** and which reference to use is a decision. A never-logged island matches unmanaged Danish reserves (48 to 154 m³/ha of logs). The code holds about 0.6 m³/ha of logs and about 20 sticks/ha, while measured fine debris is 12.2 m³/ha (about 17,000 one-metre sticks/ha). One option is to treat a stick object as a bundle; that is a decision for Main.
5. **Gorse (*Ulex europaeus*) is an oceanic, frost-tender shrub, not native to the Baltic** [UNVERIFIED for Bornholm]. Its place in SHRUBS should be reconsidered.

## Laws proposed
- **Stems from cover:** N = C · SDI_max · (25/D)^−b (Reineke). D comes from H through fire-constants sec. 19: pine D = 1.721 H^0.842, oak 0.489 H^1.334, aspen 0.343 H^1.371, ash (100H/290)^1.938 [cited R97/R98].
  - Values: pine SDI_max 890 (range 395 to 1354), b = 1.593; oak 733 (393 to 1113), b = 1.424 [DelRio20, PB05].
  - Ash and aspen: SDI_max is a [gap]. Use crown packing instead: N = C·10⁴/(πK²/4), with K from [Hem05].
  - Check: pine at the measured mean height of 22.7 m gives D 23.9 cm and N 959, against the measured dq 28.4 cm and N 802. Oak at 21.7 m gives D 29.7 cm and N 575, against 26.4 cm and N 823. Both are within about 30%.
- **Crown width:** K (m) = a + b·dbh(m) [Hem05 Table 1].
  - Oak 1.717 + 15.616 d; ash 0.758 + 20.357 d; poplar (stands in for aspen) 0.801 + 18.950 d.
  - Pine: no source found [gap]. At canopy closure, r = √(10⁴/πN_max) gives 1.5 m at 17 m height [derived].
- **Heights:** a stand's top height is its site index, H100. Within the stand, height spread follows a two-parameter Weibull diameter distribution passed through H(D).
  - Shape c ≈ 2 to 3.6 for normal to dense stands, rising to about 24 in peaked ones [SM13; the c values are [abs] only].
  - Pine H100 in southern Sweden: 24 to 27 m [abs, Heureka studies via search, UNVERIFIED].
  - Measured mean heights at about 72 years: pine 22.7 m (10.8 to 31.2), oak 21.7 m (9.1 to 32.3) [DelRio20].
  - Species maximum heights: pine 42, oak 38, ash 42, aspen 30, hazel 10 m [R97, read from the sqlite].
  - The generator has no stand age. That is a [design] input: an old-growth asymptote, or age as a field.
- **Patch objects** (bush, fern, herb, flower, reed and tussock patches): count = −ln(1−C)·A/a. The covered share of random discs is 1 − e^(−Na/A), so the cover share C fixes the count once the footprint a = π(size/2)² is known [derived, standard Boolean model]. This removes every `cover × magic` multiplier.
- **Deadwood pool:** V = I/k, where I is mortality input and k the decay rate.
  - k: aspen 0.066/yr and boreal pine 0.014/yr [SK14 abs]; pine 0.032, beech 0.054 [HKB15 abs]; large beech logs gone in 40 to 50 years [Chr05]. Oak and ash are ring-porous and decay slower [abs Kahl17]. Their k is a [gap].
  - I, the mortality input, can be derived from the self-thinning line [derived; not computed].
- **Clast sizes:** a power law N(>d) ∝ d^−2.9 for till [HI95 abs]. Using it for boulders is an extrapolation [UNVERIFIED].
  - Anchor on Swedish surface-boulder counts: boulders ≥ 2 dm counted in a 314 m² plot fall in classes 0, 1 to 20, and over 20, which is 0, 32 to 640, and over 640 per ha [SLU].
  - "Large-boulder" ground: more than 5 boulders over 1 m per 100 m², or under SGU's newer definition at least ⅔ of the surface covered by boulders over 1 m³ [SGU].
  - Then stones ≥ 0.1 m = 7.46 × the boulders ≥ 0.2 m, and 7% of boulders ≥ 0.2 m exceed 0.5 m [derived].
