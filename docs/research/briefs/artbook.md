# Nomads art direction: the isometric island

## 1. Brief (from the operator)
- Feeling: a world full of possibilities and adventure.
- Era: open; art director picks among SimCity 2000, SimCity 3000, Transport Tycoon and RollerCoaster Tycoon.
- People: small, like SimCity; the land is the star.
- Screen: just the world, no interface.

## 2. Reference board (what to take from each)
- SimCity 2000 (Maxis, 1993): land sculpted from slope tiles on whole-number heights, earth showing on the map's cut edge, water and traffic animated by color cycling. Take: slope tiles, the cut edge, color-cycled life. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SimCity_2000
- Transport Tycoon Deluxe (art by Simon Foster, 1995): "the perfect compromise between clarity and detail"; nothing is static. Take: a crisp, clean tile set and a world that moves. https://www.filfre.net/2020/10/transport-tycoon/
- RollerCoaster Tycoon (Simon Foster, 1999): tiny people who read by color and what they are doing; scenery dressed in dense clusters. Take: small, busy people. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RollerCoaster_Tycoon_(video_game)
- SimCity 3000 (1999): lusher trees and softer shading than 2000. Take: richer forest masses.
- Populous (Bullfrog, 1989): the land as a god's sculpted landmass. Take: the island as a jewel on the sea.
- The Settlers II (1996): small workers on paths between home and resources. Take: paths that tell you where life goes.
- Age of Empires II (1999): forest clumps with strong silhouettes, banded shallows, fish. Take: forest massing and readable shorelines.
- eBoy "Pixorama" posters: dense pixel worlds full of tiny stories. Take: reward for looking closer.
- Adventure and landmarks: Disney's "weenie", a landmark that pulls you toward it (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/a-taxonomy-of-weenies-the-landmarks-that-define-i-ghost-of-tsushima-i-). Take: every view shows somewhere you want to go.
- Craft rules: 2:1 lines, vertical heights, one light from the upper left, three tones per face (https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2022/11/28/pixelblog-41-isometric-pixel-art, https://pixelparmesan.com/blog/fundamentals-of-isometric-pixel-art).

## 3. Visual pillars (every decision is judged against these)
1. A horizon of promise. Every view shows somewhere you have not been yet: a peak, a waterfall, smoke from a distant fire, birds circling a cliff, an islet offshore.
2. The clarity of a toy. Each tile reads at a glance: one light, three tones a face, distinct silhouettes. It must survive the squint test.
3. A living island. Nothing is still: smoke, surf, fish, deer, birds, people at work.
4. Rich in clusters. Detail gathers into little stories (a fallen tree with mushrooms, a reed bed with a heron) with quiet ground between.

Avoid: even noise, cliffs everywhere, interface, uniform scatter, garish saturation.
