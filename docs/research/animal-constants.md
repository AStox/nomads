# Animal constants

Substrate 5 of `rule-audit.md`: deer, rabbits, wolves and red foxes, and (sec. 11) the fish, herons, eagles, gulls, crows and butterflies. Each species is a population over the island's tiles (src/sim/herds.ts), how many there are on a tile and the fat each carries, run on energy: what the ground grows for them to eat, what they burn, what they bear and what eats them. Individual animals stand on the ground only within sight of people, and are the tile's own: one killed, trapped or eaten is one fewer on its tile. Every number below is cited, [derived], [design], [gap] or [UNVERIFIED]. References continue from `time-constants.md` (R361 on).

## 3. Forage

Two pools a tile, each split into what is green and what has died and still stands, in g per m2 of its own share of the tile, from the generator's cover there:

- The ground layer: the open grass and the field layer in shade, under the canopy and in its gaps, as much of each tile as the generator's field layer covers (ground.ts `field`, terrain-constants sec. 12c). Both deer and rabbits eat it.
- Browse: the woody shoots within a deer's reach, a share of the shrubs' cover and a smaller of the trees' [design: 0.3, 0.1]. Only deer eat it.

Green grows by the plant rule (terrain/growth.ts): 1.4 g per MJ of the season's sun there (climate.ts seasons, MJ/m2 a day), the share a sward intercepts [UNVERIFIED 0.8], warm and watered enough as the seasons there are, scaled by the ground's fertility (isle.fertility), at the year's pace (plants.ts YEARS_PACE), slowing as green and dead together near what a sward stands at. The field layer grows in the light the generator gives it (ground.ts `fieldLight`: 10% of the open sky in the canopy's gaps, as at Suserup, and 2% under a crown) [derived].

- A deer is a ruminant: its rumen holds a day's fibre (NDF) of 1.2% of its weight (Mertens, R399). Green is 40% NDF (grazed grass 30 to 40%, R400), dead 70% (mature grass over 65%, straw to 80%, R401) [derived], the bare broadleaves' twigs 61.9% (R702). It takes the green first by its response to it, then twigs and then the dead, each by its response, in what fibre the rumen has room for. A rabbit passes fibre through fast and eats to its own most [derived].
- Snow: a season's snowpack (climate.ts, mm of water) stands as deep as Sturm et al.'s maritime snow is dense on the season's middle day of the year, (0.5979 - 0.2578)(1 - e^(-0.0010 h - 0.0038 doy)) + 0.2578 g/cm3 (R409); the crowns hold up to 60% of it off the ground under a forest (R412), as the tile's trees cover it [derived]. - Rabbits graze from their warrens, heaviest within 50 m (R405), most of it within 10 m of a refuge (R406), so a tile's rabbits reach only the share of its ground a warren can be dug in: soil over both the bedrock and the water table deep enough for the tunnels, 0.3 to 0.75 m down in heavy soils (R407), nearly all the chambers within 0.55 m (R408), from none at 0.3 m to all of it at 0.55 [derived]. They crop that share first; the deer take what is left of the ground layer, and the browse. Rabbits move only toward ground they can dig.
- Where the grazers want more of a pool's green or dead than they can reach, each gets its share of what there is; a deer's asks split between the pools as they stand.

## 4. Energy

Each step of dt island days, at the year's pace (a herd's day is YEARS_PACE days of eating and burning, as a plant's day is of growing):

- A grazer eats what brings its reserve to full and pays its day, within its rumen or its most, as sec. 3 has it, out of what the tile stands.
- Its reserve gains what it ate at what each gram was worth and loses its field metabolic rate.
- Below nothing, as many die as the shortfall is in full reserves [design], and the rest stand at nothing.
- Wolves' and foxes' reserves gain what they take from their kills (sec. 2), a wolf's deer less what the ravens take (sec. 6).

## 6. Wolves killing deer and rabbits

Kills per wolf a day = 0.0336 X / (0.46 + X) moose-equivalents, X the moose-equivalent density of prey in a wolf's reach (km2), Messier's functional response for wolves on moose (R370) [read: abstract], taken by biomass: a moose 400 kg [UNVERIFIED], so X = the deer's and rabbits' kg a km2 there / 400, and the kills come out in kg [derived assumption: wolves' killing saturates on prey biomass, whatever the prey]. Each kind is killed as its share of that biomass [derived assumption: no preference]. Wolves eat lagomorphs as buffer prey when ungulates are short (R379, R380). A pack kills no more than brings its reserves to full and pays its day [derived: kills are what wolves eat, as a grazer eats], so wolves fed on rabbits leave deer be. Ravens take their share of each deer before the pack has eaten it: a pair loses 37% of a moose, a pack of six 17% (R398), a pack of four 27% between them [derived], so a pack kills that much more; a rabbit is eaten whole at once. A wolf hunts over its pack's territory: a square of land as big as Bialowieza's mean 201 km2 (R402), 95 tiles across, its prey's density taken over the land in it [derived]. Hunting over the 7 x 7 tiles round it (about 1 km), packs set down where the deer were few starved before they found any, at a tile a step. Every tile's wolves hunt at once: each takes its kills as a share of each kind in its territory, and each tile's deer and rabbits lose the sum of the shares of the wolves whose territories take them in, which kills exactly what the wolves ate unless they would take more than are there [derived]. Wolves within sight of people kill as individuals instead, and their tile's predation then waits on them.

As individuals (sec. 12) a wolf searching kills at the response's searching rate, 0.0336 / 0.46 = 0.073 moose-equivalents a wolf a day for each moose-equivalent a km2 [derived]: Messier's curve is kills over whole winters, the days a pack spends at a kill and full after it among them, and here that time is the pack's own, in its larder, which it eats down before it hunts again (R709).

### Foxes killing rabbits

A fox kills rabbits by Pech et al.'s measured response at Yathong, as Pech and Hood fitted it: at most 100.01 kg of rabbit a quarter of a year, on a Type III curve with its inflection at 1.32 rabbits a ha (R383), N the rabbits a ha over its home range, a rural fox's 200 to 600 ha (R403), taken as 4 km2, 13 tiles across [derived]. It kills no more than brings its reserve to full and pays its day, and makes up what the rabbits leave it short from the carrion on its range (sec. 11), by Mulder and Hendriks' predators' half-saturation (R374): ungulate remains were 44.9% of what a boreal forest's foxes ate in winter (R448). Its share of the rabbits round it is added to the wolves', and every tile's foxes hunt at once as the wolves do. Within sight of people foxes are individuals: they keep 40 m from people and 80 m from wolves, and a hungry one runs down the nearest rabbit within 100 m and eats it [design].

## 7. Moving

Each step a share of a tile's animals moves to whichever neighbouring tile would feed them faster (deer look further, along eight bearings at doubling distances out to a step's walk, 3.2 km a day, the least of the 3.2 to 9.7 km a red deer walks, R411 [derived]): grazers to where their response to the green and the dead there, at what each is worth, gives the most energy (which falls as those already there graze it down), wolves to more prey by its biomass, foxes to more rabbits; the share set by how far they range [design]: deer half a day (hinds range 2 to 4 km2, R371), wolves and foxes all of it, rabbits a fiftieth (home ranges of 0.3 to 0.7 ha, R372, a tile being 2.25 ha).
