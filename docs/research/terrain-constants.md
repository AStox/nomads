## 10. Gaps

1. **Relief is an input, not a result.** At steady state with n = 1 the highest summit stands (U / K) chi above the sea. With U and K at the middle of their cited ranges (U / K = 35 m) the peaks came out at 413 to 445 m, chi about 12.2 (12.7, 12.1, 11.8 on seeds 1 to 3) where the notes' hand estimate of 4.8 assumed a channel head 150 m below each divide; on a 75 m grid every cell is a channel and the cells near the divides add most of it. Applying the water notes' channel-head rule (channel where a S^2 is at least C, C = 25 to 200 m) raised them further (460 to 695 m on seed 1). Bornholm's relief is inherited, from the Late Cretaceous uplift along the Sorgenfrei-Tornquist zone (R529) with the ice and the till over it, not a steady state, so the steady state is made to reproduce it: U0 is inverted from Rytterknægten's 162 to 165 m with granite's K and the generator's chi held fixed, 0.95 m a Myr, inside Jansen et al.'s measured 0.8 to 7.7 (sec. 3). K, D and everything else stay as cited. The peaks then come out at 159 to 178 m, as the generator's chi varies by island.
2. **Rain.** With the relief Bornholm's the climate's rain on the land is 700 to 733 mm against Bornholm's 674 (the interior 695 to 721); at the old 445 m peaks it was 850 to 930 mm.
3. **The island is 250 km2, not 588.** The map is 256 cells of 75 m (19.2 km) a side; Bornholm would need about twice that across [design].
4. **The sediments lie flat at the till's 6 m.** Mudstone's K (2.2e-4) holds under a metre of relief at steady state, and the blocks' uplift is slower, so the south is a till plain a few metres above the sea; southern Bornholm's 20 to 100 m are inherited too.
5. **Lakes are three and a half to four times too many** (1.6 to 1.8% of the land against 0.45%; 48 to 58 on 250 km2 against 18 on 588), more than at the old relief (0.68 to 0.83%) as the joint valleys' depth (drawn up to 50 m) does not scale with the land's. Each long joint valley's sill makes a closed hollow of each half, most deeper than the 3 m water table; real joint valleys open to the lower ground at their ends. The valleys' spacing, length, depth draw and sill width are [design]. Streams are fewer too (0.45 to 0.48 km/km2 against 0.71): more of the runoff ends in the lakes.
6. **Bare rock is 5.5 to 6.8% of the land** under the knoll rule ([design]: the basement bare wherever it stands 5 m above the ground within 300 m); at the old relief it was a quarter. Bornholm's bare share was not found.
7. **Dolerite covers 1.6 to 1.9% of the land**, drawn one cell wide where the dykes are metres across; their length is a gap.
8. **Rain in the erosion is uniform**: the rain shadow (Bornholm's interior gets about 12% more) is not in the landform, as the climate comes after it.
9. Gaps carried in the tables: sandstone's, limestone's and dolerite's erodibility; limestone's and dolerite's texture and phosphorus; every rock's carbonate (`base`); pyrite's amounts; the till's flint share and its thickness over the sediments; the hummocks' wavelength; the ice's sliding speed for Herman et al.'s law. The infiltration share `soak` is no longer read (water.ts takes recharge from the aquifer itself, sec. 11) and can go from ROCKS.
10. **The deer fall far below the field's densities on the new ground.** Seed 1's new world holds 78 deer and 9 wolves on the whole tree (75 and 9 in the smoke run), where HEAD's held 1,346 deer and 15 wolves; on the old tree with only this terrain (HEAD plus 11b) it holds 7 deer and no wolves, and HEAD plus 11a only kept about 2,000 deer (11a's check), so the shortfall is the terrain's. In the herds test the deer stand at 0.03 to 0.08 a km2 on HEAD plus 11b and 0.19 on island 5 on the whole tree, against the field's 1.7 to 31.4, and the deer no longer climb in spring (1.0 m on HEAD plus 11b, the test wants 10). At the old relief (peaks 413 to 445 m) the deer were gone altogether, and giving the bare rock its soil back, the old transmissivity, the old nourishment or no heath on sour soil each left only 10 to 18. What changed is the ground's cover: open grass is 9% of the land (6% at the old relief) against HEAD's 16%, the land two thirds granite under till or none, and ground.ts's cover rules (old hand-set ramps, 11d's) give the rest to trees and heath. Not retuned here.

### 12c. Cover (P31)

The hand-written habitat key (pTree, pShrub, pMarsh, pGrass, moor, harsh and the fixed cascade) is gone. In each cell each form's best kind is the one whose compensation point over the year (growth.ts yearComp, at the cell's sun, warmth and soil water, its fit from niche.ts and its own p) is lowest. A stand fills in until the light at its floor, under its crowns and between them, falls to its own leaves' compensation point, Monsi and Saeki's optimum leaf area (R614): with crowns letting UNDER_CROWN through, (1 - c) + c UNDER_CROWN = comp, so it covers (1 - comp) / (1 - UNDER_CROWN) of the ground (growth.ts closes) [derived]. Under a crown, 2% of the open sky's light reaches the ground (growth.ts UNDER_CROWN): Emborg's relative light in the Danish near-natural beech and ash wood at Suserup stayed below 2% in every phase of its forest cycle but the gaps (R633), and temperate forests let through under 5% (R634); it was an eighth. Where trees keep themselves, 11.75% of their ground is gaps (the median of the gap fractions measured in primeval and old-growth beech forests: 16 and 14.6% in Slovakia, 13.6 and 8.2% in the Carpathians, 3.9 and 9.9% in Slovenia, the means of R637's two periods; R635, R636, R637) [derived]; Drössler's 0.387 to 0.529% of the forest opened a year over the some 35 years a fallen canopy tree takes to rot gives 14 to 19%. The gaps get 10% of the open sky's light, as Suserup's do (R633). In them the young trees close first, by the same rule in that light, then shrubs and reeds, then the field layer. On the open ground the shrubs and the reeds (the taller of their winners first) take the light, the sward what is left; beach sand grows nothing. Two passes settle the loop through organic matter and water [design]. There is no thermal treeline on the island: Paulsen and Körner's season of 94 days at 0.9 C or more averaging 6.4 C is met everywhere (R603). Fire, grazing and farming open no further ground in the generator [gap].

The field layer is its own share (`field`, of the cell's ground, and `fieldLight`, the light it gets): grass and herbs in shade, under the canopy and in its gaps. The grass, herb or flower (the sward, yarrow, sorrel, mint, buttercup, daisy, clover, harebell, poppy) that keeps itself on the least light closes over what the gaps' young trees, shrubs and reeds leave, and over the canopy's ground, by the same rule, its need taken as a share of the light there; its carbon joins the plants' input.
