## What was built

### Overlaps are ranked by the regional height (R1680, R1681)

`regional` (kernel and twin; `FLD.ZR`, `FLD.ZR2`, 22 fields now): after `elev`, each column's height is averaged over the same plate's cells in the 3 by 3 around it, 32 sweeps (even, so the result ends in `ZR2`, `regionalField`); `claim` bids with that height in place of the column's own. An elastic plate carries a narrow load over its flexural scale [cited R1411 sec. 3.16: elastic thickness 54 km for one basin; the tectonics note: flexure smooths loads over 50 to 100 km], so the buoyancy that decides who rides over is the regionally compensated one. The flexural parameter for Te = 54 km is alpha = (4 D / (rho_m g))^(1/4) = 106 km with D = E Te^3 / (12 (1 - nu^2)) [derived; E = 70 GPa and nu = 0.25 remembered, UNVERIFIED]; 32 sweeps of a 3 by 3 box have a variance 2 x 32 / 3 = 21 cell^2 a side, sigma = 4.6 cells = 57 km, about alpha / 2 [design: 53 km]. Cortial's three rules (R1410: oceanic under continental, the older of two oceanic under, continent meets continent in a collision) are what the column rank already gave; the regional height keeps them and ends the lottery of the continental case.

Effect (seeds 1 to 4, the flow law below on, regional sweeps 0, 8, 32, 96): boundary 7.8%, 5.6%, 4.2%, 3.7%; pieces of plates 190, 90, 53, 38 (of fewer than 200 cells: 132, 52, 23, 12). The test counts the pieces of seed 1 at 100 My: 86 with no regional height, 19 with 32 sweeps.

### Thick crust flows by channel flow (R1684 to R1686, R1691, R1692)

`crustflow` replaces the sandpile `topple`: crust of 60 km or more flows sideways down the surface slope in a weak lower-crustal channel [cited R1684]: Poiseuille flow between stationary boundaries, flux per unit width q = H^3 rho g / (12 eta) times the slope, H = 15 km, eta = 1e19 Pa s (the channel weakened by melt at 700 to 750 C) [cited R1685: Klemperer 2006 summarising Royden et al. 1997, Clark and Royden 2000 (15 km channel, 1e16, 1e18 and 1e21 Pa s under the plateau, gentle and steep margins) and Beaumont et al. 2004 (the 1e19 channel); read as that summary]. The crust is weak where its Moho is near 700 C, which standard geotherms put at 60 km of crust [cited R1686: Wang 2001, as summarised in the same paper]. In thickness the law is diffusion, d h / d t = D_h (laplacian of h) with D_h = (1 - rho_c / rho_m) H^3 rho_c g / (12 eta) = 3,694 km2/My [derived R1692], so one sweep sends the share Fo (h - h_n) / h of a column to a lower neighbour n, Fo the diffusion number of the sweep: 0.181 for a link of two weak columns (11,836 of 65,536), 0.0018 for a link to a cold column (eta 1e21 Pa s, the steep margin, the cited ratio of 100) so a plateau keeps a steep edge. The sender's composition goes with the volume (the move's exact integer shares, 7 fields, conserved to the unit). Flow goes into continental crust only [design: a continental margin is not a channel into ocean floor].

The diffusion number is D_h dt / (sweeps dx^2) with dt 2 My, dx^2 the mean cell (127.8 km2) and 320 sweeps; the explicit stencil is stable below 1/4 [design], and the number is capped at 0.2: below 1e19 Pa s (or with fewer sweeps) the cited law cannot be integrated and `Setup.flow.share` falls below 1 (it is 1 at the defaults; at 1e18 Pa s and 320 sweeps it is 0.11). The sweeps' count is numerical resolution, [design].

### The foundering depth (R1688)

The crust left past the foundering depth is cut as before (`trim`, the flux TRIM), but the depth is now 65,500 m, derived: the lower crust becomes eclogite and denser than the mantle (3.33 g/cm3) where the pressure reaches 1.8 GPa, and arc crust of at least 65 km is the Cordillera Zealandia case [cited R1688: Chapman et al. 2017, Sci. Rep. 7: 13039, read]; 1.8e9 / (2,800 x 9.81) = 65.5 km [derived]. What founders sinks into the mantle: it is the dense root, not erosion, and the surface falls only by isostasy. The foundering depth lies inside the thickness a collision can hold by force balance, 51.6 to 71.2 km for the cited 3e12 to 8e12 N/m resistance of Tibet's potential energy [cited R1687: Nature Communications 2024, read; the potential energy contrast of crust of thickness h over 35 km is (1/2) g rho_c (1 - rho_c / rho_m) d (d + 2 H0) with d = h - H0, 3.08e12 N/m at 52 km, 6.38e12 at 65.5 km, 7.65e12 at 70 km, derived and checked by integrating the columns in the test]. The ridge push is about 3e12 N/m and the slab pull about 1e13 N/m, absent below a collision [cited R1687]. Molnar and Lyon-Caen's principle, that a range spreads sideways rather than upwards once work against gravity rises, is the reason for both [cited R1687, read as the EGU summary of the 1988 paper]. The model has no force balance (poles are fixed), so the potential energy is a check, not a law the run applies.

### Landscape-scale denudation by relief (R1689, R1693, R1694)

`relief` and `waste` (erosion): a 12 km cell holds no valley, so the stream power law sees only the slope between cell means. Landscape-scale erosion follows the local relief R: E = E0 + K R / (1 - (R / Rc)^2), Rc = 1,500 m, E0 = 0.01 mm/yr, K = 2.5e-4 mm/yr per m (E = 0.46 mm/yr at 1,000 m of relief); tectonically active belts have mean local relief of 1,000 to 2,000 m in a 10 km window whatever the climate, under 2% of land has more than 1,500 m and rates above 10 mm/yr are rare; where erosion cannot keep pace with uplift mass accumulates until the relief is limited by the crust and a plateau develops, which can grow laterally but rises no further [cited R1689: Montgomery and Brandon 2002, EPSL 201: 481 to 489, read, eqs. 1 and 2 and fig. 4]. R here is the height of a cell over its lowest axis neighbour (12 km, about the width of the 10 km window) over the sea level at the least; it is held under 15/16 of Rc where the law is singular (E then tops out at 2.9 mm/yr) and integrated in eight sub-intervals of the substep so that a steep scarp loses height as its relief falls [design R1693]. The lowering of a cell is the larger of the law's and the river's incision (the law is an empirical total, so the two are not added), taken off the top of the column like the river's, and the sediment goes down the tree. A cell with no water (no rain, no discharge: the atmosphere's dry belts) has nothing to carry its debris and keeps it [design R1694]: Davy and Lague's share G w / (G w + A) is 0 / 0 there, which the first version of the chain turned into a pile-up flag at step 0.

## The second pass: the flow re-cut what erosion carved (R1695 to R1698)

The first pass left the ranges flat (the median 3 by 3 relief of the ground over 2 km 191 m after the whole chain, the flat tops half of it), and the problem named at the start, that tectonics re-cut each step what erosion carved, had survived in a new form. It was measured before anything changed.

**The cycle of a step** (`scripts/_scratch-ranges.ts`, removed after: seed 1, 100 My, uniform rain of 1,000 mm, four substeps a step; the median 3 by 3 relief of the cells over 2 km after the tectonic step, then of the same cells after the step's erosion) [R1697]:

| t | first pass: after tectonics, after erosion | flow off (0 sweeps) | second pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 My | 892, 992 m | 1,324, 1,310 m | 879, 910 m |
| 50 My | 191, 395 m | 1,109, 1,133 m | 639, 687 m |
| 100 My | 78, 132 m | 813, 842 m | 521, 558 m |

Erosion doubled the relief of the high ground in a step, and the next tectonic step took it back: the channel flow was a diffusion of each column's own thickness at the cell size (any column of 60 km or more sends Fo (h - h_n) / h to a thinner neighbour, 320 sweeps a step, a diffusion length of about 170 km a step), so a valley cut into a plateau was filled from its walls within the step, and with the flow off the relief is ten times higher. The second cause is upstream of it: a step stacks the whole closed strip into the one or two cells of the overlap (the first pass's thickening table: 25 km in 2 My at the front), and only the flow spread it.

**What was built.**

1. *The channel feels the regional load* [R1695]. The pressure that drives the lower-crustal channel is the weight of the crust over it, and the strong upper crust carries a load narrower than its flexural scale by bending, passing on to the channel only the load averaged over that scale [INFERENCE from the flexure of an elastic plate, R1411 sec. 3.16, and the channel under a strong upper crust of R1684]. Kernels `loadInit`, `loadSweep` and `loadVol` (and their twins) average each continental column's thickness over the same plate's continental cells in the 3 by 3 around it, `Sweeps.load` = 32 sweeps (sigma 57 km, the same scale as the regional height, [design]), and store it as a volume on the column's area (`FLD.VR`, `FLD.VR2`: 24 fields now). `crustflow` sends Fo (hr_j - hr_n) of thickness from a column whose regional thickness hr is 60 km or more to a continental neighbour of lower hr, and moves the regional volume by exactly the rock it moves, so a column's departure from its region (h - hr) is what the flow leaves alone: a valley stays a valley and a peak a peak, while the plateau's mean still spreads at the cited viscosity. The test states it exactly (every one of the 393,216 columns keeps its departure, to the unit, through a sweep that moves over 1,000 columns).
2. *The wedge spreads a step's stacking* [R1696]. `resolve` records in `VR` the crust an overlap stacks on the winner (the losing plate's sediment, metasediment, granitoid and volcanic rock under it) and counts it (`FLUX.STACK`, no source or sink); before the channel, `crustflow` in its wedge mode (P20 = 1) diffuses that stack over the same plate's continental cells, 54 sweeps of number 0.2 (`Sweeps.wedge`), the sender's composition moving with it. ## Gaps (open, and why)

1. **Collisions never end.** Plate poles and rates are fixed for the run, no force balance throttles convergence as the potential energy of the thickened crust approaches the driving force (England and Houseman, Molnar and Lyon-Caen: the potential energy is computed here, 6.4e12 N/m at the foundering depth, but acts on nothing), no slab breaks off, no plate reorganises. This is the cause of the steady growth of the high ground (0.034 points of the planet a My in the second pass) and of the tables at the foundering depth (the 3.5 to 5 km bump of the height-area curve, 4.6% of the planet); it needs a force model of the plates (the tectonics note's gap 2). A speed law from forces was considered and not built: every form found (the slab pull and ridge push of R1687 against a collision's resistance, with a basal drag) needs a drag coefficient or a normalisation by the drawn speed that no source here gives.
2. **The planet is small.** Orogens are 100 to 250 km wide on any planet [cited R1413: 50 to 200 km], so with Earth's tectonic pattern the high ground is about 5% x 6,371 / 2,000 = 16% [derived R1673; the 5% is UNVERIFIED]; the model's 6.8% (the chain) to 10.6% (tectonics alone) is of that order and should not be tuned toward 5%.
3. **The channel flow's viscosity is an input in a cited range with a numerical floor.** 1e19 Pa s is cited for a melt-weakened channel, 1e16 to 1e18 under the Tibetan plateau [read through Klemperer 2006, not the original papers]; below 1e19 the 320 sweeps cannot integrate the law (`Setup.flow.share`), and the flow is a diffusion in regional thickness with no temperature, no lateral change of viscosity beyond the cold/weak pair, no channel thickness change with crust thickness (H = 15 km always). The load the channel feels is averaged over the plate's flexural scale (32 sweeps, sigma 57 km, R1680's elastic thickness of 54 km); the elastic thickness of a plateau's upper crust over its channel is not read, and a column's departure from its region never relaxes by the channel (it is left to erosion).
4. **The foundering depth is a cut.** 65.5 km is derived from one pressure for one composition (Chapman et al.: the deep crust must hold more than 60% garnet-clinopyroxene cumulate to founder at the 65 km level, more than 75% in thinner arcs of 40 km, and monzodioritic arc crust does not); the root is removed whole in the step it passes the depth, with no time scale (a Rayleigh-Taylor growth time of 1 to 20 My is [UNVERIFIED]). The flux TRIM (3.5e8 km3 in the second pass, more than the arcs' 2.3e8) is delamination and continental subduction together. It acts on each column's own thickness, consistent with the local isostasy of the heights; under flexure it would act on the regional Moho (tried: it raised rims round every table, R1698).
5. **The relief law is an empirical total at a different scale.** Its relief is the height over the lowest neighbour at 12 km, standing for the range in a 10 km window [design]; the law's own K, E0 and Rc are Olympics and global fits with an order of magnitude of scatter [cited R1689]; the cap at 15/16 of Rc turns a 5 km scarp into a 1.5 km one in about 1.5 My, a design choice at the singularity. It adds to nothing in dry cells but moves rock to sediment in place.
6. **Arcs.** The arc flux per km is Earth's and does not follow convergence rate or plate age (the tectonics note's gap 3); arcs still reach the foundering depth. The 4.7 times excess of arc length is mostly gone with the boundary growth (arcs 3.37e8 to 2.25e8 km3), and 2.8 times the start's boundary length remains (4.2% against 1.7%).
7. **The boundary still grows 2.5 times** (1.7% to 4.2%): ridges lengthen as plates part (divergent 0.77% to 2.2%), the plate map is regularised by the same sweeps and a boundary cell's ownership still jitters; the regional height has diminishing returns (3.7% at 96 sweeps).
8. **The sea floor is older and deeper** (mean 3.96 km, mode 4.25 to 5.25 km against Earth's 4.3 km) because there is less turnover; no plate model's old-age cap is read (R1503 is still [UNVERIFIED]).
9. **12 km cells and local isostasy.** A cell still cannot hold a valley (erosion note's gap 7), and a valley one cell wide is compensated locally: rock removed lowers the surface by 15% of its thickness, where the flexural scale (alpha about 106 km for Te 54 km, R1680) would leave a 12 km valley almost uncompensated and lift the peaks between (the rebound of R1411's flexure spread over alpha) [derived; not built]. Building it needs the erosional unloading's departure carried with the plates (a signed field through the move) and a flexural filter of each substep's removal; the wedge and the regional load are the tectonic half of the same flexure. The sediment of a collapsing rim is routed down the tree and lands in lakes and on the shelf; no turbidity current.
10. **Sources read through summaries.** Clark and Royden 2000, Royden et al. 1997, Beaumont et al. 2004, Wang 2001 and Molnar and Lyon-Caen 1988 were read only as summarised in Klemperer 2006 (a review, read in part) and the EGU summary; their numbers are tagged [cited] for what the summary says and are not checked against the originals. England and Houseman 1988 and 1989 were not read.
11. **The second adapter was not available** (Apple M3 Pro); the check is GPU against twin, as the hard rules say.
12. **The wedge's width is one number.** 54 sweeps give a Gaussian of sigma 51.5 to 57 km (full width at half maximum 120 to 134 km), chosen inside the cited 50 to 200 km; real orogens' widths follow their taper and erosion (a critical wedge, Davis, Suppe and Dahlen 1983, not read). The stack spreads over the winner plate only (no pro-wedge on the lower plate) [design], and the step's stack is spread whole each step (no time scale for thrusting).

## Napkin note (for the repo's runbook, not edited here)

**[2026-10-19] A plate map that fragments is a symptom: rank an overlap by what a plate is, not by what each cell is, and let a law with a source stand where a cut stood.** `src/gpu/globe/tectonics.ts`: the old rule "the higher column rides over" decided plate ownership cell by cell, so continental collisions became mosaics (1.7% to 9% of the cells on a boundary, 10 plates to 248 pieces), which inflated arcs, the cut and the land lost; the cut (TRIM) at a thickness limit deleted 1.5 times the arcs' crust and did the work of erosion and delamination without a law. The ranking is now the regional height (a 3 by 3 box of the same plate, 32 sweeps), thick crust flows by Poiseuille channel flow, the root founders at the eclogite depth, and `erosion.ts` lowers a cell by Montgomery and Brandon's relief law. A law that has no water to carry its product (a relief law in a dry cell) must keep it in place: Davy and Lague's share was 0 / 0 and flagged a pile-up at step 0 of the atmosphere run, which the uniform-rain runs never showed. Do instead: test the chain with the atmosphere's rain (dry cells are exactly dry), measure the connected pieces of the plates and the boundary share before blaming speeds, and after any change in the cut or the flow count the share over 4 km, the land fraction and the pieces, not only the share over 2 km. The share over 2 km rose when the cut kept rock (17.8% with 256 sandpile sweeps), so a better conserving law can make that number worse.
