# Earth-like facts the planet must reproduce without being tuned to them (plan Unit 7, R6)

Working document, 2026-10-19. References R1480 to R1489 are this file's; R1410 to R1466 are the topic files'. Tags: [cited] [derived] [design] [gap] [UNVERIFIED] [INFERENCE] [analogue].

Rule: each fact below has a number, a source and a form of check. A miss is written into the gap list, never cured by moving a constant. The planet is smaller than Earth (radius 2,000 km, a 24 h day), so each fact says what to expect there, from the laws of the topic files. Where a fact depends on a place (Squamish, Vancouver Island) the check is a statistic of a kind of place (a west coast at a given distance from the storm track), not a place.

## The facts

### F1. Dry belts near 30 degrees

- Number [R1481, derived]: zonal mean precipitation peaks at 6.06 mm/day at 6.25 N (the ITCZ); falls to minima of 1.70 mm/day at 23.75 N and 1.87 at 21.25 S; rises to maxima of 2.78 at 38.75 N, 2.97 at 41.25 S and 3.20 at 56.25 S; and is below 1 mm/day poleward of about 76. Ratio minimum to ITCZ peak: 0.28 (north), 0.31 (south). The zonal-mean minimum is about 22 to 24 degrees, 6 to 8 degrees equatorward of the cell edge, which the reference simulation of R1432 puts at nearly 30 degrees and R1430 gives as 25 (minimum extent) to 29 (exact equal-area, atmosphere file). The WMO chapter (R1439) describes the same: a peak near 6 N, subtropical dry zones and mid-latitude maxima at 30 to 60 N and 35 to 65 S.
- Land [R1480]: arid climates (B) are 30.2% of the land, cold (D) 24.6%, tropical (A) 19.0%, temperate (C) 13.4%, polar (E) 12.8%; the largest single type is hot desert BWh at 14.2%, then savanna Aw at 11.5%; by continent arid B is 77.8% of Australia, 57.2% of Africa, 36.3% of Europe, 23.9% of Asia, 15.3% of North America and 15.0% of South America. Trenberth (R1434): depletion time of moisture by rain exceeds a month in the dry zones of the subtropics against 5 days in the tropical convergence zones.
- Planet: the exact Held-Hou edge at radius 2,000 km and 24 h is 59 degrees and the eddy-limited edge about 45 to 55, so the belt of descent lies at 1.5 to 2 times Earth's latitude (atmosphere file). Expected: the precipitation minimum at about 0.75 times the edge, 35 to 45 degrees (Earth's zonal-mean minimum is 0.78 to 0.82 of its edge of 28.8 to 30) [INFERENCE]. Check [design]: the latitude of the zonal-mean minimum between 0.6 and 0.9 of the model's own edge, and the minimum under 0.35 of the ITCZ peak; do not check against 30 degrees. To reproduce Earth's 30 degrees the planet would need a day of 7.5 h, which the game's sun and people cannot take; the plan's "compressed climate belts" are in fact belts that move poleward, and that is a decision for the operator.

### F2. Rain shadows behind ranges

- Numbers [R1482, secondary]: Forks, Washington (47.95 N, 124.38 W, 93 m, on the windward side of the Olympic Mountains) 3,046 mm/yr (119.9 in); Sequim (48.08 N, 123.10 W, 51 m, in their lee about 95 km away) 428 mm/yr (16.9 in): ratio 7.1 [derived]. The Olympic gradient in R1414 from the abstract and background: precipitation above 6,000 mm/yr in the west of the range and under 1,000 mm/yr in the east, Pleistocene ELA from 1,000 m (west) to 1,800 m (east). The Pacific storm winds are westerly. Regina (577 m, interior) 390 mm/yr and Tofino (open coast) 3,271 mm/yr at the same 49 to 50 N.
- On the rain stage's own scale (rain note R1320 to R1329): a 150 m dome of 9 km radius gives contrasts of 57 to 1,062 mm by the delay choice; the real Bornholm gives inland minus coast of +67 mm (DMI) against +120 modelled. These are the validated small-hill checks; the Cascadia-scale range has none.
- Planet: the dimension of ranges (50 to 200 km, heights set by isostasy: 5 km needs crust of 68 km) and the physics of the lift do not depend on the planet's radius, so the same windward-to-leeward ratios are expected for the same wind and moisture. Check [design]: for ranges 1.5 to 4 km high, 50 to 150 km wide, across prevailing flow, the lee side's annual rain at 100 km under the crest is under one half of the windward's (Earth 1/7 for the Olympics, 2.4 km); no condition on a place. Smith and Barstad's validity limits (R1320) apply: width over 20 km and height over 1.5 km.

### F3. A mild, wet mid-latitude west coast

- Numbers [R1482, secondary]: Tofino (49.15 N, open Pacific coast of Vancouver Island): January mean 5.3 C, July 14.7 C, year 9.5 C, precipitation 3,271 mm and 208 days; Squamish (49.70 N, head of Howe Sound, 5 m): 1.8 C, 18.5 C, 9.8 C, 2,344 mm and 191 days; Regina (50.45 N, 577 m, prairie interior): January -14.7 C, July 18.6 C, year 2.8 C, 390 mm. Differences at the same latitude: January 20.0 K (Tofino minus Regina), annual 6.7 K; the annual range (July minus January) is 9.4 K at Tofino, 16.7 at Squamish and 33.3 at Regina, so continental to maritime 3.5. [derived]
- Cause [R1450, cited]: advection of maritime air by the westerlies, the seasonal release of heat stored in the ocean's mixed layer, and the stationary waves; the ocean heat transport is a minor part (1.3 PW against 6 PW, about 4.5 C of the area north of 35 N); the winter contrast between western Europe and eastern North America can exceed 15 C and the Atlantic coast of Europe is 5 to 7 C warmer than the Pacific coast of Canada. Trenberth (R1434): rain falls 30% or more of the hours in the Northwest in January.
- Planet: the westerly belt and storm track lie near 55 to 65 degrees (atmosphere file), so the analogue is a west coast there, and the continents are small enough that the maritime air reaches most of the interior (ocean file): expect a contrast smaller than 20 K and an annual-range ratio nearer 2 than 3.5 [INFERENCE]. Check [design]: on west coasts in the westerly belt, January mean above the same-latitude interior by more than 8 K, annual range under half the interior's, precipitation above twice the interior's, and the ratios reported; no tuning of the fetch length lambda (ocean file) to land the numbers.

### F4. Fjords where ice met the sea

- Numbers [R1465 abstract, R1484 secondary]: fjords reach kilometre depths and extend tens of km inland, cut by ice because rivers cannot erode bedrock much below sea level; Sognefjord reaches 1,300 m below sea level with a sill of 100 to 200 m at its mouth; the principal fjord regions are west Norway, the west coast of North America from Puget Sound to Alaska, southwest New Zealand and Chile, all against westerlies that lift moist air onto high ranges; Norway has nearly 1,200 fjords. Lowstand of the Last Glacial Maximum: 134 m below present (R1464). Okanagan Lake, a fjord lake, has bedrock 650 m below sea level and 2,000 m below the ranges around it [UNVERIFIED secondary].
- Law check [derived]: depth below the lowstand needs grounded ice: d <= (rho_i/rho_w) h = 0.89 h, so 1.5 km of ice cuts 1.3 km (the ice article's flotation limit).
- Planet: the Equilibrium Line from the planet's own summer temperature; fjords need a coastal range whose crest stays over the equilibrium line at the lowstand, ice flowing to the sea, and a prevailing onshore wind for snow. On Earth the coastal ELA at 50 N is 1,830 to 2,260 m and falls to 1,000 to 1,500 m at 60 degrees (F5); on the planet the belt of westerly snow is at 55 to 65 degrees. The planet's smaller equator-pole contrast (atmosphere file) may leave its high latitudes too warm for ice sheets, though mountain glaciers can still reach the sea where the ELA is under the crest. Check [design]: among coastal cells whose bed is deeper than the lowstand by 150 m or more and steeper than 15 degrees, nearly all lie in cells where the modelled ice thickness at any time exceeded 500 m, on coasts with onshore snow; the fraction of such deep channels without ice is the failure rate; sills at the mouth present in most.

### F5. Snowline falls poleward

- Numbers [R1462, cited data]: ELA medians from 92 glaciers: 5,737 m at 29 N (Himalaya), 5,441 m at 16 S (Andes), 3,725 m at 35 to 45 (n 6), 2,838 m at 45 to 55 (n 27), 1,435 m at 55 to 65 (n 29), 973 m at 65 to 90 (n 21), 150 to 200 m in Antarctica (63 to 66 S); a fall of about 140 m per degree from 50 to 60; the summer temperature at the ELA is 2 to 8 C and the precipitation there 1,000 to 3,000 mm (fits P = 5.87 T^2 + 230 T + 966). The equator's snowline is 4,500 m and rises to 5,700 in the dry subtropics [R1484 secondary]. Maritime against continental: Garibaldi (49.9 N) 1,828 to 2,257 m against Ram River in the interior Rockies (51.9 N) 2,838 m. Pleistocene ELA in the west Olympics 1,000 m.
- Planet: the profile is the law's, not a target: with the planet's T_JJA(lat, z) and P(lat, z), the ELA must lie on the P/T curve and have its maximum in the dry belt near 35 to 45 degrees (the Himalaya at 29 N on Earth) [INFERENCE]. Check [design]: every glaciated cell's ELA within one standard error (650 mm of P) of the curve; ELA falling monotonically poleward of the dry belt; ELA at maritime coasts lower than at interior cells of the same latitude by 500 to 1,000 m.

### F6. Big rivers on big continents

- Numbers [R1483, secondary and R1483 Dai and Trenberth cited]: Amazon 209,000 m^3/s from 6.02e6 km^2 (mean runoff 1.10 m/yr; 5,330 km^3/yr at Obidos observed), Congo 41,400 m^3/s from 3.71e6 km^2 (0.35 m/yr), Orinoco 39,000 m^3/s from 1.01e6 km^2 (1.21 m/yr) [derived runoff]; global discharge 37,288 +- 662 km^3/yr, 7.6% of the global precipitation and 35% of terrestrial precipitation (Antarctica and Greenland excluded); the 921 largest ocean-reaching rivers drain 79.5e6 km^2, 68% of the 117e6 km^2 of land that is neither ice nor desert [cited].
- Planet: land is 1.46e7 km^2 (29% of 5.03e7 km^2), a tenth of Earth's 1.48e8. A single basin larger than 40% of one continent is not plausible; the largest basin is therefore at most about 4e6 km^2, the Congo's, and a river of 40,000 to 140,000 m^3/s at runoff 0.35 to 1.1 m/yr. Scaling law [derived]: Q_max = A_basin x runoff; runoff = (P - E) and 0.35 of land precipitation by Earth's budget. Check [design]: largest basin divided by the continent it lies on is within 0.1 to 0.4; discharge equals area times runoff within the model's own budget (a conservation check, not a fit); the number of rivers with discharge above 1,000 m^3/s scales with the area; the Amazon's own value is not a target.

### F7. Cold west-coast deserts (dry belt on a west coast)

- Numbers [R1484 secondary]: the Atacama ecoregion is a strip of nearly 1,600 km along the coast from Arica (18.4 S) southward with cold-desert (BWk) climate and mild temperatures, hyperarid since the Middle Miocene and linked to the cold current; the Namib is 80,950 km^2 on a coast with fog.
- Planet: needs the eastern-boundary upwelling that the ocean file leaves as a gap (cooling not sized), so this fact is a check that will fail on the first pass; it should be listed rather than tuned.

### F8. Hypsometry, crust and spreading

[R1411 Appendix B.2, R1412]: land 1.48e14 m^2 of 5.10e14 (29%); continents with margins 2.0e14 (39%); mean land elevation 875 m, mean ocean depth 3,794 m; continental crust 35 to 40 km, oceanic 6 km; ocean floor mean age about 100 My, continental crust about 1 Gy; spreading rates 9.7 to 154.4 mm/yr (1,696 observations), at ridges the depth is 2.5 km and grows as the square root of age (355 m per square-root My); ranges 50 to 200 km wide with crust of 55 to 70 km (a 5 km height needs 68). Check: land fraction within 0.25 to 0.33, depth law exact by construction (a check of the code, not of the planet), bimodal hypsometry with peaks near 0.1 km and 4.5 km below.

### F9. Moisture and the water budget

[R1434, R1435]: global precipitable water 24.6 mm, precipitation 2.69 and evaporation 2.78 mm/day, e-folding residence 8 days; recycling 9.6% at 500 km and under 20% at 1,000 km; moisture recycling length scales 500 to 2,000 km (tropics, mountains), 3,000 to 5,000 (temperate) and over 7,000 (desert). Check: model mean residence between 6 and 10 days, recycling in range, P - E integrated over a closed planet zero to the rounding of the integers.

### F10. Ice-age amplitude and rates

[R1464]: lowstand 134 m, 52e6 km^3 of extra ice, deglaciation 12 m/ka over 8 ka, a peak of 40 mm/yr; interglacial 4 to 8 m above present. Planet: 130 m needs 5.1e6 km^3 and about 350 m of mean ice cover on land (23% of land at 1.5 km). Check: the lowstand's volume equals the ice volume times 0.917 over the ocean area (a conservation check) and the rates scale with the ocean area (a 12 m/ka rate needs a tenth of Earth's ice flux).

## How Unit 7 would run it

A script over many seeds, one statistic per fact, written to the gap list with its number and the Earth's number side by side; the pass bands above are the sources' own spreads (the standard error of the P/T fit, the zonal-mean offset, the span of the continents' runoff) and are not tuned. A fact that fails because a law is missing (F7) is reported as a gap and stays in the table. The island generator's sibling script (`bun scripts/island-gaps.ts`) is the pattern; the runs for facts that need the full pipeline (ice ages over 300 kyr) are under the 5 minute rule only if the ice step is cheap (ice file, open 1).

## Open

1. Gauge catch correction: GPCP is not corrected for undercatch; mountain precipitation is underestimated at gauges (rain note, open 5).
2. The station normals are second hand; the fact that the Olympic gradient is 7:1 over 95 km is a measure of two towns, not of a field.
3. No source read gives the latitude of the polar front or the Ferrel boundary, so the storm track's latitude on the planet is an inference.
4. The fjord check depends on the glacial erosion exponent (ice file, open 1); the river check on the moisture budget's runoff, which depends on evaporation over land, which this note does not size.
5. The checks for the planet (the 0.6 to 0.9 ratio, the factor of 2 for the annual-range ratio) are inferences from scaling, not measured on any planet; the first results should be read as a test of the scaling as well as of the code.
