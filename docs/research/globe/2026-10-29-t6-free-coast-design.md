- Bound on lowland relief [derived]: valleys cannot fall under the sea, so a window held at a mean of H m over the sea can hold ridges of at most about 2 H m (a 4 m plain: 8 m). Hills of tens of metres on the river and K3 plains would break the tier-0 low pass that the band hold keeps, and tier 0's own plain (15.5% of land under 10 m, T5) is what says they are low. A fine uplift law cannot give those plains hills; it gives hills only where tier 0's mean is high, and there the hold's lift (the loss, smooth over two tier-0 cells: isostatic rebound, Molnar and England 1990; Montgomery 1994) already is the fine uplift.

## What is wrong, then, and the three changes

1. **The shoreline is where the fine field crosses the sea, and the hold must not know the coast.** The band hold fits the land's loss only on land cells (`btally_*`, `bgw`, `raise`), so a coastal tier-0 cell's submerged children never rise with the land and the carry's mask stays. Uplift and rebound are crustal: they lift every column of a tier-0 cell, covered or not (Whipple and Tucker 1999: U is rock uplift against base level), and what the rivers shed is deposited on the submerged part of the same cell. Build: the Gram system and the raise run over all cells of a tier-0 cell that has any land (a mask-free hold); a submerged child that reaches the sea level becomes land at one unit; the sea level stays a threshold, never a mask. Test: land share, tier-0 box error, coast positions against the old and A2 coasts; kill if the shelf under 100 m of whole cells floods, or the tier-0 means (meanErr p99) rise.
2. **A coast is shaped by the sea.** On a low coast (the river and K3 plains, and the backs of spits) the sea level crossing of a 1 m plain is set by what the waves and rivers do within a metre, which no fluvial law resolves. The cited law is the cell model of Ashton, Murray and Arnoult 2001 (Nature 414: 296 to 300) and Ashton and Murray 2006 (J. Geophys. Res. 111: F04011): alongshore flux Q = K cos^(6/5)(phi) sin(phi) of the angle between the waves and the shoreline, instability above 42 degrees, diffusion below, sand conserved. It is cheap (shoreline cells only, 1024 by 1024 at 75 m is some thousands). Wave climate: the planet's wind (`seawind.ts` zonal mean). Applies only where a coast cell is backed by land under the active profile's height (a rock coast is not a sand coast). Prototype on CPU on the tier-5 region states first; pictures decide whether it goes into the chain. Kill if the capes are the same shape everywhere (one climate) or move sand across tier-0 means by more than 1 m.
3. **Fine relief where relief can exist.** Keep the hold's lift as the uplift (no second law: a prescribed U on top of it breaks the tier-0 mean; the bound above). Fix what is left of lowland flatness in the carry and the sea: the lake of K3 and the 1-unit puddles are dry hollows of tier 0 and of the integer ramp, not lakes in the baked world (water stage, 3 m rule): judge them as the water stage sees them. The harness is extended to cube edges and tx 0 (E2) so all nine places are drawn.

Gates, never tuned to: T4's per-window maxima (`scripts/gates/straight.ts`) and the old planet's own; no 45-degree trunk, lock windows 0, sinuosity, ridge spacing 1.5 to 3 km (Perron, Kirchner and Dietrich 2009), no staircases or squared pits. Pictures at the nine places beside the old planet decide.

### Shelf statistics of the chain (key b4c6e7d1b819; `scratch/shelf0.ts` in the worktree, tier-0 `planet.height` plus the ice offset)
- 3,737 coast land cells. Sea neighbour (4-neighbour) depth: under 1 m 2%, under 10 m 18%, under 30 m 13% (33% under 30 m), 30 to 100 m 20%, 100 to 300 m 18%, 300 to 1,000 m 18%, over 1 km 11%. 47% of coasts face sea over 100 m deep in the next cell. Three cells out (7 km): 54% face sea over 1 km.
- Earth for comparison (to cite and check, not read today): shelf mean width about 65 km, very variable, break near 130 m depth, shelves about 5.5% of Earth's surface (8% of the ocean). Here the shelf is 0 to 2 cells.
- Scaling decision to make: this planet's coast per land area is about 6 times Earth's (T5: 1.4e-2 against 2.4e-3 per km). A shelf of Earth's 65 km all round 6,700 km of coast would be 435,000 km2, 23% of the planet, more than its land (25%). Matching Earth's shelf-to-land area ratio (about 0.18) gives a mean width near 13 km (5 cells). Pick one by argument and say which; the lead asked for a check against Earth's width distribution.
- The ice stage cannot set the shelf break: `planet.sea.lowM` is -0.22 m (this planet's ice is small), so the 130 m break of Earth (a relic of the Pleistocene lowstand shoreline, Kennett 1982, remembered) has no analogue; the break has to come from wave base and sediment supply (Hallermeier 1981 closure depth; Swenson, Paola, Pratson, Voller and Murray 2005, J. Geophys. Res. 110: F02013, clinoform progradation) and from the margin's crust.

### Where the chain makes (and does not make) a shelf
- `START` (tectonics.ts line 81): continents start as blobs with 30 km of crust at the coast rising to 40 km four cells in and a platform cover of 2 km thinning over 12 cells ("no passive-margin law is read", a gap): the coast is one cell from the step to oceanic crust (isostasy: about 3 km of depth in one 2.4 km cell). Rifting later makes bare new basalt and carries no taper, so margins made by plate motion have no shelf at all.
- Erosion's rings (erosion.ts `SHELF_SLOPE` 1e-3, `CREEP_SEA` 100 m2/yr [UNVERIFIED], `RING_MAX` 96 scaled) fill a graded ramp only as far as sediment supply reaches: a 3 km step is never filled.
- Plan: a margin stage after the chain (so land and the plates' history stay, only sea cells within the margin change; a taper in `START` alone would be undone by the first rift). Passive margin: stretched crust thinning from the shelf to the oceanic crust over a necking width (cite), thermal subsidence of it by age (McKenzie 1978; Parsons and Sclater 1977), shelf topset graded to wave base by sediment (Swenson 2005), sediment mass taken from the chain's erosion ledger and checked against it. Active margin (trench within a few cells, boundary class from the chain): narrow shelf, no taper. Cite Earth's shares of active and passive coast and the shelf width distribution before choosing constants. The chain stages cost about 85 s (chain), 28 s, 110 s (ice), 27 s: a rerun of tier 0 to the ice stage is about 5 minutes, so the margin can be iterated on the real chain; tiers 1 to 3 carry 10 s a stage, the region harness 26 s a place.
