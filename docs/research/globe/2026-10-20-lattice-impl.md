Chain: `docs/plans/2026-10-20-001-feat-seamless-globe-plan.md` Unit 2, on `docs/research/globe/2026-10-19-grid.md` (the cube-sphere) and `2026-10-19-window-impl.md` (`windowOf`). R-numbers R1750 to R1753 (R1754 and up are free).

## What was built

1. **The lattice** (`lattice.ts`). Each face of the grid.ts cube-sphere is cut into 222 by 222 cells of equal steps of the equiangular coordinates (`makeGrid(222)`, so `step`, `haloSource`, `locateVec` and `pointAt` of grid.ts serve it as they are): a point's cell (`cellAt`, `cellAtPlace`), a cell's centre and corners, its four neighbours (`cellStep`) and eight (`cellNeighbours`, the diagonal null for the one cell in each face that stands at a cube corner), across face edges and corners. 295,704 cells.
2. **The window of a cell** (`windowAtCell`, `windows.ts`): `windowOf` on the cell's frame. The frame is centred on the cell's centre with x along the face's X axis and y outward crossed with it, so y is not north: `turn` is y's bearing from north toward the east (R1752). `windowOf(gpu, planet, lat, lon, frame?)` takes the frame; without one it is the east and north frame it always was and the bytes are the ones it made (the staged and seed 1 pins below). The sun's direction (`skyAt(lat, turn)`, `ClimatePlace.turn`) and the planet's wind are turned by it, so a window's hill shadows and its prevailing wind are the true ones in its own axes.
3. **The blend** (`blendWeight`, `cover`, `blended`): a window's weight in its own plane is a smoothstep rise over 16 cells (1.2 km) in from each edge, in x and y; a point's windows are its home cell's and the eight round it, and the shares are the weights over their sum. `blended` samples a field of each window made (bilinear at the point's pixel, `pixelOf`) with the shares renormalised over the windows that exist, and says what share of the blend was there.
4. **Making and keeping** (`windows.ts`): `packWindow` and `unpackWindow` (a window as bytes; the polylines as one Float64Array), a store interface (memory here, a directory in the script, the browser's cache later), `Windows` (a cache of one planet's windows by (seed, sources key, cell); one window made at a time; `fetch(cell)` goes first, `want(cells)` replaces the camera's pending prefetches; a window that cannot be made is remembered and rejects), and `wanted(focus, heading, viewM, aheadM)`, the cells a camera needs in the order to make them.
5. **The script** (`scripts/globe-lattice.ts`): `--pair "lat,lon,dir"`, `--cross [--plateau]`, `--river` make two neighbouring windows through the cache, measure their shared edge and write `<out>/<name>/seam.png` and `summary.json`; `--times "lat,lon;id"` the seconds in each part of making a window.

## The lattice's size, and what the map's distortion does to it (R1751)

The plan said squares of 19.2 km. That cannot be, for three reasons, and the count is a measured one.

- **A window cannot be its own cell**: windows that share no ground cannot be blended. The window is 19.2 km (256 cells of 75 m, fixed by the island's kernels) and the cells are smaller, so each window overlaps its neighbours.
- **The cells' ground size varies**: equal steps of an angle give cells of pitch (the centre-to-centre distance along an axis) 14.15 km at a face's centre, the most, and 10.04 km along a face's edge, area 200 to 142 km2 (the grid note's ratio 1.41). The window is a fixed 368.6 km2, so it holds 1.8 to 2.6 cells' worth of ground and overlaps its neighbours by 5 to 9 km depending on the direction and the place. The 256 by 256 grid of 75 m is the same everywhere; what varies is how many windows a stretch of ground needs.
- **The cells shear**: the axes cross at 90 degrees at a face's centre and 60.2 and 119.8 at its corners. A square window aligned with a cell's X axis does not hold the cell's far corner: at a cube corner the cell is a rhombus whose far corner is 0.8 of a pitch from its centre along a diagonal. With the pitch alone under the window less its two ramps (16.8 km), 188 cells a side would do; the three windows round a cube corner then leave a hole there. The count is the least even one for which every one of 430,000 sampled points (150,000 random, points along and either side of the 12 cube edges, 20,000 within 30 km of each of the 8 corners) stands on the plateau of some window (weight 1): 222 (220 leaves 29 sampled points at 98.7% of it, 200 one at 10%, 188 one at 0). Pitch at a face's centre is then 14.15 km, under the plateau's 16.8. [measured; not proved: no point of the sphere off a plateau was found, none was exhaustively excluded]
- **Cube corners**: the three cells that meet at a corner are neighbours of one another and no fourth stands there; 24 cells (3 at each of 8 corners) have 7 neighbours. The test samples the 8 corners and the 12 edges (and either side of them); the 30 km discs round the corners were sampled by the scan that chose 222 (a scratch script, `/tmp/lat/plat5.ts`, not kept).

Windows in neighbouring cells of one face turn 0.2 to 0.3 degrees against each other (measured on two pairs); across a face edge the axes turn 48 to 52 degrees (the two measured pairs: 48.3 and 52.3), so a blend across a face edge resamples a grid turned by that, and the bilinear sample blurs by about half a cell. A camera at most needs 5 windows at once (`wanted` at view radii 1.5 to 6 km, measured over four 200 km paths).

The blend leaves a field continuous over the overlap by construction (a point's heights are a convex mix of the windows' at it); what the picture shows over the 1.2 km ramps is one window's ridges fading into the other's, which on the first pair is two unlike mountain ranges.

What the numbers say, and none of it is a retune:

- **The surface part is not the erosion's: 3 to 65 m** (62.5 m on the first pair, the same to 3e-4 along the whole edge). It is the remap (window.ts `remap`): each window shifts its own smoothed planet height by one constant so that its own mean is the planet's overlap mean (R1700), and its box is the width of the centre cell's side, so two windows over one coarse gradient differ by a constant. A remap that is a function of the ground point alone (the box by the sample's own coarse cell, no per-window shift) would make the surface agree in the overlap; that changes `windowOf` for every window and every pin, so it is not done here.
- **The detail part is the erosion's own and dominates: 66 to 764 m rms** and a correlation along the edge from -0.31 to 0.65. Two causes were seen in the stored windows: the mean-keeping search stops at four corrective runs and the last run's land mean was within 0.5% on four of the six windows made and 6.7 and 11.3% under the planet's on the two steep ones of the first pair (the others +0.4, +0.2, -0.3 and 0.0%), so those two windows' means differ by the 187 and 210 m that is of a land mean of 2,800 and 1,850 m; and the threshold slopes (window note gaps 1 and 11) give each window its own ridges and valleys at the angle of repose, which no coarse constraint fixes.
- **Rivers do not line up.** Where a window's river crosses the shared edge, the other window's is a median 1.4 to 11.5 km along it; 0 to 10% of the river cells inside the overlap have a river within one cell of the other window's. The planet's river is only there to meet where a coarse segment crosses the edge (one pair of four had a crossing over 1 m3/s: the estuary's 1,997 m3/s, which one window's river met within 0.3 km and the other's missed by 3.2 km). A river entering at a window's own edge is the planet's crossing of that edge (R1707), and the two windows' edges are not the shared lattice edge.
- **A blend of rivers is two rivers.** `blended` mixes heights; the channel and flow fields are the windows' own, and the picture draws a river where either window has one: in the overlap a river runs in each.

## Making and keeping

- **Key**: `(seed, sources key, cell id)` as `windowKey`. The sources key is the caller's: the script's `windowSourceKey` is a hash of the planet's own key and of `window.ts`, `lattice.ts`, `windows.ts`, `sky.ts` and the island's stages (`src/gpu/terrain`, `src/gpu/wgsl/terrain-*`, `src/terrain`), so a window made by other code, or stored in an older byte format, is never read as this code's (a stored window of the earlier layout was read by the new reader and came back as another window before `windows.ts` was in the key: caught by a hash that did not match).
- **Bytes**: 22 to 66 MB a window on seed 1's samples (the sea and estuary windows 22 to 23 MB, mountain ones 44 to 66). The island's fields are 22 MB of typed arrays; the rivers were 780,000 points of 7 numbers a window, 72 of 94 MB as JSON, and are now one Float64Array. Held in memory a window is its file size (rss +24 MB for the 23 MB one). `Windows` keeps 6 [design].
- **Identity**: a window of a cell is the same bytes in any visit order and through `packWindow`/`unpackWindow` (`windows.test.ts`, staged planet, cells made A, B then B, A: each pair of hashes equal, and equal across two processes). `windowOf` at no frame is the bytes it was (the staged window 39e4d2f8d7203637 and seed 1's landing window 596470d88bb2e5c4 of the window note, unchanged after every change here), and the island's climate goldens pass.
- **Time per window** (seed 1, M3 Pro; unloaded; the first number the island's rounds, the second the carried rounds below): 44.6 then 16.2 s (the mountain window of the first pair: 6 erosion runs, summit 4 km), 17.6 then 7.1, 34.4 then 6.7, 25.4 then 8.5, 23.0 then 8.0; the river pair's 6.2 and 7.5 s; a cube corner over the sea 1.9 s. Under load (a type check and tests at the same time, or another agent on the GPU) the same window took 78 and 60 s: timings here are the quiet ones and move by two with the machine.
- **What dominates** (the mountain window, 16.2 s): the erosion's six runs 11.7 s, 72% (the first run, at the probe uplift, 1.2 s, the next ones 1.3 to 3.1 s each), the water 4.0 s, the rain 0.24 s, the CPU setup (remap, rivers) 0.18 s, the ground 0.05 s. For the flat windows (7 s) the water is 3.2 s of it. The erosion's cost is in dispatches: a step runs the flood's relaxation, the landslides' and the rest in a few hundred tiny dispatches each (65,536 cells), [INFERENCE: about 50 microseconds each, from 340 steps of some 400 dispatches in 7 s on the first timing], so a run's seconds go with steps times rounds.

### Measured speedups

1. **A run that did not settle was made again from the start, and the island's 192 rounds of flood never settled a window** (R1753). `windowOf` began every erosion run at 192 rounds, the flags said the flood had not settled, and the whole run was made again at 384: every run of every window paid a failed run first. Each run now starts where the last settled (`windowUpstreamGpu(gpu, lay, whole, first)` returns the rounds it settled in; the flood's and the landslides' are separate, flood first since a flood that has not settled leaves the landslides wrong receivers), and the first at `WINDOW_ROUNDS` = 384 and 96, measured below. **The bytes are the same**: the window at 42.37 N 72.25 W made from starts of 32, 96, 192 and 384 rounds (flood and landslides both) is one hash; from 384/192, 384/48, 384/12 and 256/12 it is one hash again, the same one; and the pins held. Windows went 44.6 to 16.2, 17.6 to 7.1, 34.4 to 6.7, 25.4 to 8.5, 23.0 to 8.0 s: 2.2 to 5 times. A flood needed 384 rounds on four of the five windows and 768 on the fifth; the landslides 48 to 96 on the one measured (settled 48 on the first run, 96 after).
2. **Fewer landslide rounds help little**: 384/192 15.9 s, 384/48 15.2 s, 384/12 16.2 s (the window settled at 48 to 96 in each): the flood's 384 rounds are the cost, and below 384 the run is made again.
3. **Two windows at once do not help**: four windows one after another 37.3 s, together (`Promise.all`) 35.8 s, same bytes. The queue makes one at a time.

### What could make a window in a few seconds (not done; unmeasured unless said)

- The flood is 384 relaxation rounds every erosion step, restarted from the edge each step. The surface changes a little between steps, so a flood warm-started from the last step's filled surface would settle in far fewer rounds: erosion is 72% of a steep window and the flood the larger part of it (fewer landslide rounds saved 4%). A kernel change that must reproduce the same fixpoint (the flood is a unique fixpoint, which is why rounds do not change the bytes); [INFERENCE] it would give the largest gain, an order of magnitude on the flood.
- The uplift search runs 2 to 4 corrective erosion runs and then the whole run (6 runs on the steep window, 4 on the others): predicting the uplift (from the neighbours' windows, kept by cell, or from a first run) would take it to 2 or 3 runs. It changes the bytes (the search's last uplift is the window's), so it is a change to what a window is, not an optimisation.
- The hydrology is 3.2 to 4.2 s on every window, nearly constant: its own relaxation rounds. Not looked at.
- Windows at the same time on two devices (the page and a worker) were not tried.

## What a camera at the valley level needs

`wanted(focus, heading, viewM, aheadM)` lists the cells whose blend reaches into a disc round the focus, nearest first, the home cell first, then those along the heading up to `aheadM`, nearest to the middle of that stretch first. Over four 200 km paths (east at 20 N, north-east at 20 N, north at the equator, east at 60 N) a pan at view radii of 1.5, 3 and 6 km touches 0.08 to 0.24 windows a km (16 to 49 windows over 200 km): a new window every 4 to 12 km, 2 to 5 held at once. At 7 to 16 s a window that is 1 to 3.5 s a km, so a camera that moves more than about 0.3 to 1 km a second outruns the making and the planet under it is what is drawn until the windows arrive. A camera that wants a window ready when it arrives must have asked `aheadM` earlier: its speed times the seconds of the queue ahead of it (12 s a window times the windows queued), e.g. at 0.5 km/s with 2 windows queued 12 km. [UNVERIFIED: the valley level's view extent and the camera's pan speed are not read here; the radii above are the page's `tileM` 18 and a few hundred tiles, assumed.] `Windows.want` replaces the prefetches at every camera move and keeps what someone waits for first.

## Gaps (open)

1. **The lattice is not 19.2 km squares** (above): the cells are 10.0 to 14.15 km and sheared, 222 a side, 295,704 windows, overlapping by 5 to 9 km, so a stretch of ground needs 1.8 to 2.6 times the windows that squares of 19.2 km would. 222 is measured on a sample, not proved.
2. **The residual at the seam** (table above): 63 to 809 m rms along the shared edge, 3 to 65 m of it the remap's per-window shift and box, the rest the erosion's independent solutions with the land's mean 7 to 11% off the planet's on the two steep windows of the first pair; rivers 1.4 to 11.5 km apart where they cross. Neither blended away nor tuned: the blend over 1.2 km hides a step and shows a ghost.
3. **The remap is not a function of the ground point alone** (shift and box per window), the cause of the surface part. Fixing it moves every window and every pin.
4. **No window poleward of 64.1 degrees (10.0% of the sphere)**: the climate stage's sun table holds 80 quarter-hour rows (a 20 h day) and the polar day at the window's tilt is 82 or more (`climateRestGpu: 82 sun samples, room for 80` at the north pole's cell, face 4 (111, 111)). `windowOf` there throws; `Windows.failed` keeps it.
5. **No window at a river over 4,369 m3/s** (the hydrology's bankfull word, `hydrologyGpu: a flow passes 4375.7 m3 a second`): 147 of seed 1's 109,831 land cells carry that much (the largest 15,515 m3/s); a window a river that big flows through fails. `Windows.failed` keeps it; the planet's picture is what is there.
6. **Memory**: 22 to 66 MB a window held; the river polylines (780,000 points) are most of a steep window's bytes and the page's `Ground` rebuilds from them.
7. **A window's frame is turned from north** (its `turn`): the renderer's sun (`live.js`) and the sim's sky and weather read the island's; Unit 3 must take `window.sky` and `turn`. Across a face edge two windows' grids are turned 48 to 52 degrees against each other, so a blend across it resamples.
8. **The camera's view and speed at the valley level are assumed** (above).
9. **One planet, one machine**: seed 1 only (the store holds no other), the M3 Pro, one adapter; the times move by two with load; the pinned hashes are this engine's.
10. **Not done here**: the page's camera and modes (Unit 3); the cache in the browser (`WindowStore` is the seam; a Cache API store is Unit 3's); a tier between 12 km and 75 m (Unit 4).
