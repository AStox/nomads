# The `air` tile stage (Unit 5 and 5b): the island's rain and climate on the whole 75 m tier, one solve

Owner: AirStage. Code: `src/gpu/globe/air.ts` (the stage and its host), `air.test.ts`; widened by a format argument, the island's format giving the text it always had (checked against a snapshot of every kernel's source and of the parameter words taken before the change, `/tmp/planet-75m/air-scratch/snap.ts`, and by the stage tests' goldens): `src/gpu/terrain/{rain,climate}.ts`, `src/gpu/wgsl/terrain-{rain,climate}.ts` (and `src/terrain/climate.ts` exports `codeRose`). R-numbers R1800 to R1829.

## Unit 5b: what "one solve" means here

A cell's stored words are a pure function of the cells within a stated reach of it, so the same cell has the same bytes under any tiling (`air.test.ts`: every stored file, equal word for word, in the cells two patches share, at shifts of 16, 100 and 128 cells and with other halos, on a staged planet and on real tiles 458, 1, 14 and 17). A tiling starts on a multiple of 4 cells (the rain's coarse cells; a tile does). The halo is `AIR_HALO` = 320 cells (a patch of 896 by 896 cells, 3.1 times a tile's cells): the largest reach below.

| stored word | reach, cells of 75 m | how it is finite |
|---|---|---|
| rain (`t4-pr*`) | `rainReach` = 4 RAIN_R + 8 + 31 + 16 MEAN_PASSES + 31 = 310 | the waves are a direct sum over the response's RAIN_R = 56 coarse cells (R1812 [design], below); the block means and the scale read the blocks either side |
| sun (`t4-sun*`), sky | 123 | a horizon sample 94.4 cells of 75 m out; a lattice step of a metre is up to 1.16 cells and a cell is up to 1.13 times the nominal width |
| wind share, breeze (`t4-wsh`), pool, temperature, humidity, pet, snow cover, snowpack | 240 (the wake) + 24 (the blur) | the wake of a break is followed WAKE_REACH = 240 cells and ended there [design, R1815]: the wake curve ends at 30 of the break's heights, so a break taller than 600 m is followed only that far; salt's spray distance saturates at the same reach (it is gone after 3 km) |
| salt | local (the far salt is a global field) | the far fall-off (31 km, 414 cells) reads the planet's distance over land to the sea (`seaDistance`, a tier-0 Dijkstra made once for a planet, read by `sampleCoarse`) in place of the distance along the wind [design, R1816: isotropic] |
| fog | local | the ground's height |
| snow years | the group of 2 by 2 cells | groups are the cells' own game tiles (face position over 2), never the patch's |
| every tier-0 field (air, sea, wind, rain, surface) | local | the bilinear read of the four nearest centres |

## The rain (R1800 to R1829)

- **The transfer is a convolution** (R1810). Smith and Barstad's transfer (R487) is linear in the relief, so the orographic part of a season's rain from a wind direction is the sum over coarse cells (4 by 4 means of the detail relief, 300 m) of the relief times the transfer's response. The response is made once for a planet by the existing integer transform kernels from a 1,024 m impulse in a domain 128 coarse cells wide (a transform of 512, the wrap 150 km away), for each of the 64 (season, direction) of the batch, at the planet's land-mean wind (4.54 to 4.61 m/s, at least the atmosphere's 2 m/s gust) and land-mean sea-level air by season (20.0, 13.0, 11.3, 18.8 C), the delays being the atmosphere's (`similarityOf(radius).s`; 31 to 83 s); kept as integers (Q24 mm an hour for the impulse's 2^30 coarse word) and summed directly on the GPU (`rainConv`, a 64-bit accumulation, no float). The cell's own gain follows its warmth and wind: `gain = [esat(t) / (t + 273.15)] / [same at the reference] * U / U_ref`, times the height scale of 16 [design: the transfer's amplitude is its vapour density times the wind; its shape is held at the reference].
- **The truncation is part of the model** [design]. The response's waves decay slowly: 90% of its energy lies within 15 coarse cells of the impulse and 99% within 55 (the propagating lee wave, wavelength 2 pi U / N_m = 5.8 km, does not decay in the theory). Against the untruncated transfer (the FFT on the same patch) on tile 458's relief, four (season, direction) responses, the rms error of the truncated sum as a share of the waves' rms: R = 8: 41 to 67%; 16: 24 to 33%; 24: 18 to 25%; 32: 14 to 22%; 40: 9 to 19%; 56: 3.0 to 10.5% (`/tmp/planet-75m/air-scratch/trunc.ts`; the domain is 64 coarse cells either side of the impulse, so 56 is the largest the table holds). RAIN_R = 56 (17 km) is the largest the table can hold and the smallest that leaves under 11% of the waves' rms; it makes the halo 320. The error is the far waves' missing rain, not an edge artefact: a halo that is too small for it throws (`air: a halo of ... is under the rain's reach`).
- **The cost** of the sum is small (R = 56, a patch of 768 by 768: 0.26 s; 512 by 512 at R = 24: 0.03 s); the halo costs more (below).
- **The background** is the planet's rain made mean-keeping at tier 0 (`bgField`): the children's mean of `sampleCoarse` is 1/8, 3/4, 1/8 of a cell and its neighbours along each axis [derived], so Q solves S Q = P0 by Jacobi passes (50: the operator's symbol is at least 1/4) held at none; the planet's own tier-0 rain is then the mean of the smooth field its children read (test: 1.5e-8 relative at nine tier-0 cells including face edges and cube corners). A pure function of the planet's tier-0 field: one background for every tiling.
- **The rain of a cell** is its background plus its orographic part less the mean of that part, cut at none, then scaled (R1811, which replaces the first version's cap and its block-constant scale): (1) the orographic part is `sum over directions of share * (max(0, background + gain * wave) - background)`, the theory's own cut at none for each direction (`rainFine`, integers); (2) its mean is read between the tier-0 blocks' centres (a mean that is constant to a block steps at every edge: the edge-step ratio was 7 where the mean was constant to a block), the block means first deconvolved by MEAN_PASSES = 1 pass of the stencil; (3) the rain is cut at none; the cut adds rain where the part is large against the background, so (4) each block's mean is brought back to its background's by a scale at most 1 (the mean of the cut rain is at least the mean it was cut from; a block whose background is none keeps none), read between the blocks' centres. There is no cap on a cell's share of its tier-0 cell's rain and no stand-in constant. Reach: above.
- **What is measured** (tile 14, a wet coast, patch halo 336; `/tmp/planet-75m/air-scratch/kpass.ts`): block means against the tier-0 rain, summed |error| over summed rain, by MEAN_PASSES 0, 1, 2: 2.94%, 1.76%, 1.24%; the tile's total 1.0047, 1.0018, 1.0009 of the planet's; of 536 blocks over 50 mm: median 3.3, 2.1, 1.2%, p90 12.5, 8.8, 7.5%, worst 89, 44, 34%; the edge-step ratio (mean |d rain| across a block's edge over the mean inside blocks, 1 is no step) 0.89 to 0.99. The block mean is not exact: it is kept to what a smooth scale can do, where the planet's own rain changes fast between blocks (a coast, a rain shadow's edge) or the orographic part is large against the background. Tile 458 is a dry range (tier-0 rain none in spring): the background is none, so the rain there is none by the scale.
- **Extremes** (measured below, "Rain extremes"): a stored word cannot saturate silently: `precipWord` holds 33,150 mm a season (sqrt(mm) * 360 in a u16), the downstream words 32,767; the stage throws if any word passes its unit (`airStage.run`).

## The sun and the lattice's metric (R1813)

The patch's lattice is read by each cell's own metric: from the steps to the neighbours' unit vectors (x +- 1 and y +- 1), J in metres along east and north; the gradient map is J^-T times CELL (`gm`, Q16), so a cell's normal is in compass axes; a horizon walk toward the compass bearing k from east (counterclockwise) takes the lattice offset J^-1 (cos, sin) CELL per step, per cell (the kernel derives it from `gm`: no table); the sun table's rows, the normal and the horizons are all in the compass's bearings, with no turning by the lattice's angle. Test (`air.test.ts` "a cell's lattice metric"): a step of the lattice offsets toward east reaches 75 m east and none north to 7e-6 at a face's middle and 8e-6 in a corner tile where the axes are 26 degrees off square. Across a face's edge the same cells are read by two lattices, so a horizon is the bilinear read of the same ground along the same bearing on two lattices: not the same word; measured below.

## What was generalised, and how the island's goldens were kept

- `wgsl/terrain-rain.ts`: `RainFormat {n, coarse, cells, wide}` (`ISLAND_RAIN` = the island's); `rainConv(f, radius)`; the fine pass with per-cell words (`cells`: the background rain of an hour Q20, the turn of the rose, the gain). `terrain/rain.ts`: `rainParams(..., fmt)`, `rainWaves` (the transforms' output, for the response), `rainConvolved`, `cloudDelays(ice, s)`, the inverse's 1 / M^2 (the kernels' shifts hold it for M = 256 only), `esat` exported.
- `wgsl/terrain-climate.ts`: `ClimateFormat {n, patch}` and `layoutOf`; with `patch`: per-cell sea-level air, sea and air's water by season, the wind's turn, the band of latitude, the metric `gm`, the global distance to the sea `dsea`, the truncated wake (`XMAXQ`), the snow years over host-made groups of four cells, the blur's sum in sixteenths, the wider shore words. `terrain/climate.ts`: `PatchInput`, `runClimate` for either format.
- Kept byte for byte: the WGSL text of every island kernel (hash snapshot, checked after the last change) and the rain's parameter words; island goldens one case at a time (final report).

## The planet at tier 5 (391.1 km, 8,192 cells a face)

The stage takes the tier as an argument (`airStage(tier, block)`, files `t<tier>-*` by `bakedir.ts tierFile`); tier 4 is unchanged byte for byte (tile 458's pin `ee38721050ea4efc`, every tiling, the face edge, the resume case and the ground stage's pin all as before). What the tier changes: a tier-0 cell has 2^tier children, so the rain's blocks are 32 cells at tier 5 and the rain's reach is `4 R + 8 + (2 B - 1) + B passes + (2 B - 1)` = 390 cells, the halo `airHalo(5)` = 400 (tier 4: 310 and 320). Every other reach is in cells of 75 m and does not change (the planet's cells are 75 m at both). The snow years' groups are keyed by the tier's own face size. `tier5.test.ts` (a synthetic sparse tier-5 store) proves: every stored file is the same bytes in the cells two patches share at a cube's corner (shifts 16 and 100), at the opposite corner and along a face's edge (halos 400 and 416); the stage's files land at the tier's offsets for tiles 0, 1,023 and 1,024; a block of 2 x 2 tiles at one patch writes the bytes four single tiles do. Per tile (contended): 5.2 s a tile of its own patch (1,056 x 1,056), 2.24 s in 2 x 2 blocks, 1.12 s in 4 x 4 blocks (patch 1,824, 2.1 GB RSS): 6,144 tiles in 1.9 hours. Cost model: `2026-10-22-planet-bake-driver.md`.

## A bug found on the real tier 5 (2026-10-22), and fixed

The first real tier-5 run stopped at tile block (face 3, 16, 16): 42 words of season 2's rain past the u16 word (33,139 mm), all in the patch's halo; one cell held 55,136 mm where its tier-0 cell has 50 mm (the ground there 3,083 m beside cells at -570 m). The cause was the block scale of R1811, which the notes and the comments called "at most 1" and the arithmetic does not bound: the smooth mean read between the blocks' centres is not the block's own mean, so where a block's neighbours hold far more orographic rain than it does (a cliff) the cut rain's mean falls under the background's, and `lambda = background / cut` (about 58 there) is unbounded. It is now `min(1, background / cut)`. On the failing block no word saturates and the rain's maxima are 15.8, 12.0, 12.8 and 18.3 m a season (the planet's tier-0 maxima 11.2, 10.7, 11.2, 12.1 m). The stored words were not widened: nothing legitimate was past them. Tier 4: tile 458's words change (pin `ee38721050ea4efc` to `e387c8d10bc31d38`; the tile's rain total 1.0587 to 1.0289 of the planet's, block-mean error 16.5% to 16.7%, edge-step ratio unchanged), so lambda above 1 had occurred there too; every tiling test holds as before. Tiles made before the fix (3,648 of the real tier 5) were made again.

## Gaps (open)

1. **The truncation of the waves** (above): the rain's orographic part is the transfer's within RAIN_R = 56 coarse cells (17 km): 3 to 10% of the waves' rms is missing, more beyond what the table holds.
2. **The block mean is not exact** (above), and **dry blocks stay dry**: a block with no background has no rain, though the orographic term of the theory is independent of the planet's rain (it has no vapour field to be dry in).
3. **The response's shape is the planet's land mean** (wind and warmth by season); only its amplitude follows the cell.
4. **The wake and the far salt are finite by definition** [design]: the wake is followed 240 cells (18 km), so a break over 600 m is followed less than its 30 heights; the far salt is the isotropic distance to the sea.
5. **The grid is read as a square one only where the metric is not used**: the blur, the wavefront's steps (the wake and the spray, in lattice bearings) and the snow years' groups are the lattice's; the horizons, normals, sun and slopes are by the metric.
6. **The Taylor and Lee speed-up is not capped**: on 5 km relief the 600 m "around" gives breezes above the coast's.
7. **The planet's sea-level wind is slow** (Unit 4's gap) and the roses, the sea wind and the wet-hours' rain share are Bornholm's.
8. **Cloud, humidity at sea level, fog and beam/glow are Bornholm's** at every latitude.
9. **The planet's seasons are the atmosphere's four** and the sun's table takes Bornholm's cloud through them.
10. **Stored words are quantised**: humidity 1/255, rain 1/360 of its square root, snowpack 1/2 mm.
10b. **A cold range's snowpack holds at the word**: the kernel's pack is Q16 mm in a 32-bit word (32.8 m of water) and, in a patch, adds with a hold at the word rather than a wrap [derived]; a mean over the ten snow years reached 28.6 m (tile 1343), 18.1 m (tile 937), so a peak's last years are at or near the hold. The model has no ice flow: the snowpack of a high cold range is a number that grows with the years simulated.
11. **Pinned hashes are this engine's, this planet's.**
