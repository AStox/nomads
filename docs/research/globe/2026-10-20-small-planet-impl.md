# The planet at 195.6 km, and what the 75 m tier over the whole of it costs (plan Unit 4 of the seamless globe)

Chain: `docs/plans/2026-10-20-001-feat-seamless-globe-plan.md` Unit 4, on the generator plan (`2026-10-19-001`), the grid, tectonics, erosion, atmosphere, ice and window notes. Code: `src/gpu/globe/grid.ts` (`SMALL_RADIUS_M`), `tectonics.ts` (`makeSetup`'s speed scale, `makeHotspots`), `erosion.ts` (`runChain` in parts), `window.ts` (the radius through the planet's stages, `spinDaysFor`), `regional.ts` and `src/gpu/wgsl/globe-regional.ts` (the global finer tier, kept), `scripts/globe-planet.ts` (stages, statistics, maps), `scripts/globe-regional.ts` (the tier's stage runner). Tests: `regional.test.ts` (3 cases), `erosion.test.ts` (a chain stopped and resumed). R-numbers R1760 to R1767 (R1768 and up free).

## What was built

1. **The radius is a parameter of the planet.** The grid already took one (`tierGrid(tier, radius)`); the stages did not pass it. `buildStage`, `planetOf`, `chainStage`, `iceStage` and `climateOf` in `window.ts` now take `radius` (default `RADIUS_M`, so every pin of the 2,000 km planet is what it was: tectonics' pinned hashes for seeds 1 to 3, erosion's pinned chain, the staged window's pin and the island's goldens ran and hold), `Planet.radius` carries it, the chain's record stores it, and `scripts/globe-window.ts sourceKey(root, radius)` puts it in the store's key. The bake's planet bytes and the viewer's meta carry it (`bake.ts`). R1760: `SMALL_RADIUS_M = 4096 x 75 x 2 / pi = 195,569.6 m` [derived: the face spans a quarter turn over its cells]. A cell is 75 m across at a face's centre and 63 to 75 m over the face (the square root of the grid's 1.41 area ratio is 1.19): "75 m everywhere" is 75 m at the six face centres.
2. **What depended on the old radius.**
   - Plate speeds (`speedScale`): the default was Earth's radius ratio (a fallback the tectonics note called a gap); it is now computed from the radius given, so a plate crosses the same angle in the same time as on the 2,000 km planet: 0.3 to 3.1 mm a year, 0.0307 of Earth's (R1767) [design, gap: no source gives the scaling].
   - Hot spots (`makeHotspots`): the footprint (100 km [UNVERIFIED]) and the flux were set against 12 km cells. The footprint's radius and the flux's square scale with the radius, so a hot spot keeps its angle and builds the same cone's height (R1765) [design: a similarity of the horizontal lengths, heights kept]. Arcs need nothing: their band is 10 to 14 cells wide (`PHYS.ARC_LO`, `ARC_HI` in chamfer units) and the thickness added a step is flux over width, so the per-km flux the code reads is the similarity's already.
   - The atmosphere takes the radius from the grid (`planetOf`, the circulation's scaling of Kaspi and Showman, R1606) and needed no edit: see the gaps for what it makes. Its marching step is set by the cells (`chooseStep`: 52 s on 1.2 km cells, 500 s at the cap on 12 km cells), so a call took 249 s where it took 40 s, over the limit. The 30 days of spin-up before a window are a numerical settling, not a physical time; on the synthetic two-continent planet 5 and 10 days gave the same rain (80.4 and 80.3 mm a year, the held water's drift over the window 0.3%), so the small planet marches 10 days (`spinDaysFor`, 108 s a call) (R1766) [design, measured]. The 2,000 km planet keeps 30.
   - The ice's sensitivity (`SENSITIVITY_K_PER_WM2`) is Earth-scaled to 2,000 km, but `lgmForcing` rescales the forcing to the cited glacial cooling, so it cancels; nothing changed.
3. **The chain in parts.** The chain at 195.6 km took 257 s with a uniform rain (the atmosphere's three calls not counted), over the limit. `runChain` takes `budgetS` and `resume` (a `ChainState`: the tectonics state, the sediment in transit, the rain field and when it is next asked for, the steps done, the books) and stops before the next step once the budget has passed (every part makes at least one step); `chainStage` keeps it as `chain-part` in the store. A run in parts is the bytes of the run whole: `erosion.test.ts` "a chain stopped at its budget resumes to the bytes of the run made whole" (a run of three tectonic steps and one erosion substep, stopped after each, the state, receivers, discharge, carry and books equal).
4. **The global finer tier (`regional.ts`), kept.** What it is and why it is kept is below.
5. **`scripts/globe-planet.ts`**: `--stage chain|climate0|ice|climate1` into `/tmp/planet-small/store`, and `--report` (statistics as JSON and equirectangular maps).

- **The atmosphere makes no wind and so no rain belts.** The year's mean wind is 0.04 to 0.08 m/s in every 10 degree band to 80 degrees (0% of the area over 1 m/s; 11% over 1 m/s poleward of 80), the Held-Hou edge stands at 86 degrees (the circulation's scaling, `groupR = g z* d / (omega^2 a^2)`, grows as the radius falls: the planet has one overturning cell from equator to pole), and the rain is flat with latitude (band means 230 to 540 mm, no dry belt at 30 degrees, no wet equator, no west coast). Land gets 151 to 212 mm because moist air is rained out or does not reach it. R1606's scaling was cited from a figure over planets of 0.5 to 2 Earth radii [UNVERIFIED]; at 0.03 it is far outside it. - **The temperature contrast is 12 K** (Earth's is 50) for the same reason (`diffusionFor(radius)`: the energy balance's diffusion rises as the radius falls). The planet has no permanent polar ice (ice only on ranges), so the validation facts of the generator plan (R6: dry belts at 30 degrees, rain shadows, a wet mild west coast, snowlines falling poleward) cannot hold, and "big rivers on big continents" cannot: the largest continent is 71,000 km2 (Earth's Australia is 7.7 million) and the longest river 339 km.
- **Slopes are ten times steeper than the planet this generator was built for.** The heights are the same physical heights (crust is 35 to 70 km thick whatever the radius), 6 km of relief over continents 200 km across.
- **A hot red sliver at the south pole in seed 2's air map** (a few cells near the polar edge of the grid): not looked into.

## Gaps the small radius opens (not retuned)

1. The circulation and the temperature contrast are extrapolated far outside R1606's range (above): no wind, no belts, 94% of land under 250 mm.
2. The tectonics' lengths that are physical and were set in km are carried in cells and so are 10 times too narrow: the crustal wedge's spreading (54 sweeps of diffusion, sigma 57 km at 12 km cells, 5.7 km at 1.2 km cells, against the flexural scale of 57 km [cited R1680]), the regional load's 32 sweeps, the start's continents' margins (cells), the 150 km convergent-range zone the measure reads. Ranges are therefore narrow ridges.
3. The erosion unit's cell-scale laws are applied at a tenth of the scale they were set: the landscape-scale law of local relief (cited for 10 km windows) reads the relief over 1.2 km neighbours; the shelf's rings (`RING_MAX` 96 rings of one cell) are 115 km wide, not 1,100 km, at the shelf slope of 1e-3; creep's diffusivity (4e-3 m2 a year, a physical value) moves 63 m in a million years, 5% of a cell, not 0.5%. The relief law and the stream power law are unchanged by the similarity, creep and the shelf are not.
4. Gravity is 9.81 m/s2 at any radius (a body of 195 km radius at Earth's density has 0.30 m/s2): the ice law, the isostasy and the atmosphere's scale height take Earth's.
5. The atmosphere's step is 52 s on these cells and the spin-up is cut to 10 days (R1766): a call is 65 to 110 s, not 40 s on 2,000 km; the climate is converged to 0.1% in rain on a synthetic planet and not checked on the real ones.
6. Plate motion: 0.3 to 3 mm a year is a similarity (R1767), not a measurement of anything.
7. The grid's cells are 63 to 75 m across (face centres to edge midpoints) and the axes cross at 60 degrees at the cube's corners: "75 m everywhere" is 75 m at six points.
8. Continents are 5 to 70 thousand km2: a game play area of 19.2 km (369 km2) is 0.6% of the largest continent of seed 1 (58,941 km2); there is room for a few such areas, one range, one river system of a few thousand km2 each.
9. The coarse planet is the generator's tier 0 (1.2 km cells), 12x finer than the old planet's; the rock and rain of a 75 m cell are 1.2 km values (16 cells to a side).

The flood is 80% of a step and its rounds are the longest water path in cells at one cell a round (1,280 rounds to cross seed 1's 3,300-cell longest river twice over: a depression's fill adds to it). Tier 4 has 4x the cells and 1.4x the rounds of tier 3 and its flood costs 4.7x.

- **Staged calls under 5 minutes.** At 270 s a call, a tier-4 erosion of 40 steps (the number the tier-3 bake on the 2,000 km planet was run for, whose receivers still changed by 1.2% over its last 4 steps) is 800 s: 3 calls (13 steps each); a state is 403 MB of heights and 1.6 MB of carries. Coarse to fine shortens it: the tiers below cost 4.2 s a step at tier 3 (40 steps, 170 s, one call), about 0.5 s at tier 2 (measured on the 2,000 km planet's heights), so the tier-4 run starts from the tier-3 result and needs fewer steps (not measured how many). The island's other fine stages (hydrology, rain, climate, soil, ground) are not measured at global size: a window's 65,536 cells took 7 to 16 s, 72% in dispatch counts the global kernels do not pay per cell; no figure is claimed for them.
- **Disk for the tile pyramid** (never written here): a tile is 256 by 256 cells, 16 by 16 of them on a face's side at tier 4: 1,536 tiles at tier 4 and 2,046 over tiers 0 to 4 (510 over tiers 0 to 3). The page's render set is [INFERENCE: the 14 fields `windowBytes` names, quantised: height 2 bytes at 0.25 m, rock, drift, drift depth, temperature, water, flow, the five covers and soil one byte each, rain two] 16 bytes a cell: 1.61 GB at tier 4, 2.15 GB with the pyramid; at tier 3 0.40 and 0.54 GB. Measured: the heights alone, in 0.25 m Int16 and gzipped (level 6) over 171 tiles with land at tier 4, are 0.571 of their bytes (0.642 over 227 tiles at tier 3), on terrain no finer than the remap and three steps of erosion, so a proxy; with every field and that ratio the pyramid is about 1.2 GB at tier 4 and 0.35 GB at tier 3. The simulation's Ground has some 35 float fields a cell: at 4 bytes that is 14 GB at tier 4 for the whole planet, which is not kept: the play area's fields are derived from the stored ones when a play area is cut (Unit 6).
- **What the page streams.** A tile is 1.05 MB raw, about 0.6 MB gzipped. A 1600 by 1000 screen at 2 screen pixels to an art pixel is 800 by 500 art pixels: at the tier whose cell is an art pixel that is 3.1 by 2.0 tiles, 12 tiles in practice and 20 at worst: 7 to 12 MB to fill the screen at any zoom, a third more for the coarser tier a cross-fade holds, and 4 to 5 new tiles (3 MB) for every tile width panned. The whole planet seen from afar is the 6 tiles of tier 0 (3.6 MB) and the finest tier a view ever asks for is tier 4 at the `close` level. The page holds a cache of tens of tiles (a few tens of MB), never the planet.

**Recommendation: 4,096 a face.** The global erosion step is 19.9 s (24.5 s on the planet with the longest rivers), the flood needs 1,280 to 1,664 rounds against a sort that holds paths of 16,382 cells (3,854 the deepest seen), the solver's 5.6 GB sits in one device with room (9.5 GB peak of 18), a bake of the erosion is 3 calls, and a 2,048 planet would give the game 150 m cells where it is tuned at 75 m (the sprites and the island's kernels are built for 75 m, so a 150 m planet would need its play area refined from it: the per-place detail the operator rejected). What this does not settle, and Unit 5 must measure before it builds on it: the island's other fine stages at 100 million cells (each field is 403 MB, so a stage holding 12 fields is 4.8 GB and the stages must run in groups of fields), and the disk a bake needs between stages (the planet is fine at 1 byte a field: 100 MB a field; 35 fields as floats are 14 GB). If those do not fit, 2,048 is the fallback with its cells at 150 m and every number here a quarter.

## What of the regional tier is kept

`src/gpu/globe/regional.ts` and `src/gpu/wgsl/globe-regional.ts` are a global solve of a finer tier over the whole planet with flow routed across tile and face edges: it is what Unit 5's coarse-to-fine erosion needs. Kept: the remap tier 0 to tier t that depends only on the ground point (`remapHeights`: bilinear between tier-0 centres across face edges, the children of a cell summing to its total to the unit; `remapAmounts` for rain), the kernels (the flood by rounds, receivers by steepest descent over eight neighbours found from a cell's place, depth by pointer jumping, the discharge area gathered deepest first, the implicit stream power step n = 1, threshold hillslopes by rock, the uplift of each tier-0 cell's land by its mean loss so its total is held, with the remainder carried: R1760 to R1764), the table of the tier-0 cell a cell lies in (rock's erodibility, area weight, distances, threshold falls), `Regional.gather` (any weights summed down the forest), a stage runner that keeps its state by step and resumes to the same bytes (`regionalOf`, `scripts/globe-regional.ts`), and the profile. Dropped: the window-pinning parts (the channel list with each season's runoff, `riverAt`, the tier's sampling at a ground point, `tierAround`, the changes to `windowOf`, the export for the view), none of which was committed. Fields and limits: 13 i32 fields in two buffers (`SPLIT` 7), ids to 2^32, depths to 16,382 (flagged), discharge areas to 2^32 (flagged, saturating), a parent's loss in an i32 (256 children of 8 km: under 2^31).
- R1760 the tier's number, tier 3 on the 2,000 km planet (12,272 / 8 = 1,534 m) and tier 4 here [design]; R1761 the remap [design]; R1762 the uplift: every land cell of a tier-0 cell rises by the mean height its land cells lost in the step, so the cell's total height is the planet's and the relief is what the stream power law and the thresholds make of it [design: the planet's own uplift, which the chain does not keep, is the denudation a quasi-steady tier-0 cell is held at]; R1763 a step of 1 My and 40 steps [design]; R1764 the threshold slopes of the window (`THRESHOLD_DEG` by rock, `ROCK_OF`, R1714) on the tier's cells.

Run end to end through the store at tier 1 (`scripts/globe-regional.ts`).

## Unit 4b: Earth's laws by dynamic similarity (R1768 to R1782)

The operator chose Earth's behaviour on the 195.6 km planet. Rule (`similarity.ts`): s = radius / Earth's radius = 0.030697. Velocities, heights, depths of the solid Earth, temperatures and fluxes per area are Earth's; lengths along the surface are Earth's times s, so flow times are Earth's times s and rates Earth's over s; the tectonic clock is not scaled (plates cross Earth's angle in Earth's time). The 2,000 km planet is the generator's reference, its constants Earth's own (set 2026-10-19 against 12 km cells, pinned by the tests), so `similarityOf(RADIUS_M)` scales nothing and every pin held; every other radius is similar. The game's own 24 hour day and sky (`sim/sky.ts`, the sim) are not touched: the spin is the planet's climate parameter only.
