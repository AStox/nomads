## Laws and constants

### 1. Insolation and seasons (R1438, R1437)

Daily mean insolation at latitude phi, declination delta, distance r: Q = (S0/pi)(a/r)^2 (h0 sin phi sin delta + cos phi cos delta sin h0), with cos h0 = -tan phi tan delta (polar day h0 = pi, polar night Q = 0) [derived from eqs. 5 to 7 of R1438, who integrate numerically]. sin delta = sin(eps) sin(lambda), eps the obliquity (23.44 for Earth, an input), lambda the orbital longitude. S0 1366 W/m^2 [cited]. The annual mean at x = sin phi is S(x) = 1 - 0.477 P2(x) (times S0/4) for Earth's tilt (the P2 amplitude -0.477 [cited, R1437]); it depends on the tilt, which the planet takes as an input, so recompute it by integrating Q [derived].

### 2. Temperature with latitude and season (R1437)

Budyko-Sellers balance: C dT/dt = Q (1 - alpha) - (A + B T) + D d/dx[(1 - x^2) dT/dx], T in C. Cited constants: A 203.3 W/m^2, B 2.09 W/m^2/K, D 0.649 W/m^2/K (fitted to the northern hemisphere's observed second mode, T2 = -28 C; D/B = 0.31); global mean T0 14.97 C for (1 - alpha) 0.70; the annual mean solution T(x) = T0 + T2 P2(x) with each mode T_n = H_n/(B + n(n+1) D); for n = 2 the book computes H2 = -0.500 (in units of Q = 340 W/m^2, as I read it), so T2 = -170/(2.09 + 6 x 0.649) = -28.4 C against the observed -28.0 [cited, checked here]. Heat capacity per area: land C_L = 0.16 B years, an ocean mixed layer of 75 m C_W = 4.7 B years [cited] (3.1e8 J/m^2/K for the sea, which is 1,025 x 3,990 x 75).
- Seasonal swing of a column forced by F cos(omega t): amplitude F / sqrt(B^2 + (omega C)^2): land F/(1.41 B), ocean F/(29.5 B), so the land swings 21 times the sea's before advection moves heat across the coast [derived]. This is continentality; coasts take a blend by upwind land fraction (below).
- Lapse rate 6.5 K/km (R494, in `climate.ts` LAPSE), moist-adiabatic 5.3 to 6.7 K/km and environmental 4.8 to 5.8 K/km by season in the rain note's soundings (R495) [cited]. With g and c_p fixed, dry 9.8 K/km = g/c_p [derived]; the freezing level z_f = (T_sl - 0) / Gamma gives the rain stage's ice share exp(-z_f / H_w) (R1320 eq. 10, rain note) without a sounding.
- Ice-albedo: A and B are for today's cloud and gas; B = 2.09 W/m^2/K is close to the 2 W/m^2/K sensitivity Seager et al. (ocean file) quote for their runs [cited]. The ice line is the -10 C isotherm; the co-albedo 1 - alpha is 0.70 ice-free (50% cloud) and 0.38 ice-covered (Budyko's albedos 0.32 and 0.62), giving T0 of 15.0 C and -36.4 C for a planet wholly ice-free or wholly ice-covered [cited, R1437 secs. 2 and 4].

### 3. Circulation from latitude, tilt and rotation (R1430, R1433, R1431)

Cells (Earth): thermally direct Hadley cells to about 30 degrees, Ferrel cells in midlatitudes driven by eddy momentum fluxes (angular momentum is not conserved along them), a weak polar cell. Surface zonal wind: easterlies in low latitudes, westerlies in midlatitudes, weak easterlies near the poles; mean meridional surface wind poleward under the westerlies and equatorward under the easterlies and weaker than the zonal [cited, R1430, R1431].

- Torque law: the surface drag integrated over a sphere must vanish: integral of tau_x cos^2(phi) dphi = 0 over -90 to 90 [cited in words as "momentum must be removed at the surface", R1431; the integral is derived]. With tau ~ u^2 and the cos^2 weight, trade winds over |phi| < 30 and westerlies from 30 to 90 need |u_w|/|u_t| = 1.25; for a belt edge at 40 degrees the ratio is 1.8; at 59 degrees it is 3.8 [derived]. So an edge near 60 degrees would leave a westerly belt too small to balance the trades, which then must weaken; a global-Hadley regime has weak surface winds (R1432: superrotation at slow rotation).
- Held-Hou nearly inviscid, steady, axisymmetric: AMC upper wind u = Omega a sin^2 phi / cos phi; vertical-mean AMC temperature theta_m(y) = theta_m0 - (theta0 Delta_h / (2R)) y^4/(1 - y^2), y = sin phi; radiative equilibrium theta_E = theta_E0 - theta0 Delta_h y^2. The cell edge y_H and the offset c solve: continuity c - y_H^4/(2R(1 - y_H^2)) = -y_H^2 and equal area integral_0^{y_H} [-y^2 - c + y^4/(2R(1 - y^2))] dy = 0 [derived; reduces to the cited small-angle edge phi_H = (5R/3)^(1/2)]. R = g z* Delta_h / (Omega^2 a^2 T0) [R1430 eq. 1's group]; Earth: Delta_h/T0 = 80/295 = 0.271, z* 15 km give R = 0.185, small-angle edge 31.8, exact edge 28.8 degrees, minimum edge from Hide's theorem phi_M = (g z* Delta_h/(Omega^2 a^2 T0))^(1/2) = 24.6 (R1430 gives 25) and exactly 22.4 [derived against cited]. Observed: cells reach nearly 30 degrees, jet 30 m/s at 45 degrees and 200 hPa in the reference run (R1432) [cited].
- Eddy limit: the circulation is baroclinically unstable poleward of phi_c(z*) = (g z* Delta_v / (2 Omega^2 a^2 T0))^(1/4) (R1430 eq. 3, my reading of its two-layer criterion; the factor 2 and Delta_v, the gross stability, are as I derived them from the criterion -d<T>/(a dphi) = (beta/f) Delta_v and may differ from the paper's printing) [INFERENCE]; the edge is the smaller of phi_M-like and phi_c (Held 2000, as R1430 describes it). Calibrated so that phi_c(Earth) = 30, it scales as (Omega a)^(-1/2). R1430 reports that simulations fall between: extent falls with Omega more slowly than 1/Omega and rises with falling lapse rate [cited].
- Seasons: with the heating maximum off the equator (phi_0, Lindzen and Hou), a small asymmetry gives a much stronger winter cross-equatorial cell, which moves the wet belt (ITCZ) with the sun (R1433; the size of phi_0 is [gap]: the surface lags the sun by the sea's heat capacity; use the EBM's seasonal temperature maximum for phi_0 [design]).
- Rossby and Rhines: planetary beta = 2 Omega cos(phi)/a; U = 15 m/s gives a Rhines length pi sqrt(U/beta) of 2,730 km (Earth, 30 N) and 1,530 km (a = 2,000 km); Rossby deformation radius N H / f at 45 degrees is 780 to 1,160 km for N 0.01 to 0.012 and H 8 to 10 km, the same on any planet with the same Omega [derived]. R1432: the number of jets and the heat-transport efficiency follow the ratio of eddy length to planet size.

### 4. What changes at radius 2,000 km and a 24 h day [derived unless tagged]

| | Earth | planet (a 2,000 km, 24 h) |
|---|---|---|
| Omega a | 465 m/s | 146 m/s (as Earth rotating at 0.31 of its rate: a 76 h day) |
| R = g z* Delta_h/(Omega^2 a^2 T0) | 0.185 | 1.88 (x 10.1) |
| Hadley edge, exact equal-area | 28.8 degrees | 59.4 degrees (a = 3,000 km: 48.9; 2,575 km, Titan's size: 53; 1,000 km: 73) |
| eddy-limited edge, scaled from 30 | 30 | about 54 (small-angle formula past its range; treat as 45 to 55) [INFERENCE] |
| Rhines length, U 15 m/s at 30 | 2,730 km | 1,530 km |
| deformation radius / planet radius, 45 degrees | 0.12 to 0.18 | 0.39 to 0.58 |
| beta at 45 degrees | 1.6e-11 | 5.2e-11 |
| 24 h day gives Earth's edge when a is | | 6,371 km; or a day of 7.5 h for a = 2,000 km |

So the belts are not compressed; they move poleward. Under axisymmetric theory the dry subtropical belt (descent at the cell edge) sits at 45 to 59 degrees, not 30, and the eddy-driven westerlies and storm tracks poleward of it, at about 55 to 70 degrees; a wet, mild, westerly coast like Vancouver Island would be at 55 to 65 degrees on this planet [INFERENCE]. The baroclinic eddy and the planet are of one size (deformation radius about half the radius), so the storm track is planetary-scale waves with a wave number of 2 to 3 around a latitude circle (circumference at 55 degrees is 7,200 km; the Eady wavelength is about 3.9 x 800 = 3,100 km) [INFERENCE]. Kaspi and Showman's smaller planets have a smaller equator-to-pole difference (direction cited; size [UNVERIFIED]); a fixed eddy diffusivity K = D R^2/(rho c_p H) (2.6e6 m^2/s from North's D with a column heat capacity of 1.0e7 J/m^2/K) would give D/B = 3.1 and a seven-fold smaller T2, while K proportional to R gives D = 2.1 W/m^2/K and T2 about 2.4 times smaller; the cited run suggests under two-fold [INFERENCE]. Use K proportional to R as a design choice, labelled a gap.

Gravity: the plan does not say. A 2,000 km planet of Earth's density has g = 3.1 m/s^2; the game's people, fall and fire all use 9.81. R above does not depend on g if z* scales as 1/g (the tropopause at the same pressure), but the lapse rate g/c_p does: 3.1 K/km dry, so a mountain cools 3 times less per metre and every snowline rises by the same factor in metres. Choose g = 9.81 for the atmosphere (a planet of density 17.6 g/cm^3, not rock, flagged) so every law the game already holds stays true [design]; this is a decision for the operator.

### 5. Winds from the cells [design, from the laws above]

Zonal-mean surface wind, by band: trades (u < 0, v toward the equator) from the equator to the edge; westerlies (u > 0, v poleward) from the edge to the polar front; polar easterlies beyond it. Amplitudes from the torque law and the drag law tau = rho C_D |U| U (rho 1.2, C_D 1.3e-3 [UNVERIFIED: not read]); the maximum stress seen from space is 0.25 Pa (Southern Ocean; 12.7 m/s at that C_D) (R1440). The meridional mass flux of the surface layer follows Ekman: M_y = -tau_x / (rho f), 1,230 m^2/s for tau 0.056 Pa at 15 degrees [derived]; capped at |f| of 5 degrees' value near the equator. The polar front's latitude is [gap]: not read (Earth 60 to 65 is remembered). The local wind rose in the rain stage's form (16 sectors) is the zonal-mean wind direction plus eddy scatter; Bornholm's measured rose has a circular spread of 66 degrees in wet hours (R-bar 0.51) and 89 in all hours (R-bar 0.30) [derived from `climate.ts` WET_ROSE and ROSE]; trades are steadier [gap: no figure read]. Use a wrapped normal with spread 40 degrees in the trades, 65 in westerlies, 90 in the polar easterlies [design].

### 6. Moisture: sources, advection, depletion

- Clausius-Clapeyron: saturation vapour pressure rises about 7% per K (7.3% at 0 C, 6.3% at 20 C) [cited, R1436; derived values]. Tetens e_s = 0.6108 exp(17.27 T/(T + 237.3)) kPa, 0.61 at 0 C, 2.34 at 20 C (R221, `climate.ts` esat).
- Column budget: P - E = -div(vertically integrated vapour flux) [cited as the reanalysis method, R1434]. Global means: precipitable water 24.6 mm, E 2.78 mm/day, P 2.69 mm/day, e-folding residence 8 days (P 8.1, E 8.5); local depletion time 5 days in the tropical convergence zones and over a month in subtropical dry zones; mid-latitude precipitation falls about 10% of the hours, so conditional rates are ten times the means and a wet hour drains a layer in about 12 hours [cited].
- Recycling (R1435): rho(x) = 1 - exp(-x/lambda) of the rain at distance x over land comes from evaporation within it; length scales 500 to 2,000 km in the tropics or mountains, 3,000 to 5,000 km in temperate climates, over 7,000 km in deserts; time scales 3 to 20 days; at 500 km scale recycling is 9.6% globally (8.9 land, 9.9 ocean) and under 20% at 1,000 km (R1434) [cited].
- The planet's land is small: the whole sphere is 12,600 km round, and at 29% land (1.46e13 m^2) a continent is 2,000 to 5,000 km across, no more than one e-folding (lambda 3,000 to 5,000 km) from the sea at most, so continental interiors are less depleted than Earth's and there is little continentality in rain [INFERENCE]. Temperature continentality remains (section 2).
- Scheme [design]: a wet hour's air carries W = RH x rho_vs(T) x H_w (the rain note's moist layer; W near 15 mm at 10 C, 25 mm global, 50 mm tropics) along the mean wind field; ocean evaporation by the bulk or Penman law (the window's `pet` kernel, R221, is already in the code) adds W; large-scale rain is condensation by ascent, P = C_w w with C_w = rho_vs Gamma_m/gamma (R1320; about 17 mm/day for w = 1 cm/s at 20 C, ITCZ order [derived]), w from the convergence of the Ekman layer; orographic rain is the rain stage's theory on top; rain is limited by what the column holds: P dx <= W U (a sweep of first-order upwind updates along the wind, a few hundred passes over a face of 256 cells, all integer) [design]. This limit is the missing physics behind the island's rain overshoot (rain note, open 4 and 5): the theory takes the layer saturated and never runs out.
- Over land after the sea: E_land = min(PET, water available) with a bucket of soil [design]; P - E is the runoff the rivers carry; the discharge of the Amazon at Obidos is 5,330 km^3/yr from rain supplied by the whole basin and its recycling (Dai and Trenberth, validation file).

### 7. Orographic rain on the terrain: what the rain stage supplies

Smith and Barstad's validity: hills over 20 km (upslope overestimates by 5 and more below), ranges over 1.5 km, stable air, no convection (R1320, R1321, R1323).

## What a kernel needs (units and scales)

Per cell and month (12 steps a year): temperature at sea level (Q8 K or Q16 C), sea-ice cover, column water W (Q16 mm), surface wind (u, v) (Q16 m/s), large-scale ascent w (Q20 m/s), ocean evaporation and PET (Q16 mm/day), P_ls and P_oro (Q16 mm/month), runoff (Q16 mm/month), soil bucket (Q16 mm). Insolation and cell edge are host tables (a 90 x 12 table of Q) from sections 1 and 3; the equal-area solve is a host bisection. Latitude, longitude and the cell metric come from the sphere grid (Unit 1). Random inputs: none beyond the seed's plate layout; weather is a climatology.
