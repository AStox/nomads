## What was built

A planet of the tier-0 grid (six faces of 256 by 256 cells, 393,216 cells, radius 2,000 km), a seeded set of rigid plates, and a run of 100 coarse steps of 2 My (200 My). Every cell holds a column of rock; each step moves every parcel by its plate's rotation, resolves what overlaps and what parts, adds arcs, hot spots and pelagic sediment, lets crust past the thickness limit flow aside and cuts what remains. The heights are isostasy. A whole run takes 2.3 s on the M3 Pro once the kernels are compiled (6.3 s the first time, plus 4 s for the grid's tables); a step is some 70 dispatches in one command buffer.

Entry points: `makeSetup(params)` (plates, poles, rates, hot spots, arcs' clocks, the kernels' tables), `startingState`, `Tectonics` (the kernels on a device: `step`, `elevate`, `read`, `fluxes`), `runPlanet(gpu, setup)` (the whole run), `derive` (height, thickness, rock, plates, ages in flat cell order), `measure` (the checks), `classifyBoundaries` (convergent, divergent or transform by relative velocity), `stateFrom` (a planet staged from a column for each cell, for tests and later units), `Twin` (every kernel in TypeScript). `bun scripts/globe-maps.ts --seed 1 --out /tmp/globe-1` writes the maps (`Layer`s and `writeLayers` are exported for the next units).

## Fields, units and scales

All i32 words in the grid's padded tile layout (`FLD` in `wgsl/globe-tectonics.ts`). The conserved thing is **volume**, not thickness: V = H a / 16 for a thickness H in m and the cell's area weight a (Q15 of the mean cell, `T`), so a volume unit is 16 m over 1/32,768 of the mean cell, 62,400 m3 [derived: 16 m x 127.8 km2 / 2^15], and a thickness is V / a. Under 2^28 for a column of 70 km; stacks of several fit a u32.

| field | what | unit and scale |
|---|---|---|
| VB, VS, VM, VG, VV | volumes of basalt (oceanic crust), sediment, metasediment, granitoid, volcanic rock | volume units (above) |
| AGB | the basalt's age, summed: (VB >> 10) x age | age in units of 0.5 My, at most 800 (400 My); mean age = AGB / (VB >> 10) |
| ORG | the time since the column last grew by convergence, an arc or a hot spot: (V >> 10) x time | the same units |
| PL | plate | 0 to plates - 1, 255 none |
| Z | surface height above the reference level (where 35 km of crust of 2,800 kg/m3 stands) | m, i32 |
| SUB | basalt that went under in the step | volume units |
| DIST, DIST2 | distance over the overriding plate from the trench | chamfer units of 5 a cell (7 on a diagonal) |
| PL2, E2 (7 fields) | the double buffers of the plate relaxations and of the crust's flow | |

Per cell, as the task asks, and as `derive` reads them: crust type (continental when the rock that is not basalt or sediment outweighs the basalt: continental crust, arcs and plateaus, the buoyant crust that does not subduct), crust thickness (the five volumes' sum over a, crust and sediment together), age (the sea floor's), orogeny clock, plate id, lithology by origin, height by isostasy with the sea level a parameter.

**Lithology by origin** (R1520): basalt (oceanic crust, made at ridges); sediment (pelagic on the sea floor, platform and margin cover of a continent); metasediment (sediment scraped on at a trench and deformed: the accreted wedge); granitoid (the old continental shield and the batholith an arc builds); volcanic (arcs and hot spots). The class that shows at the surface (`derive.lith`): a cover of sediment or of volcanic rock if it is 1 km thick or more [design], else the thickest of basalt, metasediment and granitoid. Nothing is painted: each volume comes from the process that made it.

## Order in a step

1. `elev`: the height of every column (the rank of a parcel in an overlap).
2. `claim`, `deposit`, `resolve`: the move (below). Sum: every parcel is carried by its plate's rotation, its volumes shared among four cells; a gap fills with new basalt, an overlap sends the lower plate's basalt under and scrapes its sediment and other rock on.
3. `gapfill` (16 sweeps): a cell no parcel owned takes the plate of a neighbour, from both sides by distance, so that a ridge is shared between its plates.
4. `plateSmooth` (2 sweeps): a cell that has at most 2 of its 8 neighbours on its own plate and at least 5 on one other takes the other.
5. `distInit`, `distSweep` (16): the distance from the trench cells (SUB over 0) over the same plate.
6. `magma`: arcs 10 to 14 cells from the trench, hot spots, pelagic sediment, then the age and orogeny clocks.
7. `topple` (32 sweeps), `trim`: crust over the limit flows to the neighbours in proportion to their room, and what is left is cut.

The sweeps are numerical convergence of relaxations, [design]; each is a double buffer, so the count is even.

## The move: cloud in cell (R1500)

A parcel is a cell's column. The plate's rotation for one step (a Q30 matrix from Rodrigues' formula, `rotation`) carries the cell's centre (a Q30 unit vector in the tables) to a point; the point is found on the cube-sphere by integer search of the face's tangent nodes (the largest of the six face dot products, then a binary search of a/w and b/w against the 257 tangent nodes, then the fraction of the cell in Q16, linear in the tangent). Its volumes are shared among the four cells nearest it by bilinear weights in Q16 summing to exactly 2^16; the three smaller shares floor and the nearest cell takes the rest, for each of the seven extensive fields, so **every volume's total is conserved to the unit** and no cell is a hole. Across a tile or face edge the neighbour is the grid's own (`source`, `haloSource`); at a cube corner, where one of the four is missing, its weight goes to the cell itself. All of it integer; the accumulations are integer atomics (`atomicAdd`, `atomicMax`), which are order-free, so a run is the same bytes every time (the twin sums in a fixed order and agrees bit for bit).

This is a **conservative semi-Lagrangian remap by cloud in cell** (a particle-in-cell scheme with one parcel per cell, re-gridded every step; Hockney and Eastwood 1988 and Birdsall and Langdon 1985 are its textbooks, named from memory, not read [UNVERIFIED]; the application to plates is [design]). Its numerical diffusion: a parcel shifted by a fraction f of a cell is shared (1 - f, f), a variance f (1 - f) cell^2 per axis each step, 1/6 on average, so a front blurs by sqrt(n/6) cells in n steps: 4 cells (50 km) in 100 steps [derived]. It carries mass exactly, with no stall at a shift under half a cell: a blob of continent carried 1,530 km in 20 steps by a plate of 40 mm/yr is within 0.07 km of the rotation's own answer (0.01, 0.04, 0.07 and 0.09 km after 1, 5, 20 and 50 steps; the first moment is kept, `tectonics.test.ts` bounds it at 0.5 km). The coverage of a cell (the sum of the parcels' area in it over its own) is 1 inside a plate to 1e-3 (p10 to p90 of the interior cells of the start, 0.999 to 1.000), so the dead band of 1/16 on either side has room; it is [design].

Covering is the rule of the research note's covering test, by sums instead of a search for each plate: a cell whose coverage is over 17/16 of its area is an **overlap** (convergent), one under 15/16 a **gap** (divergent), anything between is shared (transform). The kinematics decide the class, with no boundary list: this is "boundary classification by relative velocity" carried out exactly, since parcels of plates with a closing relative velocity overlap by the closing distance. `classifyBoundaries` redoes it from the poles in float for the tests and maps (the relative velocity at the midpoint of two neighbouring cells of different plates resolved along the line between them; under sin(20 degrees) of the speed a transform [design]).

In an **overlap** the plate whose parcel stands highest (`Z` plus 8,192, a 16-bit rank, with the plate number to break ties) owns the cell, if its parcel holds at least 1/32 of it; the others' share in the overlap, (coverage less the cell) over their coverage, goes under: their basalt leaves (the flux SUB), their sediment is scraped on as metasediment, their granitoid, volcanic and metasedimentary rock stay (R1516, R1517: [cited R1410] an oceanic plate goes under a continental one, of two oceanic plates the older goes under, continent meets continent in a collision; here all three are one rule, **the higher column rides over**, since the thermal buoyancy of young floor and the crust's buoyancy are in the height, and the older, deeper floor loses to the younger [design, generalising the rules]). Other cells are owned by the plate with the most coverage (a parcel bids with at least 1/8 of the cell). In a **gap** the bare area, (area - coverage) of the cell, is filled with 6 km of new basalt of age zero (the flux NEW), and `gapfill` gives it plates.

## Isostasy (R1502, R1503)

Archimedes at the base of a column [cited R1411 sec. 2.2, eq. 2.2 to 2.5]. A column's buoyancy is the sum over its rocks of thickness times (3,300 less the rock's density): basalt 2,900 (400), sediment 2,500 (800), metasediment, granitoid and volcanic rock 2,800 (500) [R1411 for the first, second and for continental crust; the rest [analogue]; the 2,750 R1411 also uses for the continental mean would raise a granitoid column of 35 km by 0.5 km]. Against the reference column (35 km at 2,800, which stands at z = 0) the surface stands at d / 3,300 above it in air and at d / (3,300 - 1,000) under water [derived: R1411 eq. 2.3 and the water-loaded balance]. So 35 km of crust is at sea level, 50 km at 2.27 km, 70 km at 5.30 km (the height of a plateau like Tibet [derived; Tibet remembered]), 25 km at -2.17 km under water; under water a layer of sediment s raises the surface by s (3,300 - 2,500) / (3,300 - 1,000) = 0.348 s (the basement sinks the other 0.652 s) [derived]. Young ocean floor is hotter and lighter: a thermal buoyancy by age, in the table `thermalTable()`, is added in proportion to the share of an oceanic column the basalt makes (min(1, basalt / 6 km)), chosen so that a bare 6 km of basalt of age t stands at -(2,500 + 355 sqrt t) m, capped at 6,400 m [cited R1411 eq. 4.209, coefficient derived in the note; the cap is the plate model's old-age depth, remembered, UNVERIFIED]. The test solves the same balance in float with an explicit compensation depth (11 columns: shield, 50 and 70 km, a column under water, mixed rocks, floor of age 0, 10, 50 and 200 My, floor under 1 km of sediment, an oceanic plateau) and the kernel is within 6 m of it (integer floors); the twin equals the kernel word for word. Erosion is Unit 3's: it will take material from the volumes, never from a stored height.

**Sea level** is a parameter (`Params.seaLevel`, m above the reference): the heights `derive` returns are Z less the level, land is above 0. It changes no process (the eustatic shift is not isostatically compensated: an error of at most 0.3 of the level [derived: water over mantle density]).

## What the seed decides

Everything random comes from Philox (`src/gpu/rand.ts`) keyed by (seed, stream, id): stream 1 the plates (seed point, Euler pole, speed), 2 the continents' blobs, 4 the hot spots, 5 the arcs' clocks. Nothing else is random.

- **Plates** (R1504, R1506): `plates` (default 10, a parameter) seed points on the sphere; each cell belongs to the plate whose seed is nearest by great-circle distance (the largest dot product): a Voronoi partition. Each plate has an Euler pole (uniform on the sphere) and a speed at 90 degrees from it, log-uniform from 10 to 100 mm/yr [cited R1412: spreading rates 9.7 to 154.4 mm/yr; R1410 caps at 100; the shape of the distribution [design]], times `speedScale`; rate in rad a My = speed / radius in km (1 mm/yr = 1 km/My) [derived]. The poles are fixed for the run [design: no force balance].
- **Speed scale** (R1504, [gap]): the research note's gap 1. The first runs at Earth's speeds (before the smoothing and the overlap rule were as they are now) piled collisions into columns of 450 km and turned the planet to fragments, and the note's own fallback was taken: the rates are scaled by radius over Earth's, 2,000 / 6,371 = 0.314, so 3.1 to 31 mm/yr [design, stated, not tuned to a check of the final model]. `speedScale: 1` gives Earth's; the final model at Earth's speeds (seed 1) ends with land 0.170, 2 continents, the sea floor turned over 9 times (3 at the scaled speeds, land 0.250), 13% of the cells on a boundary, and every invariant holding.
- **Arcs** (R1509, R1510, R1508): over a trench, a band at 10 to 14 cells (125 to 172 km; arc to trench 149 km, 12 cells [derived: the volcanic line lies 125 km over the slab, R1411 sec. 1.4, for a slab dipping 40 degrees [design]]) and 50 km wide [cited R1415]. Each plate has a clock [design draws within cited ranges]: a background of 14 km3 a My a km of arc [cited R1415: the case studied, background under 30 +- 10], with flare-ups of 75 to 150 lasting 5 to 20 My every 30 to 70 My [cited R1415]; half of the magma is new crust [cited R1415: a crustal half and a mantle half]; thickness added to each cell of the band in a step = 0.5 x flux x dt / 50 km (280 m a step in the background) [derived]. 30 parts of pluton (granitoid) to one of volcanic rock [cited R1415, over 95% of the Coast Plutonic Complex is plutonic, R1417 UNVERIFIED].
- **Hot spots** (R1511): 5 [analogue: Earth has some 40, this planet a tenth of the area], fixed in the mantle's frame [design: they drift 10 mm/yr or so on Earth], each of 0.01 to 0.1 km3 a year [UNVERIFIED: Hawaii's order of 0.1 is remembered, not read] over a cone of radius 100 km [UNVERIFIED], volcanic rock added to every column under it (a cone: the peak a step is 3 Q dt / (pi r^2)).
- **Pelagic sediment** (R1513): 10 m a My on sea floor [derived: layer 1 is about 1 km on floor of mean age 100 My, R1411 sec. 1.1]. The floor is sea floor where the basalt is at least the rest of the rock and no event raised the column that step.
- **Thickness limit** (R1512): 70 km. Crust past it flows to the four neighbours in proportion to their room under it (`topple`, a conservative sandpile, exact in integers), and what 32 sweeps cannot carry is cut (the flux TRIM: delamination of the dense root and denudation [gap: not modelled as processes, no viscosity or flow law was read]). [design] limit; the physical bounds it stands for: Tibet 70 km [UNVERIFIED], Fiordland's arc crust 60 to 80 km [cited R1415].

## The planet at time zero (R1514, R1515)

Continents are the highest part of a field of 16 large and 48 small Gaussian blobs of random sign (angular radii 12 to 35 and 3 to 8 degrees, Philox), cut at the height that gives the share of the area asked for, 39% [analogue: continents with their margins are 2.0e14 of 5.10e14 m2 on Earth, R1411 app. B], **inside the plates**: no continent starts within 6 cells (75 km) of a plate boundary, so every continent is carried by one plate and no boundary at the start is an arbitrary tear [design; the first try let continents straddle boundaries and tore them apart at once]. A continental column is 30 km at the coast to 40 km four cells in (the mean 40 km of R1411 app. B), under a platform cover of sediment of 2 km at the coast thinning to none 12 cells in [gap: no passive-margin law is read; R1411 eq. 2.10's fill of a basin by stretching is not used]; the rest is granitoid (the shield). The sea floor is 6 km of basalt aged by its distance from the nearest divergent boundary (by `classifyBoundaries` at the start, breadth-first over the grid, great-circle distance to the nearest ridge cell) at the half rate there [cited R1411 sec. 1.1: symmetric spreading at half rates], at most 200 My [UNVERIFIED: Earth's oldest floor, 180 to 200 My, is remembered], with its pelagic sediment from that age. The orogeny clock starts at 200 My [design].

## What the maps show (`scripts/globe-maps.ts`, seed 1: `/tmp/globe-1/*.png`, `-net.png` the cube net)

`height.png`: three or four continents that were never hit stand as flat shields at 0.5 km with their coasts and a rim of platform sediment (green, the pink of `lithology.png` inside a yellow rim); where plates met, ranges stand white at 5.3 km, the 70 km limit's height, along the margins that rode over ocean floor and across the middle of the map, where several continents met: a belt of fragmented continent, island arcs and plateaus in a sea of young floor, the planet's Indonesia. `age.png`: ridges (red, age zero) with floor aging symmetrically away to old basins (dark blue, left), and grey continents. `plates.png`: blobs of plates with fingers where plates were consumed. `boundaries.png`: convergent (red), divergent (blue) and transform (green) by relative velocity: clear long lines where plates are large, a mess in the middle of the planet where they are not. `start.png` the planet at 0 My. `orogeny.png`: the time since a column last grew, near zero over the whole central belt.

What looks wrong, and why: (1) **too much high ground**: 11 to 18% of the planet stands over 2 km (Earth about 5% [UNVERIFIED: remembered]) and 6 to 7% of the cells stand at the limit's 70 km: with no erosion (Unit 3) every arc and collision piles to the limit and stays there as a flat plateau 100 to 250 km wide. (2) **arcs saturate**: the arc flux (14 to 150 km3 a My a km, Earth's, cited) for 200 My is up to 3,700 km3 for each km of arc (half of the flux, the clocks' mean), against the 1,500 that a 50 km arc can add before the limit of 70 km stops it, and the planet has far more arc for its area than Earth: most of the arcs' rock is cut (seed 1: the cut TRIM is 7.5e12 volume units, the arcs' ARC 5.9e12). (3) **the plate map is jagged in the collision zone**: the boundaries' length grows about 5 times over the run (boundary cells are 1.7% of the planet at the start and 9% at the end, 13% at Earth's speeds); the ownership smoothing cut that by a third and was not enough; a plate that is squeezed between two others becomes a sliver, and a continent hit by another is cut into pieces by the ridges and trenches around its parts. (4) **ocean floor over 200 My** (4 to 18%) is the start's, left where no trench reached it in 200 My (the floor of a plate with no convergent edge); it is not a failure of the ageing (floor made in the run is younger than the run, tested). (5) **speckle** near the 35 km line: land and sea are decided by the crust's thickness against 35 km, and a continent that is blurred thins to that line at its coasts.

## Gaps (open, and why)

1. **Plate speed and size on a 2,000 km planet** (the research note's gap 1): the speeds are Earth's, scaled by radius [design]; plate sizes are the seed's Voronoi cells of 10 [INFERENCE: Bird 2003 not read]. Earth's speeds make the planet a bag of fragments (the first run); the scaled ones still turn the ocean floor over 2 to 3 times in 200 My (sea floor median age 25 to 50 My against Earth's mean of 100 My).
2. **No force balance, no plate reorganisation.** Poles and rates are fixed for the run: plates go on driving into each other after a collision (a continent is chewed up into a plateau instead of the motion stopping); no plate splits (continental rifting at a seeded cut, R1410's rifting event, is not modelled: divergence happens only at the boundaries the Voronoi cells start with and those the motion makes); no new subduction zones start. A first version that sutured a collided terrane to the other plate (R1410, R1416: the cells of a continent next to a collision took the other plate, and the flag spread through the connected continent) fragmented the plates to noise within 30 steps (the continents, which then straddled plates, were pulled apart again around the suture) and was taken out; its numbers were not kept.
3. **No erosion or deposition** (Unit 3): the only sediment is pelagic and the wedges and platform cover that exist; ranges stay at the 70 km limit's height; no sediment is routed to margins; basins fill only with the cover of the start. The shelf is whatever the 4-cell blur of the coast leaves (about 50 km), not a passive-margin law [gap R1411 eq. 2.10's stretching].
4. **The thickness limit is a cut, not a flow law** (the research note's gap 4: delamination is not modelled): the flux TRIM is 6e12 units, comparable to the arcs' own. Tibet's 70 km is remembered, UNVERIFIED.
5. **Arc and hot spot fluxes are not derived from the slab** (the research note's gap 3): Cordilleran numbers on every arc, whatever the convergence rate or the age of the plate going down; the dip (40 degrees) is [design]; the flare-up clock is a draw; the arc stands at a fixed distance behind the trench of its plate (no rollback, no migration of the arc front at about 2 km/My, R1418 UNVERIFIED).
6. **Cloud in cell blurs**: 50 km over a run, and the plate ownership of a boundary cell is decided from sums that jitter, hence the smoothing, a numerical regularisation [design]. The dead band (1/16) and the claim thresholds (1/8 and 1/32 of the mean cell) are [design]; the leak of the move (3.5e-5 of the floor's basalt in 20 steps) comes at cube edges and corners, where the grid's lattice is not orthogonal; coverage there is not 1.
7. **A tectonic step is 2 My**: a plate that crosses more than its neighbour's width in a step can tunnel through a thin plate without touching it. With the scaled speeds the fastest plate moves 31 mm/yr, 62 km (5 cells) a step, and two plates close at twice that; with Earth's 100 mm/yr it is 16 cells, and the research note's finer step of 1 My is the remedy [design, not built].
8. **Heights are Airy** (the note's design): flexure is not resolved; the 4-cell blur and the flow of thick crust stand in for it.
9. **Rock densities** for metasediment, granitoid and volcanic rock are the continental crust's mean [analogue]; the thermal buoyancy for old floor uses the cap (6.4 km), the plate model's fit remembered [UNVERIFIED].
10. **The age of continental crust** is not carried (the orogeny clock stands in); no isotope or tectonic province ages. Hot spots are fixed and few.
11. **The sphere grid's gaps carry through**: tier 0 only; the diffusion's bounded error at face edges is not used (the crust's flow is a two-step, exact conservative scheme, not the grid's diffusion); at the 8 cube corners the move merges a missing diagonal into the cell itself.

## The move at the cube's corners (2026-10-24)

A straight 120 m step across the land at the cube corner 35.26 S 135 W (faces 2, 3 and 5), found in the 75 m tier, was in the planet's own tier-0 heights (the tier chain holds each tier-0 cell's mean, and the file equals the pooled tier 5 to the unit): the move's cloud in cell, which covers a rigid move exactly in a face, covers the cells beside a cube corner unevenly (the faces' lattices are skewed up to 30 degrees to each other there, so the parcels of one face land on another face's lattice as a moire): coverage over the cell's area 0.888 to 1.2385 within 4 cells of a corner, 0.91 to 1.12 within 8, for plate 3's step (1.000 within 0.0002 inside a face). A cell under 15/16 is a gap (new basalt, under a continent too) and one over 17/16 an overlap that keeps its extra (a single plate loses none of it), so the corner made crust each step: the three corner cells of the faces and the edge rows beside them stood 1 to 3 km thick or thin on 35 km, and isostasy turned that into the step. At the S 225 E and S 315 E corners the across-edge anomaly of tier 0 was 14 and 5 times that of the same edges away from the corner (J, the second difference of the height across the edge against the one inside), and 2 times at N 225 E; the other five corners lay in the sea or on a still plate and showed none.

The fix is in the move (`src/gpu/globe/tectonics-corner.ts`, the `stencil` of the kernel and its twin): a parcel within 28 cells of a corner is shared by the overlap of its turned cell with the cells (the four vertices turned by the plate's step, clipped on each face's plane, where cell edges and parcel edges are all straight, solid angle exact, shares to 2^16 exactly so every volume still goes to the unit), wholly from 12 cells in and in proportion between (a hard seam between the two schemes left the coverage 12 % off along it: each scheme is exact alone, the cloud in cell's smear over a seam is not the complement of the overlap's sharp edge). An overlap share is added to the cloud-in-cell share of the same cell, because a cell's plate goes to its largest piece (a piece of at least 1/8 of the cell) and unmerged pieces left cells with no owner. Coverage then is 1.0000 within 4 cells, within 0.004 to 8 and as inside a face beyond (`tectonics-corner.test.ts`). The chain at tier 0 with it (326 s, same sources otherwise): the across-edge anomaly at the corner over the same edges' anomaly away from it falls 14.1 to 1.2 at S 225 E, 5.3 to 0.1 at S 315 E, 2.1 to 0.6 at N 225 E, and stays between 0.1 and 1.4 at the other five; the planet is another realisation near the plates' tracks (land share 0.2545 to 0.2505, 1.9 % of the area changes between land and sea, mean height change 204 m), since a corner's crust is carried away by the plate and the erosion that follows is chaotic. The planet key now hashes `tectonics-corner.ts`, and every edit of the tectonics sources changes the key: the whole bake is remade (tier 0, climate, ice, the tiers, terrain, air, water, ground, the pyramid).

## Napkin note (for the repo's runbook, not edited here)

Do instead: stage a test with `stateFrom` and two plates turning opposite ways about the pole (area closed over a step is 4 omega dt R^2, checked to 3%); never name a WGSL entry point `smooth` (reserved).
