# The shelf premise refuted, the shoreline stage, and plate-boundary pinning (T7, 2026-10-29)

Chain: `2026-10-29-t6-free-coast-design.md` (Update 1). Branch `globe-t7` (from `globe-t6`, scratch `~/.cache/nomads-scratch/t7`). Pictures in `t7/out/`.

## Why there is no shelf (measured on the chain's raw dump, `t7/raw`)

- Every coast sea cell is already continental crust (median 32 km). Passive coasts (same plate, no convergence) are 91% of 3,832 coast land cells, active 7%. Median depth 1, 3, 6, 12 cells out: passive 71, 346, 839, 2,067 m; active 783, 1,194, 1,583, 2,071 m. Coast length 12,153 km, 0.0255 km per km2 of land.
- The chain's sea-floor diffusion is one linear constant (`CREEP_SEA` 100 m2/yr). At the chain's mean supply of about 4.7 m2/yr per metre of coast the bypass slope is Q/D = 0.047, which is the profile measured. Switching it off (run `raw-sea0`) gives a shelf 3 cells wide and a cliff after it, but land goes from 25.3% to 33.2% (overlap with the baseline 0.705), so a law change in the chain moves the land. A tenth of the diffusivity changes little (median 41 m at 1 cell).
- A depth-dependent diffusivity D = C0 exp(-C1 W) (Kaufman, Grotzinger and McCormick 1991, Kansas Geol. Survey Bull. 233: 489 to 508; C0 5e4 m2/yr, C1 0.05 per m, flux 5 to 20 m2/yr) with the chain's 100 m2/yr as the deep floor puts the break at ln(C0 / D_floor) / C1 = 124 m and the shelf width at about (C0 / C1) / Q = 1e6 / Q m. A one-dimensional run of it (python, throwaway) showed that at the chain's supply every grain bypasses a basement sloping 0.07: the supply is under the capacity D x slope everywhere, so no wedge forms without the whole basin filling first. Not built.
- Earth: shelf width mean 31 km active, 88 km passive, 57 km all (Harris et al. 2014, Marine Geology 352: 4 to 24, as tabulated on Wikipedia "Continental shelf"; not re-read).

## The test that refutes it

An artificial graded shelf in the sea cells only (land, ice and everything else byte unchanged; `scratch/proto.ts`; `s10`: 1e-3 slope to 130 m, `s30`: 1e-3 to a 30 m cliff, `s02`: 2e-4 to 130 m), pushed through tiers 1 to 5 at spit/shelf, river, K3, B (`t7/out/<place>-384-<variant>.png`). Coasts are the same as A2. Straight gates pooled over the four places (A2 / s10 / s02): axis share at 32 cells .350 / .372 / .400, reversals per 100 km 5.2 / 3.3 / 6.6.

What the pictures show: at spit and river the whole window is a plain within +-0.5 m of sea level (the old planet's rows are ridged to the coast), so the coast is where a field flat to under a metre crosses a threshold.

## The shoreline stage (route 2, `scratch/shore.ts`, commit b29d966)

A deterministic port of the CSDMS Coastline Evolution Model (Ashton, Murray and Arnoult 2001; Ashton and Murray 2006; flux K H^(12/5) T^(1/5) cos^(6/5)(phi) sin(phi), K = 0.34 m^(3/5) s^(-6/5), Ashton, Murray and Littlewood 2007 eq. 1), waves from `seawind.ts` winds with JONSWAP fetch-limited H and T, depth of closure Hallermeier 1981 (D 3.8 m at rA, 28.7 at rR, 19.8 at rK, 17.3 at rB), low coasts only (land within 3 cells at most 3 m), sand conserved. Runs of 10 to 300 yr on A2's region states.

Gates pooled over rA, rR, rK, rB (A2 / 10 / 30 / 100 / 300 yr): axis share at 32 cells .350 / .169 / .149 / .159 / .163; reversals per 100 km 5.2 / 9.6 / 10.5 / 10.5 / 7.8; coast cells 6,970 / 12,084 / 13,922 / 13,249 / 12,975. Pictures `t7/out/{spit,shelf,river,K3,B}-384-shore-f.png` (old planet, A2, 30 yr, 300 yr). The axis share halves but much of that is one-to-three-cell jitter breaking the straight-run test. At K3 a smooth arc with a dead-straight 1 to 2 cell spit 25 km long appears; at river the coast becomes a straighter diagonal with a sawtooth edge and, at 300 yr, a new perfectly axis-aligned edge where erosion meets the mobility mask; the 300 yr plain retreats 7.5 km at K3. The stage does not reach the spit's scarp (land within 3 cells is above 3 m). Not clearly more natural than A2; rL, rE not run, rH has almost no low coast.

## Why the chain makes the spit's straight coast (agent trace, scratch `trace0.ts`, `pbt.ts`, `vel0.ts`, `lattice0.ts`, `lattice3.ts`)

- It is a plate boundary. Face 4 columns 214 and 215 are a plate 0 / plate 4 boundary from row 164 to 189 and beyond, formed at steps 75 to 90 when plate 0 (thick continental crust) slid in one row at a time and stopped at 214; the relative motion there is dX -0.095, dY -1.10 cells a step, a transform almost parallel to the column. The "scarp" is plate 0's 35 km crust thinning to 22 km over columns 211 to 214 (surface 0, -0.1, -0.8, -1.8, -2.9 km); 35 km is the isostatic reference, so the land beside it is flat at 0 m.
- The move splats each parcel onto 4 cells and the plate id goes to the plate with the most coverage (or the highest rank in an overlap), so sub-cell displacement is dropped each step and a boundary whose normal motion is under about half a cell a step never moves. Tectonics-only plate-boundary share of straight runs of 16 or more cells at step 1 / 2 / 4 My: .173 / .068 / .033 (smaller steps pin more).
- Plate boundaries are lattice straight in the chain (straight-run shares at 6 / 10 / 16 cells .205 / .118 / .072, 13,283 edges). 28% of the new chain's coast edges are cliffs over 300 m (old chain 42%); 54% of those lie within 4 cells of a plate boundary against 21% of cells overall (66% for cliff runs of 10 or more).
- Global coast straightness at tier 0 is not above the controls and matches the old planet (coast runs of 6, 10, 16 or more collinear edges: new .158 / .065 / .014, old .146 / .059 / .016, smooth-blob control .120 / .084 / .054, rough-blob control .177 / .071 / .005; tectonics only .131 / .060 / .032). The lattice cause is local to plate boundaries, not a global anisotropy.
- One-cell ridges: .0023 of land cells in the new chain, .0009 in the old; tectonics only .0011, so erosion roughly doubles them (inference from one sample, no erosion variant run).
- Fix tried (`scratch/stoch-claim.patch`, reverted from src): in a cell not overlapped, the plate is drawn with probability equal to its coverage (an integer hash of cell and step), a stochastic rounding so a boundary advances by the mean sub-cell displacement. Step-size dependence of the plate-boundary share (16 or more) falls to .061 / .036 / .027. Full chain: plate boundary runs 6 / 10 / 16 or more .205 / .118 / .072 to .170 / .090 / .051; cliff runs of 10 or more 139 to 93, of 16 or more 21 to 0; coast runs unchanged (.158 / .065 / .014 to .144 / .070 / .016); land share .253 to .250; land overlap with the previous chain .8755 (the old planet's is .8484); the spit is not in the same place. One seed.

## Where this leaves the nine places

Nothing built so far is clearly more natural than A2 against the old planet at the nine places. A2's coast problems have two parts: plains within a metre of sea level (a threshold on a flat field: the shoreline stage changes the shape but makes jitter and new axis-aligned edges) and plate-boundary scarps on lattice columns (a chain fix, stochastic plate claim, reduces the longest runs and moves the places). Pictures of the stochastic-claim chain through the fine tiers were not made.
