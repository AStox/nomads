# The sphere grid: an equiangular cube-sphere of 256 by 256 tiles, with halo exchange, a gradient and a conservative diffusion step

Chain: `docs/plans/2026-10-19-001-feat-globe-generator-plan.md`, Unit 1. Code: `src/gpu/globe/grid.ts` (host, tables, twins, the kernels' driver), `src/gpu/wgsl/globe-grid.ts` (kernels), tested by `src/gpu/globe/grid.test.ts` (run one case at a time: `bun test ./src/gpu/globe/grid.test.ts -t "<name>" --timeout 280000`). R-numbers used here: R1400 to R1404 (R1405 to R1409 are free).

## What was built

1. **Planet and cell sizes.** Radius 2,000,000 m [design, the operator's decision of 2026-10-19]. Tier 0 is six faces of 256 by 256 cells (393,216 cells). A tier-0 cell is 12,272 m wide at the middle of a face (a quarter circle, 3,141.6 km, over 256), the side of a square of the mean cell's area is 11,306 m, and the mean area is 1.278e8 m2. Each tier splits every tile into four: tier 1 has 2 by 2 tiles a face (512 cells a side), and a cell at tier t is 12,272 / 2^t m wide. Tier 7 is 95.9 m and tier 8 is 47.9 m, so the plan's 75 m window is **not** a whole tier of the globe (12,272 / 75 is 163.6): Unit 8's refinement has to resample between a tier and the window's grid.
2. **Cell centres, areas, east and north.** `centre`, `areaSr` (steradians; times R squared for m2), `eastNorth`, `latLon`, `vertex`, from (face, X, Y) with X and Y counted along the whole face.
3. **Neighbours** across tile edges, face edges and cube corners: `step`, `haloSource`, the `EDGES` table; a halo-exchange kernel that fills a tile's one-cell ring from the tiles and faces round it, turning direction codes and vectors by the axes' rotation.
4. **Lookup** `locate(lat, lon)` to (face, tile, cell, fraction) and back, `pointAt`.
5. **A stencil helper:** a least-squares gradient (second order) and a conservative diffusion step, both integer on the GPU, each with a TypeScript twin that the tests compare bit for bit.

## The mapping, and why

The equiangular gnomonic cube-sphere (Ronchi, Iacono and Paolucci 1996, R1400; the equidistant version is Sadourny's, R1403). Face f is the cube's face seen from the centre through two angles a and b in [-pi/4, pi/4]; a cell is an equal step of each; the point is the unit vector of (n + tan a e_x + tan b e_y). Choice and reasons:

- **Cell edges are great circles.** A line of constant a is the intersection of the sphere with a plane through the centre, so a cell is a spherical quadrilateral with great-circle edges; its area has a closed form (below) and the edge's length and midpoint are exact. Putman and Lin make the same choice of great-circle edges for their grids and compute area from the spherical excess (R1402 eq. 19).
- **The quadtree nests exactly.** Equal steps of an angle mean a tier's cell is exactly four of the next tier's, and a tier's vertices are every other vertex of the next. `grid.test.ts` checks it (children's centres locate to the parent, shared vertices to 1e-14, areas add).
- **Neighbours across an edge are an integer map.** The equiangular spacing along a cube edge is the same on both faces (on the edge tan b is the same from either side), so the cell across is an index permutation and a flip: no interpolation to fill a halo. The conformal map (Rancic, Purser and Mesinger 1996, R1401) has no closed form (a series from a stereographic plane, per Putman and Lin) and its cell size shrinks toward the 8 corners: Putman and Lin's Table 1 gives the conformal grid's shortest grid length as 0.43, 0.33, 0.26 and 0.20 of the mean at c22, c44, c90 and c180 (and 0.16 at c360), against 0.77 here (the shortest cell edge over the side of the mean cell's area, measured at tier 0; the longest is 1.09), which would make the erosion and diffusion resolution vary five to six times near a corner (R1402 secs. 3.2 and 3.5). Its coordinate lines are not great circles, so a closed form for a cell's area and edge midpoint is lost too.
- **What it costs: non-orthogonality.** The lines cross at 60 to 120 degrees (measured 60.2 to 119.8 at the cell centres of tier 0), and the coordinates are discontinuous across the 12 edges (R1402 sec. 3.1 and app. C). That is why a diffusion step is not just the two-point flux (below).

Not chosen: the equidistant version (equal steps of tan; area ratio 5.16, measured), HEALPix and icosahedral grids (no square 256 by 256 tiles for the existing kernels), the conformal grid (above).

## The metric

The area element of the tangent plane (u, v) = (tan a, tan b) is du dv / (1 + u^2 + v^2)^(3/2), whose integral from the centre is F(u, v) = atan(u v / sqrt(1 + u^2 + v^2)) [derived]. A cell's solid angle is the alternating sum of F at its four corners (`areaSr`); six faces of F(1, 1) four times each give 6 * 4 * pi/6 = 4 pi, which the table below confirms. The cell is largest at the middle of a face and smallest at the middle of an edge, where the factor is 1 and 1/sqrt 2; the ratio tends to sqrt 2 from below.

Measured (`areaStats`, float64):

| cells a side | sum / 4 pi - 1 | integer weights' sum / 4 pi - 1 | smallest cell, sr | largest, sr | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32 | 1.4e-16 | 1.4e-6 | 1.7452e-3 | 2.4076e-3 | 1.3795 |
| 64 | 5.4e-15 | 3.3e-7 | 4.3116e-4 | 6.0227e-4 | 1.3969 |
| 128 | 9.0e-15 | 3.0e-8 | 1.0714e-4 | 1.5059e-4 | 1.4055 |
| 256 (tier 0) | 4.5e-15 | 1.4e-7 | 2.6704e-5 | 3.7649e-5 | 1.4099 |
| 512 (tier 1) | 3.7e-14 | 5.6e-9 | 6.6658e-6 | 9.4124e-6 | 1.4120 |

At tier 0 on the 2,000 km planet the cells run from 1.068e8 to 1.506e8 m2: 16.4% under and 17.8% over the mean 1.278e8. The test's tolerances are bounds [derived]: the float sum under 6 fn^2 2^-52 / (4 pi) (1.0e-11 at 256), the integer weights under 2^-16 (each rounds by at most half a unit of 1/2^15 of the mean). The plan's "up to about 30 percent with the plain gnomonic map" is wrong for both maps: the plain (equidistant) gnomonic map has a ratio of **5.16** (measured, 256 cells), the equiangular one 1.41.

Integer area weights: a = round(Omega / Omega_unit) with Omega_unit the mean cell's solid angle over 2^15, so a is 2^15 for an average cell, at most 38,603 (under 2^16, which the flux step's 16-bit long division needs: checked when the tables are built).

## Corners

At a cube corner three faces meet at 120 degrees each (a total of 360 less 90: the corner is a cone point). Three cells meet there and no cell stands in the fourth quadrant. How each piece treats it:

- **Neighbours.** The corner cell of a face has its four neighbours as everywhere else (two on the face, one across each of the two edges that meet at the corner), and they are four distinct cells. The three corner cells of a vertex are neighbours of one another. Symmetry (b is a's neighbour in direction d and a is b's in direction `back`) holds there as elsewhere; tested for all 393,216 cells. What is missing is the **diagonal** cell of those 24 face-corner cells (8 corners times 3), and only that: `haloSource` returns null for the 24 diagonal positions and for no other.
- **Halo.** A tile's halo ring has a corner word at each tile corner. At a tile corner that is not a cube corner it is the diagonal tile's cell; at a cube corner the kernel writes the (rounded down) mean of the three cells that meet at the vertex (copy and vector modes) or 4, "no direction" (code mode).
- **Gradient.** The corner cell's fit has seven neighbours (weight zero on the missing diagonal, so the synthetic halo word is not read).
- **Diffusion.** The value at a vertex is a weighted mean of the cells round it; at a cube corner the weights are one third each of the three (`vertexWeights`), exactly, with zero on the fourth, so again the synthetic word has no weight. Both cells of every link round the same integer from the same words, so the total is exact there too (tested: a bump centred on a cube corner conserves its total exactly and shares it among the three faces to the unit).

## Neighbours, edges and the rotation of axes

A cell is (face, X, Y); its neighbours are numbered 0 east (+X), 1 north (+Y), 2 west, 3 south in the face's own axes, and the corner of a cell between directions c and c + 1 is corner c (NE, NW, SW, SE). The faces: 0 to 3 round the equator (centred on longitude 0, 90, 180, 270 degrees east; e_x east, e_y north), 4 the north pole's, 5 the south's; all are right-handed seen from outside. `EDGES[face][side]` is found from the frames, not typed in: the face across is the one whose outward axis is the side's direction; the side of it that faces back; and whether the index along the edge runs the other way (`flip`, from the dot product of the two faces' along axes). The 24 entries:

| face | east | north | west | south |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 west | 4 east | 3 east | 5 east |
| 1 | 2 west | 4 north | 0 east | 5 south |
| 2 | 3 west | 4 west | 1 east | 5 west |
| 3 | 0 west | 4 south | 2 east | 5 north |
| 4 | 0 north | 1 north | 2 north | 3 north |
| 5 | 0 south | 3 south | 2 south | 1 south |

(Each entry is "face across, its side that faces back"; the test checks the table is its own inverse.) Crossing the east side of face f into face g, the cell is the edge cell on g's side that faces f, with the along-edge index kept or reversed. The axes turn by `turn = (back + 2 - d) mod 4` quarter turns counterclockwise: a step d of the first cell, in the second's axes, is `(d + turn) mod 4`. The tests check, for all 6,144 edge crossings, that the shared edge's two ends coincide to 1e-12 from either cell, that the edge's own direction is the same 3D vector in both faces' axes after the turn, and that a step across goes against the outward axis of the face left.

Tile edges inside a face are trivial (the next tile's cell, turn 0). A tile slot is `(face * T + ty) * T + tx` for T tiles on a face's side.

## The halo layout

A field is one i32 word a cell, held a tile at a time with a one-cell halo: a tile is 258 by 258 words (66,564), the interior at x and y of 1 to 256 and the halo at 0 and 257, so a kernel reads its four neighbours at plus or minus 1 and plus or minus 258 with no branching. A state buffer holds several fields one after another (field f of tile slot s at `f * FW + s * 66564 + y * 258 + x`, FW = tiles * 66564). The halo kernel runs one thread for each of the 1,028 ring words of every tile (the east and west columns without their corners, the north and south rows with them) and gets each word from a generic integer function of (face, X, Y), so the same kernel serves any tier and tile edges and face edges alike. Three modes:

- **copy**: the cell across;
- **code**: a direction code (0 to 3 in the face's own axes) turned by the rotation, `(d' + 4 - turn) mod 4`; values over 3 pass through; at a cube corner 4. This is what flow routing (Unit 3) needs: a stored "drains east" must read as "drains north" on the other side of an edge that turns the axes;
- **pair**: two fields as a vector along the face's own axes, rotated by the inverse quarter turns. Exact only for index-axis vectors (steps and displacements in cells). A physical vector should be stored as (east, north), which needs no rotation (copy mode): the equiangular axes are not orthogonal and the Jacobian across an edge is a rotation plus a shear (d b' / d a = -sin 2b at the edge), so a rotation by quarter turns is not the transformation of a vector in the face's own axes.

The kernel is checked against its twin for all three modes at tier 0 (6 tiles, face edges and corners) and tier 1 (24 tiles, tile edges inside a face too); the twin is checked against the neighbour definition (cell ids copied, codes and vectors consistent with each other, 24 corner words).

## Lookup

`locateVec` takes the face whose outward axis has the largest dot product with the point (the lowest face number on a tie, so a point exactly on an edge has one answer), then a = atan(p . e_x / p . n) and the cell and fraction from (a + pi/4) / (pi/2) times the cells a side. `pointAt` goes back. The fraction is in [0, 1] (1 only on a face's edge). Measured at tier 0: every one of the 393,216 cell centres finds its own cell exactly, at a fraction within 1e-9 of 0.5; 200,000 random points come back to 1e-14 in the unit vector, in latitude and in longitude times cos latitude; the 8 corners and points on the 12 edges (and 1e-12 either side) land in a cell and come back to 1e-14.

## The stencils

All of it integer on the GPU. Q formats [design], sized by the ranges [derived]; the host builds the tables in float and rounds them.

- **Tables** per tile cell (41 words a cell, 64.5 MB for tier 0): the area weight a (Q15 of the mean); for each of the four links a coefficient kappa (Q24) and a cross coefficient (Q24); for each of the four corners the four weights of the cells round that vertex (Q20, summing to exactly 2^20: the largest is adjusted); the gradient's sixteen weights (Q28). A link is computed once, from the cell with the lower id, and both cells take the same words.
- **Rounding margin** (like `rain.ts tableMargin`): the least distance of any scaled table value from a rounding tie is 6.0e-8 of a last place, and 8 of 8,257,536 rounded words lie within 100 units in the last place of a double of one (so a JavaScript engine whose `Math.tan` or `Math.atan` differs in the last places could round those eight the other way). Byte identity is claimed for one engine and checked there (Bun, JavaScriptCore); the tables are not rebuilt on a second engine here. Building tier 0 takes 4.0 s on the M3 Pro.
- **Gradient.** Weights are the first two rows of the least-squares fit, to the (up to) eight neighbours, of `q_k - q_c = g . d + (d^T H d) / 2` (g two unknowns, H three), d the neighbour's offset in the tangent plane at the cell by the log map, in cell widths. It is exact for any quadratic, so second order **across edges and at corners too** (the lines bend there and a four-neighbour fit is only first order: measured 1.5 and 1.0 for it before this was changed). The answer is (east, north) in Q6 of the field's unit a cell's width: divide by 64 and by the cell width in metres (R times pi/2 over cells a side, at the face's centre; use the exact `eastNorth` metric for more) for a gradient a metre.
- **Diffusion step.** Conservative flux form with an exact integer total. The state is q and a remainder r with 0 <= r < a, the mass in a cell is q a + r, and the sum of it over the sphere is the conserved total (a BigInt sum in the test). A step gives each cell, from each neighbour, an integer flux that the neighbour takes with the opposite sign; the cell's new mass is divided by a (16-bit limbs) back into q and r. A field of one value has no flux at all, so a uniform field is unchanged **exactly**, remainders included. The flux of a link is `fo (kappa (q_j - q_i) + c (V_left - V_right))` where V is the weighted mean of the four cells round an end of the shared edge, left and right seen from cell i looking at cell j, fo = D dt / (the mean cell's area) (`foOf`), kappa = L / (|d| cos alpha) and c = -(t . e) / cos alpha with L the edge's length, |d| the distance between the centres in the tangent plane at the edge's midpoint (centres put there by the log map), alpha the angle between the edge's normal and the line between the centres, t that line's unit vector and e the edge's unit vector. Derivation: write the edge normal on t and e, `n . grad q = (t . grad q - (t . e) e . grad q) / cos alpha`, and replace each term by the difference it is: the first by (q_j - q_i) / |d|, the second by (V_left - V_right) / L (the Mathur and Murthy / Jasak "non-orthogonal correction" in this form, here worked out for the cell's own metric and not read from a source). Seen from the other cell the link has the same kappa and c and the two ends swapped, so the flux is exactly opposite. Two roundings, half away from zero, both antisymmetric. fo is at most 0.25 (the products' range) and below `maxFo` (0.0983, `a / (2^16 sum kappa)` in the worst cell: the two-point part's stability; the cross term is not covered by that bound and the tests run at 0.09). |q| under 2^20.

## Measured behaviour

**Gradient, float weights, area-weighted rms and largest error, against the exact gradient, on grids of 16, 32, 64 and 128 cells a side:**

| field | rms, orders between grids | largest, orders |
|---|---|---|
| axis . r, a tilted axis (0.30, -0.50, 0.80), the stream function of a solid-body rotation | 1.9e-3, 4.8e-4, 1.2e-4, 3.0e-5: 1.99, 1.99, 2.00 | 2.7e-3 to 4.8e-5: 1.90, 1.94, 1.97 |
| the degree-3 Legendre in z | 2.3e-2 to 3.7e-4: 1.99, 2.00, 2.00 | 4.2e-2 to 6.7e-4: 1.97, 1.99, 2.00 |
| cos of latitude (a cone at the poles, the centres of faces 4 and 5) | 3.9e-2 to 4.9e-3: 1.00, 1.00, 1.00 | 0.46 at every size: the pole's own cells |

The solid-body rotation u = axis x r comes back from the gradient of its stream function (east, north) = (grad north, -grad east) to second order, largest error 1e-4 or under at 128 cells a side. cos latitude is first order in the rms because its gradient jumps in direction at the pole, which no stencil resolves; away from the poles the error is that of the smooth fields. The kernel's integer answer equals its twin bit for bit over all 393,216 cells and is within one last place of the Q6 answer of the float weights applied to the same integers.

**The diffusion operator, why the cross term is there.** Float, a field of degree 1 (axis . r, Laplacian -2 r.axis), the divergence of the flux over the cell's area against the exact Laplacian:

| | rms, 16 to 128 cells a side | largest |
|---|---|---|
| two-point flux only | 1.00, 1.44, 2.05, 2.90 (grows) | 6.4, 13.5, 27.8, 56.3 (grows as 1/h) |
| with the cross term and the vertex weights | 3.5e-3, 2.2e-3, 1.3e-3, 6.7e-4 (orders 0.67, 0.81, 0.90) | 2.7e-2, 4.9e-2, 6.3e-2, 7.0e-2 (a few percent of 2) |
| same, degree 3 (Laplacian -12) | 0.134, 0.094, 0.066, 0.047 (order 0.5) | 0.90, 1.03, 1.09, 1.12 (9% of 12) |

The two-point flux is inconsistent at every cell beside a face's edge (the error is a flux's size over a cell's area, so it grows as the grid is refined): a field diffused by it would draw the cube's edges into the landscape. The cross term with position-aware vertex values removes it to a bounded error confined to the cells on a face's edge (the strip's cells have a kinked stencil; the plain mean of the four cells round a vertex is wrong by about a quarter of a cell along the edge there, which is why the vertex weights are least-squares weights that put the weighted centre on the vertex). That residual is **first order in the rms and bounded, not convergent, in the largest error**: see the gap list.

**Diffusion on the GPU, tier 0 (393,216 cells; the kernel equals its twin bit for bit over two steps; 200 steps take 60 to 80 ms on the M3 Pro, with the halo exchange):**

- a bump 3 cells wide put on a cube corner, 200 steps at fo 0.09: the total is exact, the three faces' shares are equal to the unit (191,402,869,726 each), the peak falls from 511,475 to 67,193, the least value is -1 (the cross term is not monotone: an undershoot of one quantum);
- the same on the middle of a face edge: the two faces' shares are equal to the unit (286,113,657,940), peak 502,849 to 67,007, least -1;
- a uniform field (with arbitrary remainders) unchanged exactly after 25 steps over the edges and corners;
- **an eigenmode:** Re (x + i y)^3 is a spherical harmonic of degree 3 and diffusion should take it down as exp(-12 D t / R^2). After 3,000 steps at fo 0.09 (decay 0.1035) the measured and exact log amplitudes are -0.1035428 and -0.1035437: **8.4e-6 off**, 1000 steps the same (8.4e-6). The discrete operator is conservative and its error in a smooth mode's projection is second order, even though the pointwise error in the edge strip is not;
- two runs give the same bytes, and a hash of the result of 60 steps from a corner bump is pinned.

## Gaps

1. **The diffusion's divergence is not convergent in the largest error** (table above: a few percent of the field's Laplacian for degree 1, 9% for degree 3, bounded, in the one-cell strip along face edges and the cells round the 8 corners). The cause is first-order accuracy of any one-sided reconstruction at the kink of the coordinate lines (a flux error of order h squared there, which over a cell's area is order one); a fix needs a halo of two cells and a reconstruction in the face's own coordinates (the literature's way), which the 256 by 256 per-tile kernels do not have. The global behaviour is accurate (the eigenmode within 1e-5).
2. **Tables cover a whole tier.** `buildTables` does a whole tier (6 tiles at tier 0, 64.5 MB; tier 1 would be 24 tiles, 258 MB, over a default storage-buffer binding), and the halo kernel assumes every tile of the tier is resident in slot order. A window or a partial tier (Unit 8) needs a tile-to-slot map in the halo kernel and tables built for a tile set.
3. **Table rounding across engines:** 8 words within 100 ulp of a tie (above); not rebuilt on a second engine here. No second adapter on this Mac (the GPU result is checked against the twin, not across adapters).
4. **Pair mode is exact only for index-axis vectors;** physical vectors are to be stored as east and north (above).
5. **The Q formats limit a tracer to |q| under 2^20** (a million levels of the field's unit); a field with more range needs scaling by its user, or a wider flux word.
6. **R1400 and R1401 were not read beyond abstract and secondary account**; the grid's closed forms (area, vertex and edge geometry) were derived and checked numerically instead (sum to 4 pi at 4e-14, shared vertices to 1e-12).
7. **Stability of the cross term** is not bounded analytically; tested at fo 0.09 (no growth over 3,000 steps); the host refuses fo over the two-point bound.
