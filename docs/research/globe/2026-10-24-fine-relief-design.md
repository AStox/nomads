# Fine relief for the 75 m tiers: the candidates, the laws, the decision (2026-10-24)

Defect (terrain-shape-causes 6.2 and 6.3, band-limited-hold): a washboard of parallel ridges two tier-0 cells apart on mountain faces, straight coasts, straight channels. The exact per-tier-0-cell mean hold stays (tier-0 means exact). The erosion law alone leaves planar threshold facets 3.5 to 5 km apart (ridge peak ratio 4.0 to 4.5 against 1.6 to 2.5 for a scale-free surface; surface spectrum k^-5.7 over 0.6 to 19 km and k^-10 between 150 m and 2 km; natural k^-2 to k^-3). The hold needs the new process to be non-uniform in space and to add relief between 100 m and 4 km.

## Scales on this planet

Cell 75 m (tier 5), tier-0 cell 2.4 km, mean loss held by the lift 25 m/My ([2026-10-22-planet-island-law.md] implied uplift), first-order incision K sqrt(A) S = 7.1e-8 x 75 m x 0.6 = 3 m/My [derived], hillslope diffusivity D = 3.98e-3 m2/yr (CREEP = middle of 4.4e-4 to 3.6e-2, R527), K granite 7.1e-8, sandstone and limestone 3.2e-7, volcanic 7.1e-6 per yr at m = 1/2 (erosion.ts, R523), threshold 30 degrees (sediment) and 36 (crystalline) (THRESHOLD_DEG).

## Candidates, sources and what they can change

1. **Valley spacing from diffusion against incision, with spatially varying rock** (Perron, Kirchner, Dietrich 2009, Nature 460, 502-505, read in full). Pe = K L^(2m+1) / D, Lc = (D/K)^(1/(2m+1)), "valley spacing 6.4 Lc <= l <= 12.7 Lc", independent of erosion rate for linear creep and incision; a threshold "widens valley spacing, though not as much as" a like change in D or K. At m = 1/2: granite Lc = sqrt(3.98e-3 / 7.1e-8) = 237 m, spacing 1.5 to 3.0 km; sandstone 112 m, 0.7 to 1.4 km; volcanic 24 m, 0.15 to 0.30 km [derived]. So the repo's own constants give one regular valley spacing in granite of the washboard's order, and spacing goes as K^-1/2: a uniform rock gives evenly spaced valleys (Perron's result), and only variation of K, threshold and D in space can break the regularity. The measured insensitivity of the washboard to K x4, x0.25 and D x10, x0.1 shows its spacing is not set by Pe alone (facets sit at the threshold slope, where creep flux is not linear), so lithology must act through the threshold and through where channels start, not through Pe. K ranges over orders of magnitude between rocks (Stock and Montgomery 1999, JGR 104, 4983, via R523 and the repo's classes: factor 100 from granite to volcanic here; paper not opened, tag [cited second-hand]). Layered rock with K contrasts of about 10 is the standard test (Forte, Yanites, Whipple 2016, ESPL 41 [snippet]); with n = 1 and subhorizontal strata no steady slope contrast exists, the landscape makes cliffs, undercuts and consuming knickpoints, and a stepped profile survives only for a K ratio below about 3 (Perne et al. 2017, ESurf 5, 85-100, read).
2. **Erosion threshold or stochastic discharge** (Lague 2014, ESPL 39, 38-61, read passages: a threshold makes the effective slope exponent n_s = 1.33 to 5.33 for k = 0.1 to 3, "typical k = 0.5 and omega_s = 0.25 ... n_s = 2.2"; DiBiase and Whipple 2011, JGR 116 F04036, snippet). A threshold removes incision where K sqrt(A) S is small, i.e. exactly in first-order cells, which here already sit at the slide threshold (3 m/My against 25). It would keep more facet planar and widen valley spacing (Perron 2009), so it cannot add relief between 100 m and 4 km. Judged not to move the measured mechanism; not built.
3. **Coasts as the sea-level crossing of the fine field** (headlands and bays from differential erosion of rock of unequal strength; Sunamura 1992, Limber and Murray 2011: not read, no numbers used). In the fine tiers the land mask is fixed inside a tier (land cells stay 1/256 m above the sea at the floor, sea never rises) and is only re-cut when heights are carried to the next tier, so a rougher coast has to come from relief at the shore that the carry turns into a rougher zero contour. Beds and fracture zones give that relief at the shore; lowering the floor below the sea would need a lowered base level, a physical process of its own (a sea-level lowstand drowned after, rias) that this note does not build.
4. **Multiple flow direction or D-infinity routing** (Freeman 1991, Quinn et al. 1991, Tarboton 1997; not read). The receivers are already drawn among the lower of eight neighbours, so a channel's direction is not tied to one of the eight; the straight channels follow parallel valleys, which routing does not change. Not built.

## Decision

Order by what can change the measured mechanism: lithology (1) first, since it makes the law non-uniform without touching the hold. Variant V1: bedded rock (strata tilted and folded, erodibility and threshold slope vary with position and height). Variant V2: V1 plus fracture zones, the island's own way to make oblique lines (joint valleys and dykes, R528), non-uniform in the lattice's frame. Candidates 2 to 4 are not built (reasons above).

## The operator (src/gpu/wgsl/globe-lithology.ts, src/gpu/globe/lithology.ts)

Beds [design except where cited]. T(p) is the structural height of the bedding: a sum of 6 plane waves of the planet's 3D position (so no seam at face edges and no direction tied to the cube lattice), wavelengths log-uniform 12 to 400 km, each with a steepest dip of 0.15 (8.5 degrees; the island's regional dip is 0.05, R528, DIP), directions and phases from a counter-based integer hash. The table holds T at each tier-0 cell (word 36) and a cell reads it by the table's interpolation. At a cell of height h the bedding coordinate is u = h - T. Octave o (o = 0 to 6) has period P_o = 32 m x 2^o (32 m to 2 km, below which the 75 m cells at slope 0.3 to 0.6 span less than a bed and above which a 1.2 km tier-1 cell sees one bed); each period carries a sign from an integer hash of (octave, period), so runs of one sign are beds of geometric thickness [derived: mean 2 P_o], the same family as the exponential bed-thickness law of erosion-bounded beds (Straub et al. 2012, JGR 117 F02003, read) and nested in octaves (power-law turbidite thicknesses, Carlson, Grotzinger, Rothman 1996 abstract, read). A cell spans the heights its slope gives it, w = (|h_E - h_W| + |h_N - h_S|) / 2, so each octave's sign is averaged over [u - w/2, u + w/2]; with independent octaves the window average of the erodibility multiplier is a product over octaves of cosh(s) - sbar sinh(s), and dividing by cosh(s) gives each factor a mean of one: factor_K = product (1 - sbar tanh(sigma_o)), factor_T = product (1 + sbar tanh(sigma_T,o)) [derived]. So the thickness-weighted mean erodibility of the bedded column is the tier-0 table's (the pools' thickness-weighted K), a coarse tier sees only thick beds and has the mean of a fine one, and every tier reads one definition. sigma_o = sigma_K / sqrt(7), sigma_K = ln(100) / 4 (+-2 sigma spans the repo's volcanic to granite K ratio of 100), sigma_T = ln(tan 36 / tan 30) / 4 (the repo's two threshold classes) [derived]. The kernel recomputes the factors in `cellmeans`, which runs at the start of the flow (K) and at the start of the slides (threshold), from the heights then.

Fracture zones (V2) [design on R528]. Two families, each parallel planes of the 3D position at the island's joint gap (1,500 m), line positions jittered +-0.5 gap (a uniform place inside each gap), 2 lines in 5 present, each cut into segments of 4 km (planes across, one segment in 5 of 8 kept), zone width 150 m x (0.5 to 2); the cell's share of zone (width of overlap over the cell's width) sets K x (1 + share x 9) and the threshold tangent x (1 + share (tan 30 / tan 36 - 1)). The table carries each family's two phases at the tier-0 centre and their gradients (words 37 to 48); the cell extrapolates linearly (the error over half a tier-0 cell is 1e-3 of a period on a sphere of 391 km [derived]).

All integer, counter-based; no constant was tuned on a result. Amplitudes are the repo's own class contrasts; the widths, spacings, dips and octave range are [design] and listed above.

## What removes the band between 150 m and 2 km (measured)

1D profile slope of P(k) over periods 150 m to 2 km (rows and columns of the best all-land 512 window; 2D radial slopes are in the table, the same ordering): the start surface (tier 3 carried down twice, a B-spline carry) -5.5; the eroded baseline -9.16. Erosion itself deletes power the carry had. Ablations on R1 from the baseline tier 3, baseline law (lithology off), one process changed: hillslope diffusivity x0.1 (D = 4.0e-4 m2/yr, which is the lower end of the cited range of 4.4e-4 to 3.6e-2, R527) gives -3.96 (2 to 19 km -3.21); D x10 -5.79 (2 to 19 km -5.77); slides effectively off (threshold tangent x3) -9.10 (unchanged); control with the new code and lithology off -9.16 (reproduces the baseline). So creep removes the band: sqrt(D T) over the 266 My schedule is 1.0 km, and creep smooths every feature under about 2 km faster than 3 m/My first-order incision can sharpen it (the K x4 and x0.25 runs of terrain-shape-causes moved nothing for the same reason). Slides, the hold, the floor and the carry are not the cause. Target: natural profiles are k^-2 to k^-3 in 1D (Huang and Turcotte 1989: not read; Perron, Kirchner, Dietrich 2008 show a rolloff at hillslope scale: not read). Beds and zones leave the band at -8.7 because they act through K and the threshold, not through D. The cited range of D spans 80 times; the planet's choice of its geometric middle is what smooths it. The operator's question is whether D should be the range's lower end for the fine tiers; not tested beyond the spectrum (relief, slope and valley statistics of that run are in /tmp/globe-fix-t/TRelief/land/A15).

## Gaps and not done

- Fracture traces are straight parallel planes cut into segments: a lattice-like artifact. No curved or en-echelon geometry with cited length and spacing distributions was built (no source read); decision: V2's zones are not adopted as they stand. V1 (beds alone) fails the slope gate (0.233); the zones are what bring the slope back and cut the straight rivers. Next step: draw fracture traces with a cited length-spacing law (Narr and Suppe 1991 spacing proportional to bed thickness; not read) and curve them by the fold field.
- Stream density, parallel stream spacing, the diagonal-valley statistic and the 47.86 N 37.77 E place (face 4, X 7334, Y 6735, tile 28 by 26, 2,341 m in the live bake) were requested late and not measured.
- Candidates 2 to 4 not built (reasons above); no ablation of the 45 degree diagonal valleys.
- Streamed chain not done and blocked: the threshold factor is taken at the slide stage from the cell's height and its four neighbours' after the incision, and a streamed slide visit starts from halos that earlier visits have already slid, so a patch and a tile would differ at block edges. Fix needed before adoption: compute the factors once per step (at the incision visit) and keep the threshold word per cell for the slide visit, or take the threshold's window from the pre-step heights. tiers-stream.ts, regional-patch.ts, tiers-stream.test.ts, tiers.test.ts and regional.test.ts were not run (regional.test.ts step hash and every stage hash change with the table width, 49 words). Any edit of tier or kernel sources changes the planet store key.
