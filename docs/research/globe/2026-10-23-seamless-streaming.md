# Seamless close zoom: how shipped products stream, and what to build here

Operator, 2026-10-23: "it technically is working, but it takes a long time to load in the close up views when you zoom in. I want it to appear seamless." Research only; nothing here was run (the GPU and Chrome belong to another agent). Chain: `docs/plans/2026-10-20-001-feat-seamless-globe-plan.md` Units 3 and 6, `.claude/napkin.md` Shell item 7.

Marks: **[S]** the source says it (link given). **[M]** measured in this repo, from the plan. **[R]** read in the code. **[I]** my inference, not measured. Where a source could not be opened it is listed under "Not verified" at the end.

## 1. Where the wait is (read and measured)

- The planet layer already refines in place: `pyramid.js` keeps tier 0 whole, the finer tier replaces the coarser as it arrives, 6 fetches in flight, LRU of 192 layers. Its only gaps: no prediction, no cancel, inflate and `packFields` on the render worker's thread, no persistent cache. [R]
- At close zoom the ladder draws nothing away from the island until a window exists. The page asks for one only when the view is narrower than a window (`live.js wantGround`, half-extent under `SIZE/2 - 1800` m = 7.8 km, and zoom at least `ZLAD + 0.5` = -1.0), then waits for the worker. [R]
- `groundwin.js build()` is one all-or-nothing job of 1.3 to 4.2 s [M]: ground tiles 0.04 to 0.24 s, fine ground (GPU, then a readback of about 33 MB and float conversion) 0.2 s, painter packing (CPU loops over 1,021 by 1,021 layers, then `packGround` and an upload) 0.5 s, scatter 0.5 to 3 s [M]. Then `applyGround` swaps ground, objects, canopy, far lists and the survey in one go. [R]
- The scatter is the big part and its shape is the problem. For each of up to four tiles `scatterTile` re-runs the raster and fine kernels (a second time; `windowFine` already ran them), runs `table`, `count`, `rank`, `rows`, `scan` and `write` on the GPU, reads five arrays back, and the CPU then loops over every object twice (`collector.add`: count pass, fill pass, a JS `place` closure per object), thins by seed hash to at most 8.4 million slots, and counting-sorts them by 75 m cell (`lay`). 6 to 8 million objects land in a 19.2 km window, which is 368 km². [R]
- A close view shows a small part of that. The ladder's close levels use 6.25 to 11.7 m tiles (`ladder.js`), so a close screen is on the order of a kilometre across. Most of the 6 to 8 million objects are built before anything can see them. [I]
- Every kernel the window uses is made with `pipeline()`, the synchronous path (`tile.ts windowFine`, `scatter.ts scatterTile`, `scenery.ts objectsOn`). The repo's own bench says a large kernel takes 100 to 300 ms to compile and that 16 first-run kernels once stopped the frames 2,961 ms (`src/gpu/kernel.ts` header comment citing bench-r9c.json compileStalls). `pipelineSoon` (async) already exists and the sim's store uses it. The window's kernels are not warmed. [R]
- JS parts of the build (packing, cutting, laying) run on the render worker. Its awaits yield during GPU work, but each JS loop is a frame-sized stall. [I]

## 2. Techniques, with sources

### 2.1 Always draw the best data resident, per region

**Parent fallback while the child loads.** Asobo's Flight Simulator terrain requests tiles recursively, raises the requested level of detail by estimated tile screen size and estimated network bandwidth, and draws "using the best currently available data"; "tiles can use data from a parent". Because a tile may arrive at any time it must also cut arbitrary holes (index buffers for 2 by 2 cuts, a mask texture and `discard` in the general case, "works but expensive"). [S] [Asobo, Fuentes, "Designing the Terrain System of Flight Simulator", slides "Requesting and drawing tiles" and "Cutting tiles"](https://www.asobostudio.com/files/inline-images/Designing_Terrain_System_Fuentes_Lionel.pdf)

**Substitute tiles.** MapLibre retains, for each wanted tile without data, its loaded children, else a loaded ancestor ("retain their loaded children so they can be displayed as substitutes pending load of each ideal tile (to reduce flickering). If no loaded children are available, fallback to seeking loaded parents"), up to 10 levels up and 3 down; raster tiles cross-fade. [S] [maplibre-gl-js `tile_manager.ts`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/main/src/tile/tile_manager.ts) deck.gl's `refinementStrategy: 'best-available'` is the same idea, and it schedules tiles nearest the viewport centre first and aborts requests for tiles that left the view. [S] [deck.gl TileLayer](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/geo-layers/tile-layer)

**Virtual texturing fallback and fade.** Mittring (Crytek) keeps a tile cache with an indirection texture, requests missing tiles asynchronously "while using the content of a lower mip-map as fall-back", and uses a spare indirection channel as a blend value so tile replacement is hidden "by slowly blending tiles in or out". [S] [Mittring, SIGGRAPH 2008](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2008/SIGGRAPH%202008%20-%20Advanced%20virtual%20texture%20topics.pdf) Unreal's streaming virtual texturing says plainly that it is "reactive by nature": the CPU learns a tile is needed after a frame already needed it, so popping can occur. [S] [Unreal SVT docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/streaming-virtual-texturing-in-unreal-engine) Reactive requests alone are not enough; prediction is needed on top (2.3).

### 2.2 Refine locally, not as one big unit

**Geometry clipmaps.** A square window of samples per level is cached around the viewer and moves with the viewer, rewriting only the L-shaped strip exposed; finer levels are predicted by upsampling the coarser one and only "residual detail" is stored or synthesized; geometry and normals blend toward the next coarser level near each window's edge. "The relative motion of the viewer within the windows decreases exponentially at coarser levels, so coarse levels seldom require updating." Losasso and Hoppe numbers: n = 255, 11 levels, 20 billion samples in 355 MB, about 90 fps. [S] [GPU Gems 2 ch. 2](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-i-geometric-complexity/chapter-2-terrain-rendering-using-gpu-based-geometry)

**CDLOD and geomorphing.** One distance function picks the quadtree nodes to draw and the data blocks to stream ("the same LOD selection algorithm used for rendering is also used to select nodes in range, which are then used to mark data blocks as potentially visible and to stream them in or out"); vertices morph from fine to coarse positions over the last 15 to 30% of each range so level swaps do not pop. [S] [Strugar, CDLOD paper](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fstrugar/CDLOD/master/cdlod_paper_latest.pdf)

**Cesium 3D Tiles.** Screen-space error picks the level (default maximum 16 px). `skipLevelOfDetail` drops the rule that all children must be loaded before a parent refines and lets children draw beside parents; REPLACE vs ADD refinement decides whether the parent leaves. Request controls: `foveatedScreenSpaceError` (centre of screen first), `cullRequestsWhileMoving` (do not fetch what motion will make stale), `preloadFlightDestinations` (fetch at the destination while flying), `loadSiblings` (ready for a turn), `progressiveResolutionHeightFraction` (a quick low-resolution layer first), `cacheBytes` 512 MB with a soft overflow. [S] [CesiumJS Cesium3DTileset](https://cesium.com/learn/cesiumjs/ref-doc/Cesium3DTileset.html), [3D Tiles spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CesiumGS/3d-tiles/main/specification/README.adoc)

**Procedural refinement of coarse data (Outerra).** The engine takes coarse elevation (about 150 m) and makes finer tiles "by fractal algorithm entirely on the GPU", textures too, "fully asynchronous". Fine detail is a deterministic function of the coarse tile, so there is nothing to fetch. [S] [Outerra intro](https://outerra.blogspot.com/2008/07/intro.html) This is what our fine-ground kernel already is.

### 2.3 Predict where the camera is going

Nothing found prescribes a velocity predictor. The pieces that exist: Cesium's `preloadFlightDestinations` and `loadSiblings`; Unreal World Partition activating a streaming source at a teleport destination so cells load before arrival, and PCG runtime generation scheduling by distance and view direction with "Generate Bounds Modifier" so content exists before it enters the frustum ("to prevent components from suddenly appearing"). [S] [World Partition](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/world-partition-in-unreal-engine), [PCG generation modes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/using-pcg-generation-modes-in-unreal-engine) A zoom-direction and pan-velocity predictor is ours to build from those parts.

### 2.4 Vegetation: generate on the GPU, near the camera, under a budget

**Horizon Zero Dawn.** Placement is density maps plus dither thresholds evaluated on the GPU per layer, "deterministic" and "locally stable", in a world around the player built "on the fly" from 2D maps streamed in sections (about 4 MB per km², placement maps 1 to 2 m). 500+ asset types, 100,000+ objects in the scene, about 250 µs average GPU load, about 10 µs per generate dispatch and 7 µs per placement dispatch, the pipeline instantiated 64 times to hide flushes. [S] [Guerrilla talk page](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/gpu-based-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn), [slides](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf) (the numbers were read from a local copy of the 238 MB PDF).

**Ghost of Tsushima.** Blades are generated in compute every frame with no stored list: a lane id maps to a position on the terrain tile grid and is jittered, then distance and frustum cull, a per-tile texture lookup (grass type and height), drop lanes without grass, occlusion cull, write survivors to an instance buffer with indirect draw args. A far-LOD terrain texture stands in for distant grass. Streaming: 200 m terrain tiles, 513 by 513 maps, about 10 MB for all far LODs of the world; a virtual texture cache that "always update 1/36th of 1 slice per frame". [S] [GDC 2021 procedural grass (archive copy of a draft deck)](https://archive.thedatadungeon.com/ghost_of_tsushima_2020/documents/gdc_2021/gdc_2021_procedural_grass_in_got.pdf), [GDC 2021 streaming](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC+2021/ghost_streaming_gdc2021.pdf)

**Flight Simulator trees.** 23 million generated trees, 6.3 million in the frustum; positions by Halton sampling, "always uniform, whatever the chosen density. Density can be controlled at draw time"; height from the DEM and colour from the aerial so "trees fade out naturally to the aerial"; 3D impostors, "1 draw call per tree specie per tile", one vertex per tree. [S] same Asobo PDF as 2.1. The nested property (a lower density is a subset of a higher one) is exactly what our `unitHash(seed) < share` thinning has.

**Unreal PCG, runtime.** Generation grids per asset size (trees on a large grid, grass on a small one), cells scheduled by distance and view direction, `pcg.FrameTime` 16.667 ms of PCG per frame by default, 16 components generating at once, generation radii that grow with grid size. PCG GPU (Beta) runs a thread per point, instances "exist only at runtime in GPU memory", and the docs say to keep GPU nodes grouped to minimise CPU-GPU transfers. [S] [PCG generation modes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/using-pcg-generation-modes-in-unreal-engine), [PCG GPU](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/using-pcg-with-gpu-processing-in-unreal-engine?lang=en-US)

**HLOD, RVT, Nanite foliage.** World Partition HLOD proxies stand in for unloaded cells (an "Instancing" layer type is "ideal for imposter meshes, such as trees and foliage"). Runtime virtual texturing builds texels on the GPU on demand; the docs recommend streaming baked low mips and rendering only high mips at runtime. Nanite foliage (Experimental) uses assemblies (one tree's asset 3.5 GB to about 29 MB) and voxels instead of billboards in the distance. [S] [HLOD](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/world-partition---hierarchical-level-of-detail-in-unreal-engine), [RVT](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/runtime-virtual-texturing-in-unreal-engine), [Nanite foliage](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage?application_version=5.7) Nanite is too heavy to port to a WebGPU page; the ideas that carry over are baked coarse layer plus generated detail near the camera.

**GPU-driven culling.** Assassin's Creed Unity: coarse CPU cull, GPU instance cull, cluster cull, compaction, multi-draw; Hi-Z built from the best occluders plus reprojected last-frame depth, 1 to 2 orders of magnitude fewer draw calls. [S] [Haar and Aaltonen, SIGGRAPH 2015](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/aaltonenhaar_siggraph2015_combined_final_footer_220dpi.pdf) Our worker already culls objects on the GPU into an indirect draw, so this is held, not new.

### 2.5 WebGPU practice

- **Compile off the hot path.** `createComputePipelineAsync` / `createRenderPipelineAsync` resolve when the pipeline "can be used without any stalling"; MDN calls the async form "generally preferable" because it "prevents blocking of GPU operation execution on pipeline compilation". [S] [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GPUDevice/createComputePipelineAsync), [spec](https://gpuweb.github.io/gpuweb/#dom-gpudevice-createcomputepipelineasync) The spec expects browsers to keep compile caches that help "after the first visit"; the first visit still pays. [S] spec section 2.2.4.
- **Generate data on the GPU; avoid readback.** Toji's upload guide ends at "generate your data on the GPU" as the ultimate technique: no staging buffers, no copies. [S] [toji.dev buffer uploads](https://toji.dev/webgpu-best-practices/buffer-uploads) Indirect args let compute define the next dispatch or draw without CPU readback (`dispatchWorkgroupsIndirect` takes three u32). [S] spec section 16.1.2. Put all indirect args in one buffer: on Dawn D3D12 separate buffers add hidden validation dispatches (412 draws: about 3 ms down to about 10 µs when combined). [S] [toji.dev indirect draws](https://toji.dev/webgpu-best-practices/indirect-draws)
- **Multi-draw indirect is not standard**; Chrome's is experimental behind a flag. Use one `drawIndirect` per possible draw with counts written by compute. [S] [Chrome 131](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-131), [gpuweb #1354](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/issues/1354)
- **Do not queue long GPU work.** Chrome's watchdog kills the GPU process on an operation taking "10 seconds or so"; the spec says a watchdog may fire after "a few seconds". [S] [toji.dev device loss](https://toji.dev/webgpu-best-practices/device-loss) Pacing slices with `queue.onSubmittedWorkDone()` is [I] (the spec defines the promise, not the use).
- **Uploads.** `writeBuffer` is "always a safe fallback"; Chrome 144 made `writeBuffer`/`writeTexture` "up to 2X" faster. [S] toji.dev buffer uploads, [Chrome 144](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-144). Measure before building a staging ring.
- **Limits.** Defaults: `maxStorageBufferBindingSize` 128 MiB, `maxBufferSize` 256 MiB, `maxComputeWorkgroupsPerDimension` 65,535, `maxTextureArrayLayers` 256. [S] spec section 3.6.2. The window's 8.4 million slots at 28 bytes (235 MB) is above the default binding size, so it already needs raised limits. [R/I]
- **Transfer, do not copy.** A transferred `ArrayBuffer` moves between workers without a copy and needs no cross-origin isolation; `SharedArrayBuffer` needs COOP and COEP. [S] [MDN SharedArrayBuffer](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Global_Objects/SharedArrayBuffer)
- **Persistent cache.** OPFS is origin-private, fast, with `createSyncAccessHandle` in dedicated workers; Chromium allows up to 60% of disk per origin, best-effort data can be evicted, `navigator.storage.persist()` opts out. [S] [web.dev OPFS](https://web.dev/articles/origin-private-file-system), [MDN FileSystemSyncAccessHandle](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/FileSystemSyncAccessHandle), [MDN quotas](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria) `fetch(url, { priority: "low" })` marks prefetches low; support Chrome 102, Firefox 132, Safari 17.2. [S] [web.dev fetch priority](https://web.dev/articles/fetch-priority)
- **No sparse textures in WebGPU.** The issue is open, milestone "4+". Use a software page table with a coarse tier always resident, which `pyramid.js` already is. [S] [gpuweb #455](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/issues/455)

## 3. Ranked plan for this page

Order is by (time the operator waits removed) over (work and risk). Effects marked [I] are estimates from the measured parts; none was run. Items 1 to 3 do not touch the generators; 4 does.

### Step 0 (before anything): split the clock

`stats.ms` has tiles, fine, pack, flora. Add inside flora: table, count/rank/rows/scan, write, readback, JS cut, lay, and the first-versus-second window difference with the GPU free. Also time `applyGround` (setSources, setGround, buildCanopy, farBuild, survey) and the page's camera-height rework (0.1 to 0.3 s). Files: `groundwin.js`, `scenery.ts objectsOn`, `worker.js applyGround`. Cheap, and it settles where the 0.5 to 3 s goes (GPU kernels, readback, or the JS loops) before items 3 and 4 are sized. This is the only item whose result decides the others.

### 1. Compile the window's kernels at load, async (cost: small; first-window effect not separately measured)

Make `RASTER`, `FINE_K`, the scatter's `table`, `count` (entries `count`, `rank`, `rows`, `scan`), `write`, and every size of window pipeline `pipelineSoon` at idle after the planet opens, so the first window finds them made (precedent: `store.ts makeAhead`). Files: `demo/tile.ts`, `src/gpu/globe/scatter.ts kernelsOf/scatterTile`, `src/gpu/terrain/scenery.ts objectsOn`, `worker.js` after `createWindows`. Why first: the first window cost 4.5 to 7.7 s against 1.3 to 4.2 s later, but the first number was taken beside a bake, so the compile share is unknown [M, I]; the bench's 100 to 300 ms per large kernel says it is not small. Risk: none beyond idle-time GPU compile threads; check no `pipeline()` call remains on the window path.

### 2. Ask early, from intent, and keep what was built (cost: page logic only)

Today the ask starts when the view already fits a window (7.8 km half-extent) and zoom is at least -1.0. Instead start the build for the window under the zoom anchor as soon as the descent has a stable target: zoom velocity inward (the wheel's target zoom, not the eased zoom), anchor within a few km for about 300 ms. This is Cesium's `preloadFlightDestinations` and Unreal's destination streaming source. Add a second trigger from pan velocity: when the camera is within about 3 km of the window's edge (the re-anchor test is `SIZE/2 - 600`) and moving outward, build the window for the point where it will be in a few seconds (Cesium `loadSiblings` as an idea). Cancel or replace a speculative build whose target moved more than a window away (`cullRequestsWhileMoving`). Keep the last two windows (ground packed buffers and object buffers) so a back-and-forth pan or a zoom out and in does not rebuild. Prefetch the four ground tiles with `fetch(..., { priority: "low" })` as soon as the anchor is stable (tiles are only 0.04 to 0.24 s, so this matters little alone). Files: `live.js wantGround/asked`, `worker.js groundWant/built`, `groundwin.js tiles Map keep`. Effect [I]: for a descent longer than the build, the wait is hidden; a wheel flick that lands in under about 2 s still waits, which items 3 and 4 shorten. Risks: builds start for places the user never reaches (GPU and memory spent; napkin 2026-10-22 warns that a window per pan step helped crash the GPU beside a bake), so one speculative build at a time and none while a window is being applied.

### 3. Ground first, objects after, fading in (cost: moderate, touches `applyGround`)

Split the swap. The ground (tiles, fine, pack: about 0.8 to 1.0 s of the build [M]) is complete and useful alone: the ladder's painter draws the 3 m ground with banks and streams, and the planet layer's cover already tells the forest from the grass. Publish it as soon as the pack is done (post `ground` with `objects: none`, `applyGround` with an empty source), then fill objects in as chunks (item 4) with a per-instance birth time and a 200 to 400 ms alpha ramp in the cull pass, the instance equivalent of Mittring's blend value and MapLibre's raster cross-fade. Until the first chunk exists nothing is empty: planet layer, then painted ground, then trees. Files: `groundwin.js build()` returns `pack` before the scatter; `worker.js applyGround/winSpec/setSources` accepts a growing source; `webgpu.js` cull pass takes a `born` word (the body has `pick` and slot words; one more column or a per-chunk uniform). Effect [I]: time to the first close frame from 1.3 to 4.2 s to about 0.8 to 1.0 s (tiles, fine, pack). Risk: the re-anchor swap currently relies on one gid for ground and objects together (`render()` stale-frame rule); keep one gid per ground and let objects be added under it; `forgetSource`, `buildCanopy`, `farBuild` and `survey` must tolerate a source that grows (they now run once per switch).

### 4. Scatter by chunks around the camera, kept on the GPU (cost: the largest; effect: the largest)

The scatter is where about half to two thirds of a window's time goes [M] and it is built for 368 km² when a view needs about 1 km² near the camera. Turn it into a generator of chunks, nearest first, never read back.

1. Chunks: cut the window into chunk squares (start at 32 by 32 cells, 2.4 km; tune by item 0). `scatterTile`'s `window` rectangle and stable ids already make a chunk the same objects as the tile's run cut to it. Priority = distance from the camera target plus view direction (PCG scheduling policy), re-sorted each frame; chunks the camera has left are dropped (Cesium `cullRequestsWhileMoving`).
2. Retain the tile's count result: `objectsOn` runs `table`, `count`, `rank`, `rows`, `scan` over the whole tile for the ids, then drops them. Keep each tile's `idx` buffer (and the tile's `packed` and fine `field`, the latter so `scatterTile` stops re-running `raster` and `fine` that `windowFine` already ran) for the life of the window, so each chunk costs only a `write` pass. Size the `write` outputs to the chunk, not to the tile (today five buffers of up to 17.7 million words each, about 354 MB, are created per tile even when a window uses a quarter of it) [R].
3. No readback: a compute pass after `write` places (the face turn is a 90 degree rotation and an offset of integer-quantized positions, `placer` in `groundwin.js`), thins by `unitHash(seed) < share`, and writes straight into the window's `body` slots and per-cell `start` runs (a count, a scan and a scatter by atomics; `ordered: false` already permits any order inside a cell). Only the run's count needs a tiny readback, or none with an indirect dispatch. Replaces `winobjects.js` `collector` and `lay`, and the 5 readbacks, and the two JS loops over every object.
4. Density by zoom, nested: the share `s` of objects needed at a ladder level is a function of that level (a tree under a pixel is not drawn), and because thinning is by seed hash, raising `s` only adds objects. Build every chunk at a low share first (wide views, far lists) and the camera chunks at full share, then top up. This is Flight Simulator's Halton "density controlled at draw time" and Horizon's mipmapped density. It also dissolves the `top` cap and its "a window is thinned to a quarter of the island's density" gap for the chunks that are close.
5. Budget: slice the dispatches so none is above a few ms (a pass is `ceil(quads / 64)` workgroups; cut by rows of quads), pace with `queue.onSubmittedWorkDone`, and cap chunk work per frame (Tsushima's 1/36th per frame, `pcg.FrameTime`). Wide views must not request a window at every pan step (napkin rule): with chunk cost proportional to what is visible and a per-frame cap, that rule can be relaxed later.
6. Pool buffers by chunk size; reuse them as chunks are evicted; one indirect-args buffer for all draws (toji.dev).

Files: `src/gpu/terrain/scenery.ts objectsOn`, `src/gpu/globe/scatter.ts scatterTile`, `src/gpu/wgsl/terrain-scenery.ts` (a `place_thin_bin` kernel beside `write`), `demo/tile.ts`, `groundwin.js build/placer`, `winobjects.js` (deleted at cutover), `worker.js applyGround/winSpec`, `webgpu.js` source growth. Effect [I]: first trees at the camera in the time of one count over the tile plus one chunk `write` and place (item 0 will say how many ms; a 2.4 km chunk is 1/64 of the window's area), and the full-window total no longer on the critical path. Risks: (a) determinism and ids: the plan says the same objects every visit; keep `write` and ids untouched and compare a chunked build with the old build's objects by id at one window on the CPU; (b) the placement port from doubles to integers must agree with `placer`'s check (6e-14 cells); (c) GPU contention and watchdog: all slices are small; (d) napkin: Apple Metal timestamps are unreliable, judge by frame times; (e) memory: a chunked source needs the 128 MiB binding limit logic of `CAPS` per chunk pool, not per window.

### 5. Remove the ground's readback and CPU repack (cost: moderate; effect about 0.3 to 0.6 s of the 0.8 to 1.0 s) [I]

`windowFine` reads back about 33 MB of Int32 fine layers and a 16 MB mask, converts to float, `cropFlip` and `flipCells` loop on the CPU, `packGround` copies into a 40 MB float array, and the result is uploaded again. The CPU truly needs only `h` (for `heightAt`, the camera heights, 0.1 to 0.3 s page work) and the river list. Do the crop, flip and Q16/Q24 conversion in one compute kernel that writes the painter's `fine` layout directly into the ground buffer `setGround` uses; read back only `h` (4 MB), late, a frame or two after (a `MAP_READ` ring), so the page's height work follows the first frame instead of gating it. Files: `demo/tile.ts windowFine` (do not destroy `field`), `groundwin.js` (lines 129 to 167), `ground.wgsl.js packGround`, `webgpu.js setGround`. Risk: `ground-parity.ts` must still agree (it caught drift before); keep `packGround` as the oracle and compare buffers for one window.

### 6. Keep the CPU parts off the render thread (cost: moderate; effect: smoother frames during a build)

Decode and assemble in a plain build worker: tile fetch, gunzip, `decode`, the per-cell `own/at` assembly and plane copy (lines 60 to 128 of `groundwin.js`), then post transferable arrays. The render worker only calls `writeBuffer`. Do not start a second GPU device unless item 0 shows the CPU parts dominate (a second device's long dispatches would still block the shared GPU process [I]). Also move `pyramid.js` inflate and `packFields` into the same worker, and give its `load()` an `AbortController` so tiles the camera left are cancelled instead of queued behind (deck.gl and Cesium both do this), with `priority: "low"` for the coarser prefetch ring. Files: new `isopixel/build-worker.js`, `groundwin.js`, `pyramid.js load/want`. Risk: transfer lists and the existing `buffer()` helper in `worker.js`; the sim also lives in the render worker and wants its thread.

### 7. Pyramid: cross-fade tiers, prefetch ring, persistent cache (cost: small to moderate)

- Blend the finer tier in over about 300 ms instead of replacing it at once (Mittring's blend value; CDLOD morph): a per-tile `arrived` time in the page table entry, read by `wgsl/globe.js`. [S/I]
- Prefetch the ring one tile outward in the direction of pan velocity and the next finer tier under the zoom anchor (`planet.js wanted()` builds the list from a 12 by 8 screen grid; add the predicted view). `inflight` 6 becomes a priority queue (centre first, Cesium foveation). [S/I]
- Persist inflated tile bytes in OPFS (worker, `createSyncAccessHandle`), keyed by the pyramid key, so a reload starts warm; call `navigator.storage.persist()`. Ground tiles (6 MB each, 6 to 8 kept) go there too. [S/I] Effect is for revisits, not first looks; lowest priority of this list.

### 8. Later: the window as a clipmap (cost: large; effect: removes the re-anchor frame and window rebuilds at the edge)

Today a window is rebuilt and the ground swapped (one 78 ms frame [M]) when the view nears its edge. A toroidal window (GPU Gems 2) would rewrite only the strip the camera exposes, coarse levels stay resident, and the ladder's ground addressing never changes frame. It touches the painter's field addressing, `FRM`, the sim's frame and `gid`; do it only after 2 to 4 land and the chunk machinery exists, since the chunks are already the strip unit.

## 4. Risks that cut across

- Determinism and ids (plan Unit 6: "the same objects every visit"): chunks reuse the tile's id space; test equality against the existing build before deleting `winobjects.js`.
- Two views at once (a wide ladder level and a close one cross-fade): chunk priority must cover both cameras' needs, and the far lists (`farBuild`) need the low-share objects for the whole window.
- GPU shared with the sim and another agent's jobs; frame cost must be read from frame times, not Metal timestamps (napkin Execution item 7 and Shell item 7).
- Any change to the scatter's kernels changes the planet store key if it hashes those sources; the plan says the key hashes grid, tectonics, erosion, atmosphere and ice sources, not scatter, but check before editing `terrain-scenery.ts` ([I], from napkin GPU Sim item 7).
- Memory: retained per-tile `idx` and `field` buffers for 4 to 9 tiles; measure before keeping more than the window's tiles.
