## 1. The global discharge (R1835)
`dischargeOf(gpu, dir, tier, fields)` sorts the whole forest of `t4-to.u32` by depth (pointer jumping, `Regional.forest`: 1.1 to 1.3 s, deepest path 2,339 cells, no flood, no tier table) and sums a u32 weight file down it exactly in 64 bits (`area64`: low word in ACC, high in ER). Output per weight `stem`: `<stem>-lo.u32` + `<stem>-hi.u16` (bits 32 to 47; a weight under 2^21 a cell cannot pass 2^48), a count of cells (`ONES`) is `<stem>.u32`. The water uses the four seasons' surplus (`t4-q0..3`, 1/64 m3 a season: the water kernel's own Q6 volume) and `t4-cells`; the year is their sum. 2.8 GB on disk (6 B x 4 + 4 B a cell).
- Staged forest crossing a face edge (tier 1, weights about 4e8 a cell): 0 of 1.5 M cells wrong against the CPU twin, 10,000+ cells past 2^32, largest sum above 2^40; `forest(to)` alone gives the same bytes as the flood's own tree.
- Real tier 4 (key c418e5fe9b5e), 1,000 cells walked up the forest on the CPU (the largest basins' mouths, other mouths, hillslope and river cells): 0 wrong, 41 past 32 bits, largest basin 1,665,951 cells. Cost: sort 1.1 s, each field 1.4 to 2.1 s including its write; the 5 fields of the water 9 s in all (load of the machine: other agents running; upper bound).

## 2. The water stage

Checked: `water.test.ts` seeds 1, 2, 3 (goldens 1456e5487361e378, b82e86c570fc740c, 116cf847c3c043b0) and `window.test.ts` "a staged window is the same bytes twice" (39e4d2f8d7203637) and "a window's channels drain" (77.2%, 98.6%, 379, 1.0%) pass, run one case at a time in a worktree of HEAD carrying my two kernel files (the main tree's island goldens currently move with the air agent's unfinished rain/climate edits, not with water). 16-bit sort keys: the island's `levels`/`gather` already use 14 jumping rounds and u32 indices (depth up to 8,190, flagged beyond).
- Receivers: the planet's `t4-to.u32` mapped with `localOf` (outside the patch: an outlet, `to = self`); flooded surface `t4-flooded.i32`; sea = height <= 0.
- Hollows (R1830): `t4-basin.u8`, made once over the whole tier by `basinOf` (CPU, 4-neighbour components of cells the flood raised by 1 cm, across tile and face edges; a component whose deepest point is over 3 m is a lake): 21.4 M land cells; a lake stands at the flooded surface at every cell, every lake spills (the island's balance for a part-full lake needs the whole hollow and its flows). The island's `hollows`/`lakecand` (O(B^2)) are not run in the planet format.
- Inflow (R1833): for each cell outside the patch whose receiver is inside, its global discharge (cells and four seasons' volumes) is added at the receiver as exact 64-bit words (`Inflow.q6`). Identity tested: over the patch's own forest each cell's surplus plus the inflow gathers to exactly the global discharge at every cell of the patch (4 seasons); and with no rain in a lake-free patch the kernel's flow in every cell away from the sea and lakes is the CPU's gather of the inflow words, to the word (0 of the cells wrong).
- Void cells (R1834): a patch at a cube's corner repeats the nearest cell where a corner leaves none: such a cell is a sea cell with zero transmissivity.
- Aquifer (R1832): a planet's patch holds no head at its ring (no water crosses its outer ring of cells; the island's ring held at the ground less 3 m fed water into the ground), is relaxed in two colours across all the grid's workgroups (`aqrb`, CHUNK 64 sweeps a submit, the change of the last sweep read back) at 1.9 to a head change under 0.5 mm. The island's 1.6 and 2 mm stop 0.7 m short of the answer on a grid this size (the stopping change over 1 - rho); the sums of head and flow agreed to 1e-6 between 0.5 and 0.25 mm. Over-relaxation above 1.93 stalls on the integer arithmetic's noise (changes of 100 to 130 units). Caps 80,000 sweeps a solve; a tile that needs more throws.
- Climate: `ClimateSource` per cell, never a window's value. Until the air stage's files exist: `planetRain(planet)` (the planet's tier-0 rain per season sampled to the cell; potential evaporation = rain less runoff) [gap: not a PET]. `climateFiles(pr, pet, perMm)` reads u16 files when they exist (names to wire from the air notes).
- Drift/rock/thickness: the `terrain` stage's files; the thickness is held where rock + drift transmissivity would pass the sweep's u32 word (a format's bound).
- Outputs (documented at the top of water.ts): `t4-water.u16` (cm), `t4-flow.u32` (Q16 m3/s), `t4-qwet.u32` (wettest season, Q16), `t4-base.u16` (Q16), `t4-chn.u8`, `t4-valley.u8`, `t4-pwr.u16` (1/16 W/m2), `t4-stb0..3.u16` and `t4-dry.u16` (table depth, 1/256 m, held at 255.99 m; was 1/64 until GroundStage asked): 2.6 GB. Not kept: springs, `under`, the year's table, slope (from the heights).

## 4. Gaps (not retuned)
1. **Lakes leak.** A lake holds the ground's head fixed at its level, as the island's; in dry mountains with a high-transmissivity rock it is a source with no inflow behind it (the gather then clamps its negative net at 0, so the leaked water appears in rivers beside it). Flow over the global discharge at cells over 0.3 m3/s: tile 14 (coast) 0.93, tile 0 1.0, tile 1 2.8, 937 5.8, 1500 26, 458 20 (median ratios). Sealing the lake bed (transmissivity 0 or 1/64) left 458 at 12 or made it fail to settle: the ground then has no anchoring head. The proper fix is the island's own balance made global: a hollow holds a lake only if its inflow (the global discharge, exact) exceeds its evaporation (`lake_mm` over the spill depth), decided once over the tier, with leakage limited to its inflow.
2. **Inflow is the surplus, not the island's weights** (surplus less recharge plus ground water out less lake evaporation): upstream cells outside the patch lack those corrections. Measured as the flow difference between halos (above), 1 to 23 of 65,536 m3/s here; a two-pass (weights out, gather again, rerun) would close it.
3. **The aquifer has long modes.** Where no drain, lake or sea anchors the head over a patch (about 2 of 24 tiles, 29,824 to 42,688 sweeps), the change falls 4x per 12,800 sweeps; the stop (0.5 mm a sweep) leaves a metres-deep error in the table that depends on the patch: tile 854 (face 3): two patches differ by 3.4 m anywhere and 0.9 m within 5 m of the ground. Not tile independent there. A coarse-grid correction or a global aquifer solve is the fix.
4. Dry hollows: no lake is dropped, so flow passes through every hollow (the island cuts at a closed lake).
5. Stand-in climate (see above) until the air stage's files; the planet's rain (212 mm a year on land) leaves most tiles with surplus near zero.
6. `basinOf` runs on the CPU once (10 s class), its output is a function of the flood only.
7. The hydrology's constants are per 75 m cell (`CELL`); tier 4's cells are 63 to 75 m (grid gap 7); no radius-dependent constant is read, so `similarityOf` has nothing to map here.
8. Tests that cannot be run here: second GPU adapter; the full 1,536-tile run (30 to 45 min of GPU) was not made.

## 7. Unit 5b, the water table as one solve: where it stands (WaterStage, 2026-10-21)
**Not met: a, b (the relaxation itself), c (the packed state), d, f. Met: the identity-defined order.** I stopped after the order and the design because the whole of requirement (d) is a port of the island's hydrology to global passes, which I could not finish in one run without leaving the tree half-wired; what I measured and what the port is:

- **The order (a, built and tested).** `src/gpu/globe/aquifer.ts` `colourOf`: the cells are four colours defined by identity (parity of X + Y on the face; a cell whose neighbour across an edge has its own parity and a larger id is `late`, colour 2 + parity). The cube-sphere's 4-neighbour graph is not bipartite (three cells at a corner are pairwise neighbours), so no two-colouring exists; the four colours are a proper colouring (0 neighbouring pairs share one at tiers 0 and 1, `aquifer.test.ts`), late cells 4 fn: 16,384 at tier 4 (0.016%), a list to visit apart from the grid. A sweep that updates colour 0, 1, 2, 3 in turn, each from the others, has one answer for any tiling, decomposition or visiting order of the work inside a colour.
- **The relaxation does not fit as the island's kernels are.** A tile's coefficients (weights ww, we, wn, ws, the source sso, the plants' sink eaf, the head, the ground, the cap) are 72 bytes a cell when packed (head, h, fxv, a meta word of isf, rock, drift and the root fill, 4 weights, sso and eaf, four pe and four sh in Q16 for the draw): 7.25 GB at tier 4 (100.7 M cells), over the 7 GB the brief allows and only with the GPU to itself; the island's per-patch layout is 150 words a cell. At tier 3 (25 M cells) it is 1.8 GB. A global relaxation reads that whole state every sweep: about 0.1 s a sweep at 100 GB/s.
- **Sweeps.** Measured on patches of 448 cells (halo 96): 5,000 to 12,000 sweeps at the over-relaxation 1.9 to a change under half a millimetre where the patch has a drain, and an unanchored plateau needs over 40,000 (the change falls 4 times in 12,800 sweeps). The settling sweeps grow with the span, as its square for Gauss-Seidel and linearly with over-relaxation: a planet 16,000 cells across needs of the order of 10^5 to 10^6 sweeps from a flat start (3 to 30 hours at 0.1 s), so a coarse-to-fine start is not optional: tier 3's solution (the same law on its 150 m cells with the harmonic-mean transmissivity of four children and the children's summed sources) is a function of the global fields alone and leaves only the short modes to tier 4. That needs the coefficients built at every tier.
- **The port (d), what a global solve of everything stored is.** The second solve's inputs (the lakes' levels, the channels' stage, the flows with the ground water) are themselves outputs of a first solve, so each is a global pass between relaxations: (1) the channel flag is an OR down the forest (a cell is a channel if it begins one or anything draining to it is): a gather of the `begins` flag with the exact 64-bit gather; (2) the year's flow is the exact discharge already made; (3) relaxation 1; (4) the ground-water outflux of each cell and the five weights of the seasons' routes, each gathered globally and exactly (the island clamps a lake cell's negative net at 0 where it passes downstream: a mask in the gather kernel); (5) the lakes' water, the channels' stage from the new flow, relaxation 2; (6) the outflux and the five gathers again, then the flows, the tables, the seasons; (7) the valley is a walk down the forest to the nearest channel or open cell (`tgt`) and the plc counts of `under` an aggregation per target: forest passes with the depth sort `Regional.forest` already gives. Every other kernel is a per-cell function of these global fields with a reach of one cell (the outer ring of a halo-2 patch changes nothing in the interior when the channel flag and the discharges come from the global files). Each pass writes global files; the disk is about 6 u32 fields (2.4 GB) more than now.
- **What I would build, in this order:** (i) `Regional.gather64` with a clamp mask and a u8 OR gather (small); (ii) a tile pass that builds the packed coefficients from halo-2 patches with the island's own prep/aqprep/aqw kernels (exact for the interior, tile independence testable by two tilings of the build: bytes equal); (iii) the global relaxation kernel over the packed state with the colours above, `gw_et` taking its parameters instead of reading F (a refactor of the one law so the global kernel and the island's share it); (iv) coarse-to-fine (tier 3 first); (v) the passes of the port; (vi) tests: two tile orders of the build and of the sweeps give equal bytes, the tier-2 staged planet end to end, and a pinned real tile. The depth-cap fallback (c) measures as follows: within 5 m of the ground at halo 96 the tables of two tilings differ on 14 of 1,551 cells by 1/64 m, so a cap does not by itself give the same bytes; only the global order does.

## 9. The real tier 3 (coarsened from tier 4 of key 883a8d9387df): the first measurement, and what it changed
**A bug of mine fixed first (ec21ea0):** `scripts/globe-water.ts` read the air stage's rain words as a linear 0.1 mm; they are companded (sqrt(mm) x 360). Every planet-wide water file made from the air files before that is stale.
**Tier-3 inputs** (`/tmp/planet-75m/water-scratch/t3`, scripts in water-scratch: t3make.ts, t3global.ts): heights are the 2 x 2 means of tier 4's, receivers and flooded surface the regional flood's on them, rock/drift/thickness the terrain stage at tier 3, rain and potential evaporation the 2 x 2 means of the air stage's mm, surplus, exact discharge (deepest path 1,534; 5 gathers 4.8 s) and the lakes (68,989 lakes, 45,065 dry hollows: the means leave many pits). The cell constants are tier 4's 75 m (so the aquifer's conductance to recharge is that of a 75 m cell on a grid of 2,048: a pessimistic stand-in for convergence, not a 150 m planet).
**Relaxation 1, real, 25.2 M cells, GPU shared (22 ms a sweep, 6 times the staged tier's 3.1 ms per M cells' worth because of load):** after 9,408 sweeps of the colours at 1.9 the greatest change of a head in a sweep is 5,134 units (4 cm a sweep) against the stop's 67 (0.5 mm): not settled, and not near. The staged planet's 1,984 sweeps were the best case.
**Why (the arithmetic of the sweep):** Gauss-Seidel with over-relaxation w on a Laplace-like problem of width N cells has the error of its slowest mode fall by rho per sweep, rho = ((w mu + sqrt(w^2 mu^2 - 4 (w - 1))) / 2)^2, mu = cos(pi / N): at N = 2,048 and w = 1.9, rho = 0.99995, an e-fold in 20,000 sweeps; at w = 1.99 an e-fold in 2,000; the optimum 2 / (1 + sin(pi / N)) = 1.9985. At tier 4 (N = 4,096 a face, the planet 16,000 across) twice that. And a stop on the change of a sweep is an error of change / (1 - rho): 0.5 mm of change a sweep at rho 0.99995 is 10 m of head.
**What is fixed by a stored word's definition, not by convergence:** a stored word is a pure function of the coefficients, the start and the number of sweeps made (the order inside a colour does not matter), so the bytes are the same under any tiling at any sweep count; convergence is a quality of the answer, not of its identity. The stop is therefore made on the cells the ground and the flows read: the greatest change of a sweep over the cells whose table is within 8 m of the ground (`Settled.shallow`, [design]; the deep table's slow modes are no part of it), reported beside the all-cell change. Calls resume (g-head<phase>-part.i32, a chunk of 64 sweeps is the whole state's step, so a split gives the same bytes).

## 10. The packed global state (tier 4's memory plan), and the first run of it
**Layout** (wgsl/globe-aquifer.ts header): 13 words a cell plus the tier's heights: A (6 planes: head, fxv, sso, eaf, meta, height), B (8 planes: ww, we, wn, ws, the potential evaporation of four seasons as u16 of 0.1 mm in two words, the unmet demand the same). `meta` holds the fixed-head flag, the rock, the drift, the colour and the root fill. 14 x 403 MB = 5.6 GB at tier 4 in two buffers of 2.4 and 3.2 GB (the 4.29 GB binding holds each; 4 storage buffers a stage). The island's own functions read it through `ld` (the island's field offsets resolved to planes, the draw's constants from the rock, drift and fill by `gw_params`, `wd` as 2^31 less the four weights, the draw's flag as `eaf` not 0): one law. The passes write the planes straight to files (5.2 GB a phase on disk at tier 4; the heights are not copied).
**Its effect on the answer:** on the planet the potential evaporation and the unmet demand are held to 0.1 mm (the air stage's own unit for the potential evaporation, so that is exact for it; the unmet demand is rounded by under 0.05 mm a season, 0.02 to 0.5% of its 10 to 300 mm). Every pass uses the same words, so the water is consistent.
**On the device (staged tier 2, GPU shared with two other jobs):** the three cases of water-global.test.ts pass on the packed kernel: eight passes equal on four offset patches (14,680,064 words), the relaxation visited in reverse the same bytes (1,600 sweeps to the shallow stop: 41.6 s and 48.7 s under three-way load; the same relaxation unloaded was 3.1 ms a sweep, so these are upper bounds). 3. Accept the sweeps as the definition (the same bytes under any tiling, a stored table that is "the table after K sweeps"): not an answer for a plateau.

## 13. The water at tiers 4 and 5 (the orchestrator's decision 2026-10-22): the global cascade at tier 3, patches seeded from it

**Definition.** The table's two solves are made once over the whole planet at tier 3 by the cascade (`water-global.ts` `cascadeOf`, constants `CASCADE` = 3,000 sweeps at each tier from the coarsest, 4,000 at tier 0, then 9,000 more at the finest, over-relaxed 1.99; in memory, 25 M cells, about 300 s a solve in calls of 230 s, resumable at any call split with the same bytes); the heads are `t3-g-head1.i32` and `t3-g-head2.i32`. Tiers 4 and 5 are tile stages (`waterStage` with `seed`): each tile's patch (halo `WATER_SEEDED.halo` = 128) starts both aquifer solves from those heads carried to the cell (`water-seed.ts` `seedOf`: bilinear in the face's index space for any ratio of tiers, across a face's edge by `cellAcross`, a function of the coarse heads alone), holds the patch's outer ring at them (`WaterFormat.seeded`, kernel `aqprep`), and makes exactly `WATER_SEEDED.sweeps` = 48 red-black sweeps (`relaxFixed`: no read back, no stop on the change). A red-black sweep reaches 2 cells, so a cell's table is a function of the coarse global heads and of the fine data within 2 x 48 + 32 = 128 cells: the same words under any tiling.
**The cell's width per tier (found by measuring).** The first tier-3 solve ran the kernels with the 75 m cell's constants on 150 m cells (the recharge over a cell and the plants' draw go with the cell's area, the reach with its width): the seeded tiles then differed from a long relaxation by 77 m (tile 854) and 127 m (tile 937) in 1 to 3% of the cells within 8 m of the ground. `WaterFormat.cell` (metres; `waterFormat(tier, side, finest)`, 75 x 2^(finest - tier)) puts it into the constants (`REACH_*`, `RA_K`, `MMV`, `SPRING_K`, `KD_K`, the channel head, the valley plain); the surplus stage, the chain's passes and the patch take it (`finest` in `WaterConfig`, `surplusStage`); with no `finest` the cell is 75 m as before (the island's goldens, the staged tests).
