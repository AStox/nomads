Operator, 2026-10-23: "I'm worried that the close-up area is the wrong way to approach this. A better approach would be some kind of data streaming that works at any level for any space (levels of detail in any area, just streamed in). I don't like these set chunks that we load in. Do deeper web research to really determine how other people are doing planet-scale rendering. Surely this is a solved problem now."

This note replaces its steps 3 and 4 (chunks inside a window) with a quadtree design; steps 1 (compile early) and 5 (GPU ground) carry over. Nothing was run for this note; the numbers in section 1 were measured on the page before the task changed.

## 0. Answer

Yes, it is solved for terrain, and mostly solved for vegetation. Every system found does the same loop: one quadtree of fixed-size tiles, the tiles to draw chosen every frame by projected error, missing tiles produced on demand (streamed, generated, or both) under a per-frame budget and a hard-capped cache, and the nearest finished ancestor drawn until the child is ready. None builds a fixed window. Vegetation is a per-tile GPU pass keyed by position, with density a threshold on a stable per-instance rank, so a coarse tile's set is a subset of the fine one; far away, trees stop being instances and become colour in the ground.

For this page: make the 6-tier pyramid the only structure. Add two procedural tiers below 75 m (the fine-ground kernel already is one), make the painter and the object culler read tiles through a page table, produce each tile's ground and objects on the GPU on demand, and delete the window.

## 2. How others do it

### 2.1 The common loop

1. A quadtree (or octree) of equal-cost tiles. 2. Per frame, a cheap pass picks tiles by projected error or distance. 3. Missing tiles go to a priority queue with a per-frame time or count budget; requests for tiles that left the view are dropped. 4. The nearest ready ancestor is drawn meanwhile. 5. A hard-capped cache evicts by LRU; if it is full, refinement stops or the error target is raised. [S] sources per system below.

### 2.2 Terrain and planets

**Cesium.**
- Tree: 3D Tiles are a tree with a `geometricError` per tile; the client turns it into pixels and refines "If the introduced SSE exceeds the maximum allowed" ([3D Tiles spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CesiumGS/3d-tiles/main/specification/README.adoc)). Implicit tiling describes a huge quadtree without listing tiles, each child's error half its parent's ([implicit tiling](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CesiumGS/3d-tiles/main/specification/ImplicitTiling/README.adoc)). Terrain uses a quadtree with `maximumScreenSpaceError` 2 and 3D Tiles 16 ([QuadtreePrimitive.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CesiumGS/cesium/1.146/packages/engine/Source/Scene/QuadtreePrimitive.js), [Cesium3DTileset](https://cesium.com/learn/cesiumjs/ref-doc/Cesium3DTileset.html)).
- Production: terrain is pre-baked quantized-mesh, "a simple multi-resolution quadtree pyramid of meshes", with skirts for cracks ([quantized-mesh](https://github.com/CesiumGS/quantized-mesh)). Where data ends, a child is upsampled from its parent on the client and a tile whose four children are all upsampled is not refined further ([CesiumJS source via the research helper, `GlobeSurfaceTile.js`; `QuantizedMeshTerrainData.upsample`](https://cesium.com/learn/cesiumjs/ref-doc/QuantizedMeshTerrainData.html)).
- Budgets (opened in `RequestScheduler.js`, [source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CesiumGS/cesium/1.146/packages/engine/Source/Core/RequestScheduler.js)): 50 active requests, 18 per server, a priority heap of 20, priority re-evaluated every frame; time slices of 5 ms (load queue) and 2 ms (height updates) in `QuadtreePrimitive`; 512 MiB cache plus 512 MiB overflow for 3D Tiles; foveated deferral of edge tiles, `cullRequestsWhileMoving`, `progressiveResolutionHeightFraction` 0.3 for a quick coarse layer first.
- Parent until child: in REPLACE mode, if descendants are not renderable and none rendered last frame, "kick them all out of the render list and render this tile instead. Continue to load them though!" (`QuadtreePrimitive.js`, relayed by the research helper; cesium-native `loadingDescendantLimit` 20, `preloadAncestors` and `preloadSiblings` on by default, optional 1 s fade, `TilesetOptions.h`).
- Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles are plain 3D Tiles fed to any renderer ([Google docs](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/tile/3d-tiles)); the page gives no tuning numbers.

**Proland (Bruneton and Neyret). The closest match to our data.**
- Tree: a view-dependent terrain quadtree, "can be used to get planet sized terrains" through a deformation; a quad of size L splits "if the viewer distance is less than splitFactor * L" (headers in the GitHub mirror `zhongnanshan/proland-git`; the official sites timed out, so this is mirror-only).
- Production: a producer and consumer framework with a ref-counted tile cache keyed (producer, level, tx, ty), LRU for unused tiles, tiles produced on demand as tasks, producers on CPU or GPU. The residual elevation producer builds each level "by upsampling the parent tile, and by adding to it residual elevations"; residuals can be stored or "procedural: by using random residuals whose amplitude decreases with the quadtree level"; "null residuals for the missing scales" keep it smooth. A 2-sample border is enough to upsample recursively (mirror headers and `terrain/doc/overview.txt`, same mirror).
- Parent until child: `TileSampler::getMipMap()` is true when "a (part of) parent tile can be used instead of the tile itself for rendering... if a tile is not ready but a parent tile is" (mirror).
- Forests ([Bruneton and Neyret 2012](https://inria.hal.science/hal-00650120)): the abstract confirms two representations, nearest trees as individual objects and the rest as a shader on the terrain, "with a seamless transition between them... without popping nor aliasing". The helper read the PDF for the details: the same producer framework makes seeds at the quadtree level where a tree is about 3 pixels and coverage tiles above it, with a smoothstep blend between them. I could not re-extract that PDF text, so treat those details as relayed.

**Outerra.** Fine tiles are "generated by fractal algorithm entirely on the GPU" from a coarse cube-sphere dataset, "Level of detail ranging from thousands of kilometers down to centimeters" ([intro](https://outerra.blogspot.com/2008/07/intro.html)); all detail below 150 m is procedural. Trees are decided per 2 m grid cell by a world-coordinate hash and an ecotype probability, "The same random number vector is computed every time everywhere" (blog posts, [lots of trees 2008/08](https://outerra.blogspot.com/2008/08/lots-of-trees.html)); the farthest tiles have trees "baked in as colors". Tile sizes, budgets and seam handling are not published in what was opened.

**SpaceEngine.** Six cube-face quadtrees, every node 256 by 256 texels and 33 by 33 vertices, split until node textures are under 256 px on screen; 12 real levels plus 3 virtual ones for an Earth-size planet; textures made on demand, normals before colour, lowest nodes first; geometry can skip 3 levels so silhouettes load almost at once ([dev blog](https://spaceengine.org/news/blog171120/)). Single-float noise cannot make detail below about 10 m on a planet, so detail textures take over.

**No Man's Sky.** A voxel octree: 32 m regions, 6 LODs each twice the size, polygonised from noise, "generate using a point based system so the stuff in front of us we generate first", coarse first then finer on top, a dither fade driven by a 0 to 1 value when a region is ready, objects re-seated onto new terrain while fading ([GDC 2017 talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sCRzxEEcO2Y), auto-captions only).

**Elite Dangerous.** Cube faces as quadtrees, patches subdivided by distance, spherified and noised in compute shaders from a planet seed, high-LOD patches flat with textures for look, normals and height, noise functions in dual-float emulated 64 bit for precision ([80.lv interview](https://80.lv/articles/generating-the-universe-in-elite-dangerous)). No numbers.

**Kerbal Space Program and Star Citizen.** Thin sources. KSP's PQS builds quads from per-vertex modules of the direction vector, and stock builds run on the main thread, which is why BurstPQS moves them to a background thread ([Kopernicus PQS docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Kopernicus/wiki/master/docs/Syntax/PQS.md)). Star Citizen's planet shader "draw[s] on data to evaluate required terrain color when viewed at a distance - taking into account the distribution of assets that are not loaded in, such as trees or grass" ([wiki, secondary](https://starcitizen.tools/Planet_Tech_v5)).

**Flight Simulator 2020.** A Bing quadtree; `RecurseRequest()` picks the requested level from "Estimated tile screen size" and "Estimated available network bandwidth"; `RecurseDraw()` uses "the best currently available data. Tiles can use data from a parent"; because a tile may arrive at any time, tiles are cut with 15 precomputed index buffers or a mask texture and `discard`; skirts hide cracks; bandwidth is measured and requested levels corrected ([Fuentes, Asobo](https://www.asobostudio.com/files/inline-images/Designing_Terrain_System_Fuentes_Lionel.pdf), slides opened and checked). Flight Simulator 2024: no primary source on terrain found.

**Far Cry 5 terrain.** The closest match to a 6-tier tile tree. A 2 km root tile always resident, a 6-level quadtree below, "a maximum of about 500 resident tiles", each node 129 by 129 heights plus normal and albedo in synchronised atlases; the GPU holds the tree as a mip-mapped index texture (`0xffff` not loaded, `0xfffe` never exists) updated by compute each frame; a node subdivides only if all four children are loaded, otherwise "We will need to render something valid for any scenario"; about 2 ms GPU for terrain ([Ubisoft GDC 2018](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2018/presentations/TerrainRenderingFarCry5.pdf); the 500, the 6 levels, the sentinels and the quote were re-opened here).

**Call of Duty (Activision, SIGGRAPH 2023).** Adaptive virtual texturing: a feedback buffer written in the opaque pass and read back 3 frames later, counted, propagated to parents, biased by camera speed, never requesting more than 1/3 of the cache; composition budget 4 pages a frame on mobile up to 20 on PC; "some child nodes may not have their own page. As a result, they must point to the parent page"; a virtual height map of 144 pages of 64 by 64 in about 1.2 MB ([slides](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2023/Etienne(ATVI)-Large%20Scale%20Terrain%20Rendering%20with%20notes%20(Advances%202023).pdf)).

**Unreal 5.** Nanite draws a cluster "when parent error is too high and our error is small enough", a local test; the root page is "always resident, so we always have something to render"; the culling pass emits page requests prioritised by LOD error and the loaded set is always a valid cut ([Karis, SIGGRAPH 2021](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf)). A too-small streaming pool makes streaming thrash even for a static view ([technical details](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/nanite-technical-details)). Nanite landscape is baked in the editor, not runtime ([docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/using-nanite-with-landscapes-in-unreal-engine)). Authors of the HPG 2024 planet terrain paper say Nanite-style systems are "not applicable as the amounts of geometry involved are simply too large" ([arXiv 2407.02215](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.02215)).

**Concurrent binary trees (Dupuy).** A GPU triangle tree held as a bitfield heap; one thread per leaf; a split or merge per frame per leaf by projected size; the 2024 version for Earth-size terrain keeps a pool of 128k bisectors in 7 MiB, updates in about 0.1 ms and renders in 1.7 to 1.9 ms ([CBT paper](https://onrendering.com/data/papers/cbt/ConcurrentBinaryTrees.pdf), [HPG 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.02215)). It makes triangulation only; heights still come from a tile cache or a function. Needs 64-bit heap ids, which WGSL lacks (emulation is [I]).

**Classic papers.** Geometry clipmaps: nested grids per level centred on the viewer, updated as L-shaped strips, finer levels "predicted by upsampling" with synthesised residual, an update budget (stop when updated samples exceed n²), lagging levels cropped so the coarser one shows ([Losasso and Hoppe](https://hhoppe.com/geomclipmap.pdf)); spherical clipmaps for planets ([Clasen and Hege](https://malteclasen.de/zib/download/SphericalClipmaps_Electronic.pdf)). CDLOD: a quadtree selected by 3D distance, one fixed grid per node, vertices morph over the last 15 to 30% of each range, "the same LOD selection algorithm used for rendering is also used to ... stream" ([Strugar](https://github.com/fstrugar/CDLOD)). Chunked LOD: a quadtree of premade chunks with geomorphs and a long preprocessing phase ([Ulrich](http://www.tulrich.com/geekstuff/chunklod.html)); our data is a function, so no preprocessing is needed.

### 2.3 Vegetation and objects at every level

- **Nested density is standard.** Flight Simulator trees use Halton sampling, "Always uniform, whatever the chosen density. Density can be controlled at draw time" (Asobo PDF above). Horizon keeps a point of a threshold pattern when the density map passes its threshold, so lowering density removes a stable subset ([Guerrilla GDC 2017](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/media/News/Files/GDC2017_VanMuijden_GPUBasedProceduralPlacementInHorizonZeroDawn.pdf)); about 250 µs average GPU load, 100,000+ objects, 500+ asset types. Ghost of Tsushima: "Far LODs drop with stochastic max distance" ([streaming talk](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC+2021/ghost_streaming_gdc2021.pdf)). Our `unitHash(seed) < share` thinning in `winobjects.js` is the same property. [R]
- **Per-tile GPU placement.** Ghost grass: one compute pass per tile, a lane id maps to a jittered grid position, then cull, map lookup, drop empty, occlusion cull, indirect draw; about 1 million blades considered and 83,000 drawn in about 2.5 ms; 8 tile buffers, 4 compute while 4 render ([transcript](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/procedural-grass-in-ghost-of-tsushima)). Far Cry 5's last grass LOD is placed per frame by a compute pass from terrain data ([GDC 2018 terrain deck](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2018/presentations/TerrainRenderingFarCry5.pdf)). Unreal PCG runtime generation: hierarchical grids by object size (trees large, grass small), a generation radius per grid size that increases with grid size, `pcg.FrameTime` 16.667 ms, 16 cells at once, scheduling by distance and view direction, generate before the frustum so cells do not "suddenly appear"; PCG GPU instances "exist only at runtime in GPU memory" ([PCG modes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/using-pcg-generation-modes-in-unreal-engine), [PCG GPU](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/using-pcg-with-gpu-processing-in-unreal-engine)). The Witcher 4 runtime PCG talk (auto-captions, [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=icIFFlOyob4)): only grass is runtime, 2 ms CPU budget, about 71 µs GPU, 10 to 20 thousand instances per 32 m cell, and "generation latency" must beat (cull distance) over (camera speed) or cells pop.
- **Whole world, per frame, no storage.** AMD's work-graph sample generates terrain, grass, flowers and trees each frame in a World, Chunk, Tile, DetailedTile hierarchy, culling at every level, a 32 by 32 chunk cap, 79,710 instances in 3.74 ms on an RX 7900 XTX, sparse one-quad grass far and dense 49,152-triangle patches near ([GPUOpen](https://gpuopen.com/learn/work_graphs_mesh_nodes/work_graphs_mesh_nodes-procedural_generation)); 1,200 trees a frame from 51 KiB with continuous LOD ([Kuth et al. HPG 2025](https://gpuopen.com/download/Real-Time_GPU_Tree_Generation.pdf)). Work graphs are DX12 and Vulkan, not WebGPU.
- **Far away, trees are ground colour.** Flight Simulator: "Trees fade out naturally to the aerial", impostors "1 draw call per tree specie per tile", "1 vertex per tree" (Asobo PDF). Outerra bakes far trees into tile colour and draws a canopy envelope for grass ([grass post 2012/05](https://outerra.blogspot.com/2012/05/procedural-grass-rendering.html), relayed). Star Citizen's planet shader includes unloaded vegetation in distant colour. Fortnite uses 12 by 12 upper-hemisphere impostors with a 3-frame blend ([Impostor Baker](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/impostor-baker-plugin-in-unreal-engine)). Nanite foliage voxels are experimental and not portable ([docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/nanite-foliage)).
- **Contrast case:** Far Cry 5 vegetation is baked nightly in Houdini into point-cloud buffers on disk ([GDC 2018](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2018/presentations/ProceduralWorldGeneration.pdf)); that is the pattern we are leaving.
- **Gap in the sources:** no source found streams procedural trees per tile across a whole planet in a browser. Per-tile GPU placement with tier-nested density is ours to build from these pieces.

### 2.4 Browser and WebGPU

- **Browser renderers.** The three.js 3d-tiles-renderer ([README](https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS)) defaults: download queue 25, parse queue 5, 250 nodes processed per traversal "Lower numbers prevent frame hiccups", LRU 6,000 to 8,000 items and about 322 to 430 MB, and "Cache limits are hard caps... coarser tiles are displayed and refinement stops". MapLibre's `_updateRetainedTiles` retains loaded children for a tile with no data, else climbs to a loaded parent, `maxUnderzooming` 10 and `maxOverzooming` 3, 16 parallel image requests and 8 per frame ([tile_manager.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/main/src/tile/tile_manager.ts)). deck.gl `best-available` and centre-first scheduling ([docs](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/geo-layers/tile-layer)).
- **WebGPU planet-like projects.** `bevy_terrain`: GPU selection into one indirect draw, tile atlas with 1 px borders, author's own gaps "There is no prioritization or load balancing" and a panic when atlas slots run out ([implementation.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kurtkuehnert/bevy_terrain/main/docs/implementation.md)). `hello-terrain` (three.js WebGPU): cube-sphere with 2:1 balancing across face edges, skirt ring, min/max propagated after a GPU readback and a one-frame fallback. `webgpu-quadtree-compute-shader`: Dupuy-style GPU quadtree, indirect dispatch and draw, no CPU work. `nanite-webgpu` (Scthe): culling dominates; no streaming; hits the 128 MB storage binding and missing 64-bit atomics ([README](https://github.com/Scthe/nanite-webgpu)).
- **Platform.** Default `maxStorageBufferBindingSize` 128 MiB, `maxBufferSize` 256 MiB ([spec](https://gpuweb.github.io/gpuweb/)). `subgroups` shipped in Chrome 134. Multi-draw indirect is experimental behind a flag ([Chrome 131](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-131)); mesh shaders are not expected soon ([gpuweb #3015](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/issues/3015)); no work-graph proposal exists in the proposals index. `createComputePipelineAsync` avoids blocking GPU work ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GPUDevice/createComputePipelineAsync)). Chrome's watchdog constants are 15 s Linux, 25 s Mac, 30 s Windows from a 2019-era commit, so recheck; slice compute to a few ms regardless ([toji.dev device loss](https://toji.dev/webgpu-best-practices/device-loss)). `writeBuffer` is up to 2x faster since Chrome 144 ([blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-144)); OPFS with `createSyncAccessHandle` in workers ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/FileSystemSyncAccessHandle)). Put all indirect args in one buffer ([toji.dev](https://toji.dev/webgpu-best-practices/indirect-draws)).

## 3. Fit to this code

### 3.1 Two facts that make our problem easier than a perspective planet

1. **The camera is near-orthographic.** The ladder is a parallel projection and the planet camera narrows its field to 3 degrees at close zoom ([R] plan Unit 3, `planet-cam.js`). Ground sample spacing on screen is therefore about the same everywhere in a view, so one view needs mostly one tier, not a distance-based mix. The perspective machinery (CDLOD morphs, clipmap rings, distance ranges) is not needed at the ladder's levels. The problem is the web-map one: tiles of one level under the view, a parent as fallback, prefetch by pan and zoom. MapLibre's and deck.gl's loops are the nearest models. `planet.js tierFor` already picks the tier whose cells are about an art pixel and requests the two coarser as fallback. [R]
2. **A tier-5 tile is a window.** A tile is 256 cells of 75 m, which is 19.2 km, the window's size [R]. A tier-5 ground tile holds 17.7 M objects [R plan Unit 6]. So tier-5 tiles cannot be the unit for close-up work; the quadtree must go below 75 m.

### 3.2 Tiers below 75 m, produced not stored

The fine ground kernel (`fineKernel`) is a Catmull-Rom cubic through the 75 m heights at quarter steps (18.75 m), plus a ground swell at 960 m and 240 m, the stream bed cut by a mask, wandering cover edges [R plan Unit 6]. It is a pure function of the tile fields in a halo, and its noise is read "at the face's own metres" so a vertex is the same whichever window holds it [R `tile.ts`]. That is Proland's procedural residual producer exactly (upsample the parent, add a deterministic residual). The painter adds texture and per-pixel noise below 18.75 m (3 m art ground) [R].

So add procedural tiers: **tier 6 (37.5 m cells) and tier 7 (18.75 m cells)**, each tile 256 cells plus a halo. A tier-7 tile covers 4.8 km. Finer than tier 7 stays in the painter, as today. Cost per tier-7 tile: the window's fine kernel is 1,025 by 1,025 vertices in 0.2 s including a 33 MB readback [M]; a tier-7 tile is 257 by 257, about 1/16 of that, so about 12 ms of kernel before the readback and conversion are removed [I].

### 3.3 Producers and consumers

| producer | makes | from | exists |
|---|---|---|---|
| pyramid tiles, tiers 0 to 5 | 16 B-a-cell fields, heights with a halo | files | yes (`pyramid.js`) |
| ground tiles, tier 5 | the 26 planes the painter and scatter read | files, 6 MB | yes (`groundwin.js groundTile`) |
| fine tiles, tiers 6 and 7 | 10 fine layers, stream mask, 12.5 m light grid | ground tiles and `fineKernel`, `RASTER` | kernels exist, per window |
| object tiles, tier 7 (and 6) | instance buffer with nested share | ground tile and the scatter kernels | kernels exist, per tile |

Consumers: the planet layer (`wgsl/globe.js`), the ladder's painter (`ground.wgsl.js`), the object culler (`webgpu.js`). Proland's `getTile`/`putTile` ref counts, LRU of unused tiles and "in use" pins are the cache contract to copy; `pyramid.js` already has the LRU, the page table and the neighbour-halo refresh.

### 3.4 What changes in the readers

- **Painter.** It reads fixed windows: `g_fineAt(layer, x, z)` over a 1,021 by 1,021 lattice and `g_isleAt(L, i, j)` over 256 by 256 cells, in plane metres of one frame, plus streams binned over the island [R `ground.wgsl.js`]. Change the accessors, not the painting: map the pixel to (face, X, Y) once, look up the tile layer in a page table once, then every layer read is a `textureLoad` on the same layer of a texture array (all layers share the tile's index, so the extra cost is about one dependent load a pixel [I]). Tiles carry a 1-cell halo for bilinear reads (`pyramid.js` already does 1 for fields, 2 for heights). Binned streams become per-tile (the mask is per fine tile, about 1 MB at 4.7 m texels [I]).
- **Face edges.** The window code lays across-edge faces flat with a per-face turn (`groundwin.js turn`, `cellAcross`). The page table needs the same: an entry holds the atlas layer and the turn, built on the CPU for the tiles in view (as `pyramid.js neighbours` already walks `cellAcross`). A view-local indirection grid in the plane's frame is the cheaper form; the global table extends `tierOffset` to tier 7 in 524 KB (6 x (4^8 - 1) / 3 entries of 4 bytes) [I].
- **Frame.** Keep the page's re-anchored plane (`FRM`). With tiles in atlases, a re-anchor is a change of transform only, not a rebuild; the 78 ms swap frame goes away [I].
- **Culler.** It already has indexed sources with per-cell run starts and per-source far and front lists, dispatched per row of cells under the view [R `webgpu.js` lines 1149 to 1190, 1725 to 1750]. Make each resident object tile a source (or one pooled buffer with a tile table). Objects stand on ground read from `gridTex` and `fineTex`, single textures today; they read the tile atlas instead. A source count of 8 to 20 is within what `setSources` handles, but each new spec size makes pipelines (`CAPS`), so use two or three fixed capacities and warm them at load.
- **Sim.** It stays in the island's frame, as now. The island remains "home" ground until Unit 6's cutover cuts the play area from tiles; until then home and tile ground coexist with the existing 1.2 km fade at the edge [R].

### 3.5 Object tiles, nested by level

Keep the scatter's draws unchanged (counter-based, ids from the tile's run). Per object tile, a compute pass writes only that tile's rectangle (`scatterTile`'s `window` rect already does this for 4.8 km-sized rectangles [R]) and writes the instance buffer directly, with no readback, thinned by `unitHash(seed) < share(level)`. Rules:

- **Share by level** (Horizon threshold, Asobo Halton prefix, Ghost stochastic distance): near tiles at full share, wider levels at 1/2, 1/4, 1/8... Raising share only adds objects, so a parent's set is a subset of the child's and a tile replaces its parent's objects in place without a visible change in the objects that were there.
- **Instances stop where sprites stop mattering** (Bruneton's 3 px, Asobo's imposters, Star Citizen's distant colour): at ladder levels where a tree is under about 2 art pixels, the planet layer's cover and the painted ground carry the forest and no object tile is made. The planet layer already draws cover, soil and rivers from the pyramid at every zoom [R].
- **Ids.** The scatter's count pass over a whole tier-5 tile gives ids; keep that pass once per ground tile and keep its output (`idx`) while the tile is held. Without that every object tile would re-run it. If ids need not be dense away from the island (there is no sim away from it), a stable (quad, k) pair would drop the count pass [I]; check what picks and the `pick` slot need.
- **Budget** (PCG 16.667 ms; Cesium 5 ms; Witcher 4 2 ms; Activision 4 to 20 pages a frame): cap GPU work per frame by slicing dispatches to a few ms and pacing with `queue.onSubmittedWorkDone`; priority = distance from the view centre, view direction, then reverse level error; drop work for tiles that left the view.
- **Memory.** At 28 B an instance, a tier-7 tile at full share is 1.1 M objects, 31 MB [I from 17.7 M / 16]. Only the nearest one or two tiles are at full share; the rest at a fraction, so a view holds on the order of 60 to 100 MB of objects. A single binding is capped at 128 MiB by default (4.4 M objects at 28 B), which a tile does not reach.

### 3.6 Scheduler

One scheduler for every producer, copied from Cesium and Activision: a priority queue re-sorted each frame, one small global in-flight cap, a per-frame ms slice, drop-on-bump, requests dropped when the tile leaves the view, `fetch` with `priority: "low"` for prefetch. Prediction: the page knows the zoom goal (`camera.goalView`) and the pan; ask for the tiles of the goal's tier under the goal's view, as Cesium's `preloadFlightDestinations` and World Partition's destination source do. Cache: byte-capped, LRU, refinement stops (or the error target rises) when full, a refusal counter in the stats (3d-tiles-renderer). OPFS for fetched tiles in a worker.

### 3.7 What is kept, replaced, deleted

- **Keep:** `pyramid.js` (cache, page table, halo refresh), `planet.js wanted/tierFor`, `groundwin.js groundTile/decode/placer` logic, `tile.ts` kernels (`fineKernel`, `RASTER`, the scatter's table, count, write), `ground.wgsl.js` painting functions, the culler's indexed sources and far and front lists, the pipeline cache (`pipelineSoon`).
- **Replace:** `groundwin.js build()` (one window) with per-tile producers; `winobjects.js` collector and `lay`; `packGround`'s CPU loops with GPU kernels that write tile layers; `applyGround` and `gid` (ground switching) with tile residency; the page's `wantGround` with the scheduler.
- **Delete at the end:** the window, the `top` thinning cap as a global rule, the 5-second re-ask, `built`.

## 4. Build order, and what the operator sees at each step

0. **Compile early** (the previous plan's step 1; needed by any design). Kernels the tile producers use are `pipelineSoon`'d at load. First zoom is no slower than the second.
1. **Fine tiles.** A producer for tier 7 (and 6) writing straight into an atlas, no readback. The painter reads the atlas through the page table, still inside the existing window for objects. Gate: painter GPU ms and `ground-parity.ts` unchanged, same pixels. Operator sees: no change yet except faster ground builds; this step is the shader-cost gate. If the extra loads cost too much, fall back to filling a toroidal clipmap of the painter's inputs from the tiles (Losasso and Hoppe), which leaves the painter's reads as they are.
2. **Ground-first and scheduler.** The ladder draws tile ground the moment tier-7 tiles for the view exist (tens of ms each [I]); the planet layer's tiers are the fallback until then, so no frame is empty. Trees still arrive from the window. Operator sees: close ground within a fraction of a second of arrival at a place the page predicted; tiles refine in place.
3. **Object tiles on the GPU, nested.** Per-tile instance passes, no readback, nearest first, fade in per tile; the window's scatter is retired. Operator sees: trees appear nearest the camera first within a few hundred ms [I, to measure], density filling in.
4. **Wide levels.** Share by level and the cut-over to ground colour for levels where trees are tiny; far and front lists per tile source. Operator sees: continuous zoom from the planet to the ground with no window edge or rebuild at any pan.
5. **Cutover.** Remove the window, `gid`, `built`; cut the play area's Ground from the tiles (plan Unit 6). Persistent tile cache in OPFS.

The previous plan's step 3 (publish ground first) is step 2 here; its step 4 (chunked scatter) is step 3 here with tiles of the pyramid instead of fixed chunks; its step 5 (ground on GPU) is step 1 here.

## 5. Risks

- **Painter cost.** The painter is the page's largest shader; an indirection per pixel and halos can cost frame time. Frame cost is 9 to 17 ms GPU with the ladder already [M plan Unit 3]. Gate in step 1; fallback is the toroidal fill.
- **Same pixels.** The window uses one `scale` number for its cells; tiles use the grid's own mapping, so ground can shift by the grid's distortion across a view. Compare against `ground-parity.ts` and a window's stills before deleting the window. The fine kernel's tile-independence is a given [R].
- **Face edges and corners.** Across-edge turns and cube corners (no cell stands there) need the table entry's turn and a null entry; the window code already handles both [R].
- **Ids and determinism** of objects (plan: the same objects every visit): keep the scatter's draws untouched; test a tile's objects against the window's by id before retiring it.
- **WebGPU limits.** 128 MiB binding per source; no 64-bit atomics; no multi-draw; compile stalls (first use of each capacity); the 15 to 30 s watchdog; Metal timestamps unreliable (napkin), judge by frame times.
- **Memory.** Atlas of ground tiles about 4.4 MB each for the 15 isle layers at f32 [I] times 16 to 24 tiles, fine tiles about 2.6 MB plus 1 MB mask [I] times 24 to 32, objects 60 to 100 MB. Budget about 300 MB of GPU memory; measure.
- **Isometric ladder.** Two ladder levels are drawn during a cross-fade, so two tiers must be resident at once; the ladder's "nearest level first" ask (`frontAsk`) becomes the scheduler's priority.
- **Sim in one area.** Unchanged until the cutover; the edge fade between home and tile ground stays.
- **Prefetch waste.** Speculative tiles cost GPU time; one speculative producer at a time, none during a switch (napkin 2026-10-22: a build per pan step helped crash the GPU).
