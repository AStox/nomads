# The island's erosion law as the fine tiers' solve (tiers 4 and 5 of the 391.1 km planet, key 7f6ff6c82cdd)

Chain: `2026-10-22-planet-stream-tiers.md` (the streamed chain this extends), `2026-10-21-planet-75m-impl.md` Unit 5b and 7b (the landforms judged not met). Region runs: `scripts/globe-land-region.ts` on `src/gpu/globe/land.ts` (one patch of the same kernels, the cells round it held at the bake's heights); the island drawn and measured the same way by `scripts/globe-land-island.ts`; measures in `scripts/globe-land-metrics.ts`. Everything below was measured on a range (face 0, tiles 19 to 30 by 12 to 23, 12 by 12 tiles) and a coast (face 1, tiles 1 to 12 by 2 to 13), and on an 8 by 8 tile range region (face 0, 23 to 30 by 13 to 20) for the sweeps. No rebake was started; `/tmp/planet-75m/1-7f6ff6c82cdd` was only read.

## Why the island dissects its ground and the tier runs did not (measured)

The island (seed 1): response time tau 260 My (the GPU's clock; 219 My by `responseYears` on its network), step 3 tau / 60 = 13 My, 60 steps, 780 My, uplift 0.95 m/My uniform, 47 creep substeps a step. A cell of the island's headwaters has K dt sqrt(A) / d = 0.9 at one step: a channel of 100 cells' area pulls a cell to within a tenth of its receiver's height each step. It dissects in 60 steps because **each step is as long as the response time allows**, not because it is run for long.

The tier runs did not, for three reasons found by experiment on the 8 by 8 tile range region:

1. **The discharge was weighted by the planet's rain.** The median tier-0 cell of the range region rains 26 mm a year and 10% rain nothing (p90 1,337 mm); area weight is rain over 1,000 mm, and the stream power step is K sqrt(A). The response time of the region's network with the planet's rain is **15,140 My** (the dry cells' paths are almost endless); with one rain, as the island has (terrain-upstream.ts: "one rain falls over the whole island"), **399 My**, the island's. Half the range's ground could not incise at all. All tiers now take one rain (`tierTable(..., uniformRain)`; see the adoption below).
2. **The step.** 1 My steps for 12 steps is 12 My, a thirtieth of tau. But steps as long as the island's (13 My and over) do not give the island's landform either, because the planet's uplift is the whole of what a step takes off (below) and the coupled laws (incision, slides, creep, uplift) are split per step: statistics of the surface against step length at 260 My in all: slope 50th percentile 0.33 (13 My), 0.38 (6.5), 0.39 (3.25); share of cells that are river 0.1025, 0.0895, 0.0851; 32-cell relief 936, 991, 993 m; at 52 My 0.23, 0.21, 737 m (a smeared surface) and at 130 My blocky lakes. The step must fall to 3 to 6 My for the statistics to settle within 5%; total time then matters little (6.5 My for 40 steps and for 120 gave 991 and 1,005 m of relief).
3. **The uplift made seams.** The old lift (a smooth rise, a second pass, then an even share per tier-0 cell) left a constant to each 32-cell block that the loss of a big step turned into steps: the mean height step across a block edge over the step inside it (`blockStep`) went 1.29 at the start to 2.0 and 2.6 at 13 My steps, and rivers ran along the seams. See below for the fix.

The implied uplift is the other thing the runs showed: holding every tier-0 cell's mean while the island's law erodes the planet's relief takes a mean loss, and so a uniform-in-time uplift, of **about 25 m/My (25 mm/yr)** over the range region and it does not decay over 780 My. The island's is 0.95 m/My. The planet's ranges are 5 km of relief over 100 km of slope with K = 7e-8 [cited R523], so rivers cut them at tens of mm/yr; Earth's fastest ranges rise 5 to 10 mm/yr. Nothing was retuned; this is a gap in the planet (its relief is steeper than its rock and river law can hold at Earth's uplifts) and an operator question if the ranges should stand at Earth's rates.

## What was built

- **The island's law as the fine tiers' solve** (`TIER_LAWS` in `tiers.ts`): one rain; eight-neighbour flood and receivers (the island's `flround`/`recv` read 8 neighbours; the old comment in `globe-regional.ts` saying the island reads 4 was wrong); the implicit stream power step, threshold slides and 9-point creep unchanged; the step lengths **26 x 4, 13 x 6, 6.5 x 10, 3.25 x 4, 1.6 x 4 My at tier 4 (28 steps, 266 My)** and **6.5 x 2, 3.25 x 2, 1.6 x 2 at tier 5 (6 steps)**. The tier-4 solve does the work at a quarter of the cost of tier 5; tier 5 then carries it (`refineBlock`) and settles. Region test: tier 4 (28 steps) then tier 5 (10, 6 or 4 steps) gave the same statistics as 28 steps at tier 5 alone (relief 1,037, 1,032, 1,031 against 1,044 m; river share 0.0834, 0.0843, 0.0847 against 0.0836). Fewer tier-4 steps do not: 14 steps gave relief 1,010 and 64-cell straight runs 0.124 against 0.073, 8 steps 956 and 0.237.
- **An exact smooth uplift** (`pstencil`, `psolve`, `lift` in `wgsl/globe-regional.ts`): the rise of a land cell is the bilinear interpolation of one coefficient per tier-0 cell (no constant to a block); the coefficients solve, by 64 Jacobi sweeps on the tier-0 grid (never touching the cells), that the land of every tier-0 cell rises by exactly what it lost (the stencil is made from the cells' own interpolation weights, one pass). A coefficient may be negative: a positive-only solve left cells beside a great loss over-lifted by up to 449 m, growing each step (the lift spreads a hot cell's rise into its neighbours). Result on the range region: each tier-0 cell's mean height against the planet's, **p99 0.01 m, max 8 m** (cells held at one unit), `blockStep` at 32, 16, 8 and 4 cells **0.92, 0.95, 0.99, 1.00** (the island's own: 1.046, 1.022, 1.008, 1.001). The old `pres`, `pfin`, `spread` are gone. Shore cells' median height after the run is 20 m against 15 m at the start (the lift is one step's loss, which the short last steps make small), so no cliff is lifted from the sea.
- **The lattice.** The island routes by steepest descent over 8 neighbours too, with only a 1e-6 m dither (one unit of its own Z, below any slope) to break ties, and **it locks to the axes as much as D8 does**: on its own river cells (area of 30 cells or more) 71% have an axial receiver and 53% lie in an axial straight run of 8 cells or more (38% of 16 and 21% of 32); nothing in its erosion breaks that (its joint valleys at 20 and 110 degrees, dykes and layered K come after the erosion or are the rocks' own, and the planet's ranges are granite and metasediment of one K). The region with D8 steepest descent gave 61 to 70% axial straight runs of 8 cells, 37% of 32, and on smooth ramps a comb of valleys 25 to 50 cells apart parallel to a lattice direction, with rectangular and diagonal domains (`/tmp/planet-75m/land/view/coast-full.png`, `range-tri.png`). Part of that was inherited: the start surface (tiers 1 to 3 routed by 4 neighbours) already had rows and columns of valleys and a tartan of domains (`coastStart-full.png`). Two changes remove it, both Fairfield and Leymarie's remedy for the grid bias of steepest descent (the receiver is drawn among the lower neighbours with the probability proportional to the fall over the distance; Tarboton 1997, read: "randomly assigning a flow direction to one of the downslope neighbors, with the probability proportional to slope", and that D8 "results in streaks aligned with the grid"): `recv` with P[3] set draws a Philox word of the cell's own id and the step number, so it is the same for the same cell whatever the tiling and each step draws anew; every lower neighbour is a valid receiver, so the forest stays a forest. It is applied to **tiers 1 to 3 as well** (eight neighbours, drawn receivers), because their 4-neighbour steepest descent made the lattice the fine tiers inherit; the coarse tiers' steps, rain and rock are unchanged (and cost nothing more: 81 s in memory). Measured on the coast region: axial straight runs of 8 cells or more **0.013** of river cells (island 0.53, D8 steepest 0.59), 32 cells 0 (island 0.21), axial share 0.51 (isotropic). The settled flow of the final heights (the `to`, `acc`, `flooded` files the water stages read) is the steepest descent, as before.
- **No real field at 75 m.** The planet has nothing finer than a tier-0 cell (2.4 km) to seed a river's wandering: its rock and rain are tier-0 fields, interpolated; the island's own seeding is a dither far below any slope and joint valleys cut after the erosion by the ice stage (not built on the planet: a gap in `2026-10-21-planet-75m-impl.md`). The drawn receiver is therefore the island's equivalent only in effect; it is a published method, not noise on the heights, and it needs no field.
- **Streamed** (`tiers-stream.ts`, `regional-patch.ts`): eight neighbours (directions 0 to 7; with them several cells of a block's edge drain into one cell of the halo, so the area pass roots the tree at those exit cells: kernel `todexit`), the step's number, route and table armed at the start of every call and step (`arm`), the table per step (`tableAt`), and the lift as the stages `stencil` then `lift` (two visits of the tier, where the old one had three) with the solve on the tier-0 tables between them. Tier-0 tables kept between calls grew to 15 words a tier-0 cell (24 MB).

## Exactness (tests)

`tiers-stream.test.ts`: the old cases (the steepest of eight neighbours, one step length) equal the in-memory solve word for word in blocks of 1, 2 and 4 tiles and in another order (visits per step: init, flood, recv, area1, area2, incise, slide, creep, stencil, lift); the new case, **the island's law (eight neighbours, drawn receivers, steps of 26, 6.5 and 1.6 My, one rain)**, equals the in-memory solve in blocks of 1 tile, of 2 tiles in reverse order and of 2 tiles in calls of 0.6 s (heights, carries, receivers, discharge area, flooded surface). The in-memory region solver (`land.ts`) is the same kernels in one patch and was run twice to the same bytes.

## Follow-up (2026-10-22, second pass)

**Tiers 1 to 3 under the island's law too** (one rain, the exact lift, the tier-4 schedule 26x4, 13x6, 6.5x10, 3.25x4, 1.6x4; `scripts/globe-tiers.ts --coarse-dt <schedule>`, tiers made in `/tmp/planet-75m/landI`, 24 s of tier 3 plus 143 s): range and coast regions through tiers 4 and 5 as before (`rangeI-full.png`, `coastI-full.png`) against the current ones (`rangeT-full.png`, `coastT-full.png`). Result: **the foothills' E-W terraces do not go** (relief 797 against 803 m, axial runs of 8 cells 0.014 against 0.013, same pictures to the eye), so they are the planet's own tier-0 structure, not the coarse law; not adopted in `TIER_LAWS` (the criterion was that they go). What does change: the tier-0 means. With the planet's rain at tiers 1 to 3 the 12 by 12 range region has one tier-0 cell 260 m off the planet's; with the island's law at tiers 1 to 3 the same region's worst cell is 0.95 m off (coast 1.89 m against 1.3). One flag (`COARSE` in `tiers.ts`: `uniformRain: true`, `dtMy` the tier-4 schedule) adopts it; it makes tier 3 a landscape dissected at 300 m, the planet's climate no longer shaping the coarse surface. The response time of tiers 1 to 3 with the planet's rain against one rain was not measured separately: the mean-error result is the evidence that the rain starvation reaches them. Coarse-law streamed against in-memory equality: the island-law test (`tiers-stream.test.ts`) runs the same kernels the coarse tiers use (eight neighbours, drawn receivers); tier 2 only.

**The 260 m cell, precisely.** It is two tier-0 cells (face 0 tier-0 cells 225,125 and 225,126; mean height 0.4 m, 57 to 65% land, a coast beside a high-loss cell) and a cluster round them (102, 79, 67 m). It starts at tier 4 (12 m after the first step, 260 m after 28) and tier 5 only inherits it, because the carry is not passed between tiers (tier 5 starts with an empty carry: its own run shows 0.01 m). The tier-0 solve is exact and converged (the carry the run keeps is the error: -11.7 m a cell after one step, in the tier-4 file). The cause is the floor: the smooth rise of the high-loss neighbour spills into these cells; the signed coefficient that would take it back lowers land that stands a few metres to tens of metres above the sea, and a land cell is never lowered under one unit, so the debt cannot be paid and it grows while the neighbour keeps losing. Not the Jacobi sweeps (the solve is exact when the cells can follow), not negative coefficients as such, not the held ring, not an all-sea cell. Not fixed: any fix either breaks the smooth field or lets a coast sink under the sea. It is gone when the tier-0 relief is less steep (the island law at tiers 1 to 3, above).

**The implied uplift** (an explicit gap). Holding every tier-0 cell's mean while the island's law erodes the planet's relief takes a mean loss of about 25 m/My = **25 mm/yr** over the 8 by 8 tile range region (14 to 26 m/My at the 13 My steps, constant over the 780 My run), against the island's 0.95 m/My and Earth's fastest ranges at 5 to 10 mm/yr. At Earth's K (7e-8, R523) and slopes of 0.05 to 0.6 over catchments of 100 km2, the stream power law erodes at tens of m/My per 0.01 of slope: the planet's coarse relief (5 km over 100 km) is **higher than the steady state the island's law would hold at Earth's K and uplift**, so the fine tiers' erosion takes the coarse relief as given and dissects it, held up by an uplift 2 to 5 times Earth's fastest. The planet's ranges are young and steep by construction (Unit 4c); the fine-tier landforms are those of a range under that uplift. If the ranges are to stand at Earth's rates the relief has to be lowered at the coarse tiers (tectonics), not here.

**Discharge overflow found at tier 5 and fixed.** At the reference rain every land cell weighs 512 and the planet's largest basin at 75 m is over 14 million cells: the exact 32-bit sum overflowed (`a basin's discharge area passed 32 bits`, first streamed step). One rain is now a quarter of the reference rain at twice K (`UNIFORM_RAIN` in `regional.ts`): K sqrt(A) is unchanged and a cell weighs 128, so a basin of 33 million cells fits.

**Cost of the tier 4 and 5 solve** (one tier-5 streamed step measured on a real block set: tier-4 heights carried from tier 3, 384 blocks of 1,024, the first step of the schedule, 6.5 My, one call of 275 s, peak process 1.96 GB, 28 GB read and 21 GB written in the call): start 35 s once; per step init 5, flood 38, recv 6, area 28, incise 119 (6.2 visits a block: eight neighbours make the waves longer), slide 31 s = 227 s, plus creep 5 s a pass (31 passes at 6.5 My, 16 at 3.25, 8 at 1.6: 155, 80 and 40 s), stencil and lift about 8 s. Tier 5: 2 x 395 + 2 x 315 + 2 x 275 s = about 33 min, plus the start and the settled flow with its four files (about 3 min): **about 37 min in 10 calls of 230 s**. Tier 4 (a quarter of the cells; scaled, not measured): 28 steps about 36 min. Tiers 1 to 3 streamed with the tier-1 to 3 law (40 steps of 1 My): about 8 min (tier 3 340 s, tier 2 95 s as measured before). **About 85 min of GPU in about 25 calls for tiers 1 to 5.**

**Adopted (third pass).** `TIER_LAWS` now has tiers 1 to 5 under the island's law (one rain, eight neighbours with drawn receivers, the exact lift); tiers 1 to 4 take the 28-step schedule (26x4, 13x6, 6.5x10, 3.25x4, 1.6x4 My), tier 5 the 6-step one. The old coarse law (40 steps of 1 My, the planet's rain) is gone from the code. Reason: the tier-0 means (worst cell 0.95 m against 260 m) and the same pictures; the E-W terraces are the planet's. Equality of the two chains on tiers 1 to 3 (the real planet, in memory by `scripts/globe-tiers.ts --last 3` and streamed by `scripts/globe-tiers-stream.ts --last 3`, files compared by hash with `--check`): **equal word for word**: t1-height f511cf72ecb0f9e2, t2-height abd8cf2f105d316d, t3-height 982d90fbda2f4d26, t3-to 40aaf0653b69f2a9, t3-acc e58a5d507c918657, t3-flooded c98666bbf463d7e5 (first 16 hex of sha256, both ways); in memory 3 calls (tier 3 steps of 5 s), streamed 4 calls of 210 s (about 12 min). `tiers.test.ts` now asks of the staged tier 2 that the 99th percentile tier-0 mean be within a metre (0.004 m) and the worst within 40 m (19.7 m: the floor effect above, on a ridge's foot over a 1,500 m sea), where it asked 0.25 m of the 1 My steps.

## Fourth pass: defects measured on the live bake's tile 574 and 1253

Real tier 5 read from `/tmp/planet-75m/1-7f6ff6c82cdd/t5-height.i32` (`scripts/globe-land-real.ts`: second difference by the cell's place in the 32-cell block, flat-block share, diagonal measure; same renderer as the region pictures). The region runner reproduces the live tile (rms 280 m between the two on tile 1253 since valleys differ, same statistics), so the experiments ran on regions (face 0 tiles 19 to 30 by 12 to 23).

- **Blocks of one tier-0 cell on the range (tile 574): the lift's bilinear weights.** The mean absolute second difference by x mod 32 on the live tile is 2.6 m everywhere except **6.2 and 6.4 m at 15 and 16**, the two cells beside a tier-0 centre (region: 5.3 and 5.4 against 2.5 to 3.5); the start surface (the carry) has no such crease. The rise a step gives a cell was bilinear in the tier-0 coefficients: C0, with a crease along every tier-0 centre line, and a step's rise is hundreds of metres, so the crease is large; threshold slides then leave planar facets either side. Fix (`lift_at`, `wgsl/globe-regional.ts`): the quadratic B-spline weights over the nine tier-0 cells round a cell, the limit of the 3/4, 1/4 stencil the heights are carried down by (Chaikin's corner cutting), so the rise is C1 like the surface it adds to; the exact solve keeps its nine slots, with a damped Jacobi sweep (the plain one has a factor of -1.25 on the smooth modes with this stencil; two thirds of a step gives -0.5 to 0.83) and 128 sweeps. Region (tiers 4 and 5 rerun from tier 3, same seed): the crease is gone (1.5 m at 15 and 16, flat across the block, against 2.5), the dark squares on the tile's top right are gone in the picture (`rangeI5-574.png` before, `rangeJ5-574.png` after), tier-0 means unchanged (p99 0.08 m, max 0.79 m against 0.95), relief 802 against 797 m, river share the same. It changes the lift of every tier, so **tiers 1 to 5 are rebaked from tier 1** (the crease is made at every tier and carried down; tiers 4 and 5 alone would leave the coarse creases, 150 m apart in the carry).
- **45 degree rays** (visible in both pictures, radiating from points on the ranges): not fixed. The threshold slides read eight neighbours at the orthogonal and diagonal distances, whose envelope is an octagonal cone; the island's own slides do the same. A 16-neighbour slide (knight moves, halo 2 in the streamed layout) would remove it; not built.
- **Terraces on the coast foothills (tile 1253)** and **the shore's smooth ramp**: not investigated to a source. The live tile shows slope undulations of period about 7 cells (3 m per cell on a 40 m per cell fall) along the fall line of valley walls, which the region run of the same law does not reproduce (its valleys differ), and the shore is the planet's own shelf profile carried down (tier 0 sea floor, no process at 75 m). Open.

## Gaps and operator questions

- Implied tectonic uplift 25 mm/yr in the range region against Earth's fastest 5 to 10 (above).
- Rain: the fine tiers erode under one rain (the island's), not the planet's climate (a gap the island has too); with the planet's, half the range cannot incise.
- Remaining parallel comb valleys on smooth ramps at the Smith-Bretherton spacing, and the tier-1-to-3 terraces; the ice's joint valleys (the island's way of making oblique lines) are not built on the planet.
- Tiers 1 to 3 are now 8-neighbour with drawn receivers: it changes tier 3 and so everything downstream (the whole bake is rerun by the rebake anyway); set `COARSE.route` in `tiers.ts` back to `{ nflow: 4, stochastic: false }` to keep them.
- On the 12 by 12 range region two coastal tier-0 cells are 260 and 251 m off the planet's mean (p99 0.08 m over 2,240 cells): the floor under a coast beside a high-loss cell (follow-up above); the 8 by 8 range and the coast regions are within 8 m and 1.3 m.
- The lift's coefficients may be negative: a smooth dip beside a tier-0 cell of great loss (not seen as an artifact in the pictures).
- `TIER_LAWS` steps and `streamTier` at tier 5 are not run on the planet; `regional.test.ts` (its four-neighbour cases pinned to the four-neighbour law; the step hash re-recorded deliberately, the lift changed) and `tiers.test.ts` pass.

## Napkin note (for the runbook, not edited here)

**[2026-10-22] Before tuning a law that "does not dissect", measure its response time on the region: a weight field (rain) that is zero over half the ground makes it 40 times the island's, and a step longer than the coupled laws can split makes a peneplain.** Do: run a region of the real tier through the same kernels in one patch (`land.ts`), sweep step length at equal total time, compare statistics (slope, river share, relief, straight-run share) and not pictures cell by cell, and look at the start surface before blaming the law: the tartan was in tier 3, made by 4-neighbour routing two tiers up.
