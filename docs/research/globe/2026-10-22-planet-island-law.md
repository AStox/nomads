# The island's erosion law as the fine tiers' solve (tiers 4 and 5 of the 391.1 km planet, key 7f6ff6c82cdd)

Chain: `2026-10-22-planet-stream-tiers.md` (the streamed chain this extends), `2026-10-21-planet-75m-impl.md` Unit 5b and 7b (the landforms judged not met). Region runs: `scripts/globe-land-region.ts` on `src/gpu/globe/land.ts` (one patch of the same kernels, the cells round it held at the bake's heights); the island drawn and measured the same way by `scripts/globe-land-island.ts`; measures in `scripts/globe-land-metrics.ts`. Everything below was measured on a range (face 0, tiles 19 to 30 by 12 to 23, 12 by 12 tiles) and a coast (face 1, tiles 1 to 12 by 2 to 13), and on an 8 by 8 tile range region (face 0, 23 to 30 by 13 to 20) for the sweeps. No rebake was started; `/tmp/planet-75m/1-7f6ff6c82cdd` was only read.

## Why the island dissects its ground and the tier runs did not (measured)

The island (seed 1): response time tau 260 My (the GPU's clock; 219 My by `responseYears` on its network), step 3 tau / 60 = 13 My, 60 steps, 780 My, uplift 0.95 m/My uniform, 47 creep substeps a step. A cell of the island's headwaters has K dt sqrt(A) / d = 0.9 at one step: a channel of 100 cells' area pulls a cell to within a tenth of its receiver's height each step. It dissects in 60 steps because **each step is as long as the response time allows**, not because it is run for long.

The tier runs did not, for three reasons found by experiment on the 8 by 8 tile range region:

1. **The discharge was weighted by the planet's rain.** The median tier-0 cell of the range region rains 26 mm a year and 10% rain nothing (p90 1,337 mm); area weight is rain over 1,000 mm, and the stream power step is K sqrt(A). The response time of the region's network with the planet's rain is **15,140 My** (the dry cells' paths are almost endless); with one rain, as the island has (terrain-upstream.ts: "one rain falls over the whole island"), **399 My**, the island's. Half the range's ground could not incise at all. The fine tiers now take one rain (`tierTable(..., uniformRain)`); tiers 1 to 3 keep the planet's.
2. **The step.** 1 My steps for 12 steps is 12 My, a thirtieth of tau. But steps as long as the island's (13 My and over) do not give the island's landform either, because the planet's uplift is the whole of what a step takes off (below) and the coupled laws (incision, slides, creep, uplift) are split per step: statistics of the surface against step length at 260 My in all: slope 50th percentile 0.33 (13 My), 0.38 (6.5), 0.39 (3.25); share of cells that are river 0.1025, 0.0895, 0.0851; 32-cell relief 936, 991, 993 m; at 52 My 0.23, 0.21, 737 m (a smeared surface) and at 130 My blocky lakes. The step must fall to 3 to 6 My for the statistics to settle within 5%; total time then matters little (6.5 My for 40 steps and for 120 gave 991 and 1,005 m of relief).
3. **The uplift made seams.** The old lift (a smooth rise, a second pass, then an even share per tier-0 cell) left a constant to each 32-cell block that the loss of a big step turned into steps: the mean height step across a block edge over the step inside it (`blockStep`) went 1.29 at the start to 2.0 and 2.6 at 13 My steps, and rivers ran along the seams. See below for the fix.

The implied uplift is the other thing the runs showed: holding every tier-0 cell's mean while the island's law erodes the planet's relief takes a mean loss, and so a uniform-in-time uplift, of **about 25 m/My (25 mm/yr)** over the range region and it does not decay over 780 My. The island's is 0.95 m/My. The planet's ranges are 5 km of relief over 100 km of slope with K = 7e-8 [cited R523], so rivers cut them at tens of mm/yr; Earth's fastest ranges rise 5 to 10 mm/yr. Nothing was retuned; this is a gap in the planet (its relief is steeper than its rock and river law can hold at Earth's uplifts) and an operator question if the ranges should stand at Earth's rates.

## What was built

- **The island's law as the fine tiers' solve** (`TIER_LAWS` in `tiers.ts`): one rain; eight-neighbour flood and receivers (the island's `flround`/`recv` read 8 neighbours; the old comment in `globe-regional.ts` saying the island reads 4 was wrong); the implicit stream power step, threshold slides and 9-point creep unchanged; the step lengths **26 x 4, 13 x 6, 6.5 x 10, 3.25 x 4, 1.6 x 4 My at tier 4 (28 steps, 266 My)** and **6.5 x 2, 3.25 x 2, 1.6 x 2 at tier 5 (6 steps)**. The tier-4 solve does the work at a quarter of the cost of tier 5; tier 5 then carries it (`refineBlock`) and settles. Region test: tier 4 (28 steps) then tier 5 (10, 6 or 4 steps) gave the same statistics as 28 steps at tier 5 alone (relief 1,037, 1,032, 1,031 against 1,044 m; river share 0.0834, 0.0843, 0.0847 against 0.0836). Fewer tier-4 steps do not: 14 steps gave relief 1,010 and 64-cell straight runs 0.124 against 0.073, 8 steps 956 and 0.237.
- **An exact smooth uplift** (`pstencil`, `psolve`, `lift` in `wgsl/globe-regional.ts`): the rise of a land cell is the bilinear interpolation of one coefficient per tier-0 cell (no constant to a block); the coefficients solve, by 64 Jacobi sweeps on the tier-0 grid (never touching the cells), that the land of every tier-0 cell rises by exactly what it lost (the stencil is made from the cells' own interpolation weights, one pass). A coefficient may be negative: a positive-only solve left cells beside a great loss over-lifted by up to 449 m, growing each step (the lift spreads a hot cell's rise into its neighbours). Result on the range region: each tier-0 cell's mean height against the planet's, **p99 0.01 m, max 8 m** (cells held at one unit), `blockStep` at 32, 16, 8 and 4 cells **0.92, 0.95, 0.99, 1.00** (the island's own: 1.046, 1.022, 1.008, 1.001). The old `pres`, `pfin`, `spread` are gone. Shore cells' median height after the run is 20 m against 15 m at the start (the lift is one step's loss, which the short last steps make small), so no cliff is lifted from the sea.
- **The lattice.** The island routes by steepest descent over 8 neighbours too, with only a 1e-6 m dither (one unit of its own Z, below any slope) to break ties, and **it locks to the axes as much as D8 does**: on its own river cells (area of 30 cells or more) 71% have an axial receiver and 53% lie in an axial straight run of 8 cells or more (38% of 16 and 21% of 32); nothing in its erosion breaks that (its joint valleys at 20 and 110 degrees, dykes and layered K come after the erosion or are the rocks' own, and the planet's ranges are granite and metasediment of one K). The region with D8 steepest descent gave 61 to 70% axial straight runs of 8 cells, 37% of 32, and on smooth ramps a comb of valleys 25 to 50 cells apart parallel to a lattice direction, with rectangular and diagonal domains (`/tmp/planet-75m/land/view/coast-full.png`, `range-tri.png`). Part of that was inherited: the start surface (tiers 1 to 3 routed by 4 neighbours) already had rows and columns of valleys and a tartan of domains (`coastStart-full.png`). Two changes remove it, both Fairfield and Leymarie's remedy for the grid bias of steepest descent (the receiver is drawn among the lower neighbours with the probability proportional to the fall over the distance; Tarboton 1997, read: "randomly assigning a flow direction to one of the downslope neighbors, with the probability proportional to slope", and that D8 "results in streaks aligned with the grid"): `recv` with P[3] set draws a Philox word of the cell's own id and the step number, so it is the same for the same cell whatever the tiling and each step draws anew; every lower neighbour is a valid receiver, so the forest stays a forest. It is applied to **tiers 1 to 3 as well** (eight neighbours, drawn receivers), because their 4-neighbour steepest descent made the lattice the fine tiers inherit; the coarse tiers' steps, rain and rock are unchanged (and cost nothing more: 81 s in memory). Measured on the coast region: axial straight runs of 8 cells or more **0.013** of river cells (island 0.53, D8 steepest 0.59), 32 cells 0 (island 0.21), axial share 0.51 (isotropic). The settled flow of the final heights (the `to`, `acc`, `flooded` files the water stages read) is the steepest descent, as before.
- **No real field at 75 m.** The planet has nothing finer than a tier-0 cell (2.4 km) to seed a river's wandering: its rock and rain are tier-0 fields, interpolated; the island's own seeding is a dither far below any slope and joint valleys cut after the erosion by the ice stage (not built on the planet: a gap in `2026-10-21-planet-75m-impl.md`). The drawn receiver is therefore the island's equivalent only in effect; it is a published method, not noise on the heights, and it needs no field.
- **Streamed** (`tiers-stream.ts`, `regional-patch.ts`): eight neighbours (directions 0 to 7; with them several cells of a block's edge drain into one cell of the halo, so the area pass roots the tree at those exit cells: kernel `todexit`), the step's number, route and table armed at the start of every call and step (`arm`), the table per step (`tableAt`), and the lift as the stages `stencil` then `lift` (two visits of the tier, where the old one had three) with the solve on the tier-0 tables between them. Tier-0 tables kept between calls grew to 15 words a tier-0 cell (24 MB).

## Exactness (tests)

`tiers-stream.test.ts`: the old cases (the steepest of eight neighbours, one step length) equal the in-memory solve word for word in blocks of 1, 2 and 4 tiles and in another order (visits per step: init, flood, recv, area1, area2, incise, slide, creep, stencil, lift); the new case, **the island's law (eight neighbours, drawn receivers, steps of 26, 6.5 and 1.6 My, one rain)**, equals the in-memory solve in blocks of 1 tile, of 2 tiles in reverse order and of 2 tiles in calls of 0.6 s (heights, carries, receivers, discharge area, flooded surface). The in-memory region solver (`land.ts`) is the same kernels in one patch and was run twice to the same bytes.

## Cost (estimates from the measured streamed step; not run at tier 5)

Streamed step at tier 5 263 s at 1 My steps (creep 98 s of it, 28 substeps); creep substeps scale with the step length (D dt / d squared), so the island's schedule costs: tier 5, 6 steps of about 165 s without creep plus 46 creep passes in all (T = 9.7 My) at 3.5 s: **about 20 min**; tier 4 (a quarter of the cells, 36 s a step at 1 My): 28 steps of about 25 s without creep plus about 315 creep passes of about 0.9 s: **about 20 min**; the carries, the start (37 s) and the final flow and four files (3 min): **about 45 min of GPU for tiers 4 and 5, in 12 to 15 calls**, against 52 min for tier 5 alone at 12 steps before. The step sizes and passes are estimates from the region runs (the in-memory region of 9.4 million cells takes about 4 s a step) and the measured streamed passes, not measured at tier 5; the tier-5 call that runs them should log it. Tiers 1 to 3 in memory: 81 s.

## Gaps and operator questions

- Implied tectonic uplift 25 mm/yr in the range region against Earth's fastest 5 to 10 (above).
- Rain: the fine tiers erode under one rain (the island's), not the planet's climate (a gap the island has too); with the planet's, half the range cannot incise.
- Remaining parallel comb valleys on smooth ramps at the Smith-Bretherton spacing, and the tier-1-to-3 terraces; the ice's joint valleys (the island's way of making oblique lines) are not built on the planet.
- Tiers 1 to 3 are now 8-neighbour with drawn receivers: it changes tier 3 and so everything downstream (the whole bake is rerun by the rebake anyway); set `COARSE.route` in `tiers.ts` back to `{ nflow: 4, stochastic: false }` to keep them.
- On the 12 by 12 range region one tier-0 cell's mean is 260 m off the planet's (p99 0.08 m over 2,240 cells): not found, one cell, at the held ring or the land's edge; the 8 by 8 range and the coast regions are within 8 m and 1.3 m.
- The lift's coefficients may be negative: a smooth dip beside a tier-0 cell of great loss (not seen as an artifact in the pictures).
- `TIER_LAWS` steps and `streamTier` at tier 5 are not run on the planet; `regional.test.ts` (its four-neighbour cases pinned to the four-neighbour law; the step hash re-recorded deliberately, the lift changed) and `tiers.test.ts` pass.

## Napkin note (for the runbook, not edited here)
