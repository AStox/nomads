## What was built

Erosion runs **on tectonics' own state**: the five rock volumes of every column (basalt, sediment, metasediment, granitoid, volcanic) in the buffer `Tectonics.S`. It is **interleaved**: after each 2 My tectonic step, four substeps of 0.5 My, so rock removed from a column and sediment laid on it are what the next plate step moves, and isostasy floats each column to its new height from the volumes (the tectonics' own `elev` is recomputed at the start of its next step). Erosion never stores a height. A substep:

1. `height`: the isostatic height of every column in 1/256 m above the sea, tectonics' law at 1/16 m (`elevOf` of `globe-tectonics.ts`, Archimedes at the base of the column, R1411 sec. 2.2) [R1567]. Land is a cell above the sea level of the setup, the sea any cell at or under it (lakes under the sea level are sea).
2. `weights`: a land cell's share of the discharge, its area times its rain over the reference rain [R1563].
3. **Flow routing** [R1565, R1566]: the priority flood of the island generator on the sphere: the flooded surface f = max(h, least neighbour f + eps) from the ocean inward (the one fixed point, so any order of threads gives the same words), found by rounds of one dispatch each until a round changes nothing (192 to 256 rounds on the real planet, 17 ms a flow after compile); every land cell then drains to its steepest neighbour on f over eight neighbours (strictly lower, so no cycle), depth by pointer jumping, the cells filed by depth (a counting sort) and the discharge area gathered deepest first. The neighbours of a cell across tile and face edges are the grid's `haloSource` (the 24 diagonals missing at the 8 cube corners are the only gaps; the table is symmetric), and the distance to a neighbour is the great-circle arc between the two centres on the 2,000 km radius (8.6 to 19.9 km).
4. **Erodibility** from the rock in the top 500 m of the column, by thickness [R1561, R1568].
5. **Incision**: the implicit stream power step in order of depth, receivers first [R1560, R1564]: n = 1 (m = 1/2) h' = h_r + (h - h_r) / (1 + f), f = K dt sqrt(A) / d; n = 2 (m = 1) x + c x^2 = h - h_r, c = K dt A / d^2, by x = 2 (h - h_r) / (1 + sqrt(1 + 4 c (h - h_r))). A cell under the flooded surface (a lake) is not cut; the receiver's base is its new height, or the sea level at a river mouth.
6. **Removal** [R1567]: the thickness the incision took, times the cell's area, is taken off the top of the stack (sediment, volcanic, metasediment, granitoid, basalt [design]); 20% of it leaves as dissolved load [R1569]; the rest is sediment.
7. **Sediment down the tree** [R1570, R1571]: Davy and Lague's deposition, the share g / (1 + g) of the flux settling in a cell, g = G w / A with w the cell's discharge weight and A the discharge area (G = 1); a lake takes up to what fills it to the flooded surface; sediment carried over from earlier substeps (below) travels down the river beside the regular flux and deposits only in lakes.
8. **The coast and the shelf** [R1572, R1573]: sea cells are ringed by distance from the land (up to 96 rings, 1,100 km). What the rivers bring to ring 1 fills each cell up to a graded shelf (ring c is c times 11.3 m deeper than the coast, a slope of 1e-3) with the isostatic subsidence the load makes (2.875 m of sediment for each metre of surface), and what is left goes to the cell's children in the next ring in equal shares (the remainder to the first in direction order): a fan. A cell with no children (a dead end between two coasts, or the last ring) fills to the sea, and what is still left is **carried over** to the next substep at that cell. Open sea beyond the rings takes what it is carrying as far as the water is deep.
9. `apply`: rock off the top of each stack, sediment on top (a column holds at most what a volume's 2^28 does: what would pass it stays in transit); the basalt's age sum and the orogeny clock are scaled with the volume so the mean ages are kept.
10. **Creep and the sea floor's diffusion** [R1574 to R1576]: the grid's conservative stencil (gradient of the height in 1/64 m with the cross term and the vertex weights), here as whole volume units a link: each link's flux is rounded to volume units by one rule at both its cells (odd, so the two cells' fluxes are exactly opposite), limited by what the giver can spare (an eighth of its column on land, of its sediment on the sea floor), land links at the creep's diffusivity, sea links at the sea floor's, a link between land and sea not at all. Iterated until the number is under the grid's stable 0.08.

Entry points: `Erosion` (the kernels on a device: `heights`, `flow`, `erode`, `apply`, `creep`, `substep(books, seen)`, `solverStep` for the stream power solver alone on given heights, `readFlow`, `ledger`, `carry`), `Twin` (every kernel in TypeScript), `runChain(gpu, set, options)` (tectonics with erosion between its steps; the rain is a callback so this file does not import the atmosphere; `afterMy` for erosion alone once the plates stop), `hypsometry`, `rivers`. `bun scripts/globe-erosion-maps.ts --seed 1 --out /tmp/globe-erosion-1 --rain atmosphere --rain-every 67 --after 50 --zoom "-45,-70,40"` writes the maps and `summary.json`. The rain input is `Erosion.setRain(mm a year per cell)`: **the unit is millimetres of water a year**; the script sums the atmosphere's four seasons of `rain` (mm over the season). Kernels run at tier 0 only (the tables are the grid's whole-tier tables, its gap 2).

## Fields, units and scales

E (28 fields of 393,216 words, `EF` in `wgsl/globe-erosion.ts`), in the tables' cell order, which at tier 0 is the grid's cell number (face * 256 + y) * 256 + x. S is tectonics' (volumes V = H a / 16, 62,400 m3 on the mean cell, 0.49 mm of the mean cell's column to a unit).

| quantity | format | range it holds |
|---|---|---|
| height HQ | 1/256 m, i32, above the sea | 8 km is 2.1e6 |
| flooded surface | the same, an atomic word; eps is 1 unit (3.9 mm) | a fill of 600 cells rises 2.3 m |
| inverse distance to a neighbour | Q28 of 1/m | 3.1e4 at 8.6 km |
| rain | Q8 of rain over the reference, at most 2,048 (8x) | 8,000 mm a year |
| discharge weight and area | 1/512 of the mean cell at the reference rain, u32 | the planet at 8x rain is 2.0e9 |
| erodibility word | K dt c0 in Q12 (n = 1, c0 the square root of the mean cell over 512 is 499.6 m) or 4 K dt A0 / (512 * 256) in Q20 (n = 2) | granite at 0.5 My: 7.1e-8 * 5e5 * 499.6 * 4096 = 7.3e4 |
| sediment fluxes | two words (lo, hi) of volume units | a basin of 1e5 cells at 0.4 mm a year over 0.5 My is 1e10 |
| a deposit | volume units, u32, under 2^28 in a column | the ring caps are under 5.5e7 |
| creep height | 1/64 m (|q| under 2^20), the grid's Q formats | 16 km |

The area unit is a rounding of each cell's weight to 1/512 of the mean cell (a mean cell at the reference rain is 512 units; the rounding is half a unit, 0.1%); the total drainage of the planet equals the land's area to 5e-4 (tested).

## What looks wrong, and why (a failing check is a gap, not a retune)

1. **Erosion barely touches the ranges.** 11 to 18% of the planet over 2 km before erosion (the tectonics note), 10.6 to 13.2% for these seeds with the same read and no erosion, 10.3 to 12.7% after, the share over 4 km 6.7 to 9.9% before and 6.3 to 9.3% after. Cause, measured: the land as a whole is worn at Earth's median rate (50 to 71 m a My with the atmosphere's rain, 119 with a uniform 1,000 mm), but the ranges are flat tables at the 70 km limit's height, which tectonics re-cuts to the limit at each step, and a table has no slope for stream power to use: erosion eats the scarps at its edges. The cited K of granite (7e-8) gives 80 to 120 m a My on the headwater cells of a 12 km grid, a tenth of the thickening a collision makes. To bring the share over 2 km to Earth's 5% needs K ten times larger (above the top of granite's cited range, 2.5e-7) and then the denudation of the land is 620 m a My, three times Earth's mean. Both say that the tectonics thickens too fast and too flatly for a river law whose K was measured on Earth, and that the limit's flat table (the tectonics note's gap 4) hides the relief erosion needs. The sensitivity (seed 1, 200 My, uniform 1,000 mm unless stated; the share over 2 km, over 4 km, the mean land elevation, the denudation): K x 0.1 13.4%, 9.4%, 2,868 m, 23 m a My; K x 1 11.8%, 8.1%, 2,564 m, 119; K x 10 3.5%, 2.1%, 986 m, 621; n = 2 13.1%, 8.7%, 2,689 m, 82; substeps 2 a step 11.4%, 7.6%, 2,481 m, 133; 8 a step 12.4%, 8.7%, 2,673 m, 112; uniform rain 400 mm 12.7%, 8.8%, 2,717 m, 82; 2,500 mm 10.9%, 7.2%, 2,329 m, 175; and with no plate motion for the last 100 My (a separate run) 8.2% and 3.5%. The step changes the result by under 10% (the implicit scheme is stable, but the flow pattern is held within a step); the rain by a tenth for six times the water (the discharge enters as its square root).
2. **The sea floor is blanketed in sediment.** The mean ocean depth comes out at 3.6 to 3.8 km (Earth's 3.8) only because 1.0 to 1.3 km of sediment on average (up to over 5 km on 3 to 4% of the sea) fills a floor that tectonics alone leaves at 3.9 to 4.0 km (and the thermal floor to 6 km); Earth's mean cover is about half a kilometre [UNVERIFIED, remembered] and its floor is recycled in 100 My, here in 40 to 50 My, so the sediment is not subducted away at a rate that keeps up. The rings fill the water near each coast before the deep sea and the rule has no turbidity current, canyon or submarine channel; a delta is a fan of equal shares, not a lobe, and the margin has no slope break. The map is `sediment.png`.
3. **Sediment in transit.** 2 to 3% of the rock removed is not in any column at the end of a run (7% after 100 My of erosion alone): it sits at dead ends of the rings (straits and bays between two coasts) and is carried over to the next substep, then comes down the rivers beside the regular flux. It is counted in the books (removed = deposited + dissolved + transit, to the unit) but it is a placement the model could not make, not a physical store.
4. **The rain.** The atmosphere's rain on the tectonic planet overflowed the unit's accumulator over 1,024 mm in the window on steep flanks (the 2 day window was shortened to 1, then 0.5 day), and rises to the 8x cap in the ranges' windward cells (`rain.png`); this unit takes it as a unit-stated input and did not tune it. The atmosphere's own gaps (rain a quarter of Earth's on average, a dry belt exactly dry) are not repeated here.
5. **A closed sea under the sea level is sea.** An inland cell under the sea level is a sea cell: it receives sediment as the ocean does and is not a lake with an outflow; the many blue specks in the quiet ranges are such cells. The flooded surface treats a depression above the sea as a lake only for routing and filling.
6. **Not every river is cut where it is.** The incision of a cell takes the thickness the implicit step computes as rock removed, as if the surface fell by all of it, while isostasy gives back 85% of it (R1567); the exact implicit solution for a floating column would erode more in a substep. The error is first order in the substep (the table of substeps above) and has not been removed.
7. **The cells are 12 km.** A cell is the headwater of its stream, so the steepest streams are never resolved: steady-state relief within a cell is not represented, the stream power law's headwater threshold (0.1 to 5 km2 in the cited papers) is smaller than a cell by four orders, and valley spacing is one cell at least.
8. **No flexure, no knickpoint-preserving scheme, no glacial erosion, no landslides, no threshold.** The implicit step smooths knickpoints (Hergarten 2020 sec. 1: Campforts et al. 2017); local isostasy (the tectonics note's gap 8) broadens nothing; the ice unit is separate.
9. **K for sediment, basalt and volcanic rock are gaps and analogues** (R1561), the dissolved share, the sea floor's diffusivity and the shelf slope are unverified (R1569, R1575, R1572), and G is a design value.
10. **Tier 0 only, a table of the whole tier, and the strip along face edges** where the grid's diffusion is first order (its gap 1): creep and the sea floor's diffusion cross the cube's edges with that error. Creep at 4e-3 m2 a year moves nothing at 12 km (63 m in 1 My); it is there for the stencil and the sea floor's value.
11. **The checks of the stream power law are mostly algebraic.** The steady state tests the discretisation across edges and corners; it cannot say that a landscape is right. The one independent check is the rain shadow (4.07 for a rain ratio of 700, the discharge's square root and the area it feeds) and the sizes of the denudation against Portenga and Bierman, which fail for the reason in item 1.
12. **The second adapter was not available** (Apple M3 Pro); the check is GPU against twin, bit for bit, as the hard rules say.

## Napkin note (for the repo's runbook, not edited here)

**[2026-10-19] Sediment placed by rings needs a bucket for what cannot be placed, and that bucket must not pass through the deposit law.** `src/gpu/globe/erosion.ts`: eroded volume is routed down the receiver forest and, at the sea, spread by rings of cells with accommodation caps; where the rings have no room (dead ends between two coasts) the surplus is kept in a per-cell `carry` and re-enters next substep. A first version added the carry to the flux and so to Davy and Lague's deposit share, which put 7.6e8 volume units (hundreds of km of sediment) in one land cell; a second let a dead end dump everything at once. Do instead: keep carried sediment in its own flux down the tree (deposited only in lakes), cap every deposit by the room above the sea level times the isostatic factor 2.875, and test the books to the unit (removed = deposited + dissolved + carry). Also: the lake test must read the height before the incision (the incision lowers a cell under its flooded surface), and a debug counter in a kernel hides in a field some other kernel reuses.
