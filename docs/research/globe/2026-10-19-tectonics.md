## Laws and constants

### Isostasy (R1411 sec. 2.2)

Archimedes at the base of a column: rho_c h = rho_m b (eq. 2.2); surface height above the mantle level h - b = h (1 - rho_c / rho_m) (eq. 2.3). Cited values: rho_m 3300, rho_cc 2800 (a mean of 2750 is also used), rho_oc 2900, rho_w 1000 kg/m^3; mean continental crust 35 km (typical), 40 km (Appendix B mean), oceanic 6 km; mean land elevation 875 m; mean ocean depth 3,794 m; land 1.48e14 m^2 of 5.10e14 (29%); continents with margins 2.0e14 (39%).

- Elevation of continental crust of thickness h, with 35 km at sea level: z = (h - 35 km) (1 - rho_c / rho_m) = 0.1515 (h - 35 km) [derived from eq. 2.3]. A 5 km range needs 68 km of crust (a 28 km root); a 1 km plateau 41.6 km; the 70 km of Tibet is consistent with a 6 km height [derived].
- Ocean basins against continents (eq. 2.5): h_w = (rho_m - rho_cc)/(rho_m - rho_w) h_cc - (rho_m - rho_oc)/(rho_m - rho_w) h_oc = 6.6 km for 35 and 6 km [cited, computed in the book].
- Erosion of rock thickness d lowers the crust by d, so the surface falls only 0.1515 d and the rest is isostatic rebound [derived]. Unit 3 must remove material from the crust-thickness state, not from a stored height. Sediment of density 2,500 laid on the shelf adds crust-equivalent mass; a basin filled to sea level after crustal stretching by factor alpha is h_sb = h_cc (rho_m - rho_cc)/(rho_m - rho_s) (1 - 1/alpha) (eq. 2.10): 11 km for alpha 2, at most 21.9 km [cited].
- Local (Airy) compensation is the law of the kernel; flexure of an elastic plate smooths loads over about 50 to 100 km [INFERENCE: R1411 quotes an elastic thickness of 54 km for the continental lithosphere in one basin, sec. 3.16 end]. At the 12 km cell size flexure is not resolved; a diffusion of the height over 3 to 5 cells stands in for it [design].

### Ocean floor and plate cooling (R1411 secs. 4.16, 4.23)

- Half-space cooling: depth w = 2 rho_m alpha_v (T1 - T0)/(rho_m - rho_w) sqrt(kappa t / pi) (eq. 4.209), with rho_m 3300, rho_w 1000, kappa 1 mm^2/s, T1 - T0 1300 K, alpha_v 3e-5 per K, ridge depth 2.5 km [cited]. The coefficient is 355 m per sqrt(My) [derived]; so depth(t) = 2.5 km + 0.355 km sqrt(t in My): 3.6 km at 10 My, 5.7 at 80, 6.0 at 100, 7.5 at 200. Observed old floor is shallower than this beyond about 80 My (the plate model, basal heating): cap the depth, a plate-model fit gives 6.4 km at old ages (Parsons and Sclater's 6400 - 3200 exp(-t/62.8) is remembered, not read) [UNVERIFIED]. Mean ocean depth 3.8 km (Appendix B).
- Lithosphere thickness y_L = 2.32 sqrt(kappa t) = 41 km at 10 My, 92 at 50, 117 at 80 (eq. 4.126; 116 km at 80 My is the book's own figure) [cited]. Plate model thickness 95 or 125 km; its time constant yL0^2 / (pi^2 kappa) is 29 or 50 My [derived].
- Sea level and ridge volume (eq. 4.214 to 4.216): the mean depth of the basins below the ridge crests is proportional to the square root of the mean age at subduction; the Cretaceous sea stood 300 m above today's with 40% of the continents flooded [cited]. It is a long-term sea level control (tens of My) and an input to Unit 6's sea level.
- Oceanic crust 6 km thick (layer 1 sediments about 1 km, layers 2 and 3 basalt, gabbro), ocean floor mean age 100 My, continental crust mean age 1 Ga [cited, sec. 1.1]. Oceanic lithosphere is created at ridges at half rates u, symmetric to first order [cited].

### Plate speeds (R1412, R1411)

- Spreading rates 9.7 to 154.4 mm/yr over 1,696 observations; fastest East Pacific Rise 120 to 145 (Pacific-Nazca), slow Atlantic 11 to 23 (Eurasia-North America), Juan de Fuca half rate 29 mm/yr; typical subduction rate 0.1 m/yr; relative plate speeds "a few tens of mm/yr" [cited]. Cortial's cap is 100 mm/yr.
- A plate is a rigid rotation: surface velocity v = omega x r about a pole (R1410); on a sphere of radius R the angular rate is v / R: 100 mm/yr on R = 2,000 km is 2.9 degrees per My, and in 200 My a plate at 50 mm/yr travels 10,000 km, 80% of the planet's circumference (12,566 km), against 25% on Earth [derived]. Earth speeds on this planet make plates lap it. [gap] The size scaling of plate speed (mantle depth, heat flow of a body this small) is not read; the speeds are Earth's [analogue] and the planet will have 3 to 4 times Earth's plate-circuits per My. If the first runs show no stable continents, scale the rates by R / 6,371 km and state it as a design choice, not a retune.

### Convergent margins: uplift, arcs, batholiths

- Subduction: the older or denser plate goes down; trench; accretionary prism scraped from the layer-1 sediments; volcanic line about 125 km above the slab [cited, R1411 sec. 1.4]; Mt Baker to Shasta is the example of an arc on a continent. On a PNW-style coast the forearc high (Vancouver Island, Olympics, Oregon Coast Range) lies seaward of a forearc low (Georgia and Puget lowlands) and the arc (R1414); arc to trench is roughly 150 to 250 km for a 30 to 45 degree slab [INFERENCE from the 125 km depth above].
- Thickening law (pure shear, volume conserved): dh/dt = h v / W for shortening rate v across width W [derived]. For v 20 mm/yr, W 100 km, h 35 km: 7 mm/yr of thickening and 1.06 mm/yr of surface uplift (x 0.1515): this reproduces the worked case of R1413 (a 100 km orogen uplifting 1 mm/yr is fed by 20 mm/yr of convergence) [derived, checks against cited].
- Observed rates [cited, R1413]: Southern Alps uplift up to 11 mm/yr on the west side, mean rock uplift 4 to 5 mm/yr, convergence component 10 to 11 mm/yr, steady state with a velocity ratio R of 1 to 4; Taiwan shortening about 50 mm/yr, uplift 7.5 to 17.5, up to 35 mm/yr; Olympic accretionary wedge exhumation just under 1 mm/yr since 12 to 17 Ma (used as 0.8 mm/yr), against 34 mm/yr of Juan de Fuca convergence (R1414), and above 2 km/My earlier. Time to topographic steady state 1 to 50 My for uplift 0.1 to 1 mm/yr [cited]. Orogen widths 50 to 200 km in the analysis; Tibet is a broad exception because the upper plate was weak [cited, R1411 sec. 1.11]; shortening of 300 km or more in the Himalaya.
- Cortial's uplift constants are not physical rates and are not used: u0 0.6 mm/yr, continental erosion 0.03 mm/yr, oceanic damping 0.04 mm/yr, trench sediment fill 0.3 mm/yr, max speed 100 mm/yr, dt 2 My, subduction distance 1,800 km, collision distance 4,200 km, collision coefficient 1.3e-5 per km (R1410 Table 3; the distances are 28% and 66% of Earth's radius and could not fit this planet). Its shapes are kept: uplift rises with relative speed (linear) and with the height of the plunging crust, with a cubic bump in the distance from the front.
- Arc magmatism (R1415): background addition under 30 km^3 per My per km of arc, flare-ups 75 to 150 for 5 to 20 My every 30 to 70 My. Over a 50 km arc this is 0.6 mm/yr of crustal thickening in the background and 1.5 to 3 mm/yr in a flare-up [derived]; the Fiordland arc doubled from 35 to 40 km to 60 to 80 km in about 10 to 15 My (3 to 4 mm/yr) [cited]. Plutonic rock is 30 times the volcanic; a half of the melt is crustal. The Coast Mountains batholith (R1417, R1418): 1,700 km by 50 to 175 km, mostly tonalite and diorite (intermediate), emplaced Jurassic to Eocene in four flare-ups [UNVERIFIED], the arc front migrating inland at about 2 km/My [UNVERIFIED].
- Terranes (R1410, R1416, R1417): continental fragments, arcs and oceanic plateaus too light to subduct are carried to a trench, resist subduction and suture to the margin; the outer part is scraped off (the Chugach terrane is the example); the Intermontane superterrane docked about 180 Ma, the Insular (Wrangellia, Alexander) about 100 Ma, and the arc then overprinted the suture. Cortial: the collision radius is r = r_c sqrt((v / v0) (A / A0)) and the uplift a compact bump of the distance to the terrane.
- Rifting (R1410): a plate splits into 2 to 4 pieces by a warped Voronoi cut, with a Poisson rate growing with the continental share and area; new ocean ages from 0 at the ridge. A rifted margin subsides as the ocean floor does (sqrt of age), the shelf filling with sediment (R1411 eq. 2.10) [derived].

## What a kernel needs (Unit 2)

Grid: the six faces of 256 x 256 at about 12.3 km a cell (2,000 km x pi/2 / 256) [derived]; trenches (about 100 km) are 8 cells, arcs 4. Steps: 100 steps of 2 My for 200 My (R1410's 2 My, 250 My to a realistic planet); a finer 1 My step if a plate crosses more than 16 cells (200 km) in one, so that 100 mm/yr is 8 cells a step [design].

Fields per cell (integer, metres and units of 0.1 My): plate id (u8); crust type (1 bit); crust thickness h (i32 m, Q0, to 90,000); oceanic age (u16, 0.1 My); orogeny age (u16) and type (2 bits: none, Andean, Himalayan); terrane id (u8); lithology (u8: basalt, sediment, granitoid, metamorphic, terrane rock, with the age); fold direction (u8 angle). Surface height is derived each step from h, type and age by the isostasy above and never stored, so erosion and deposition change h only. Sea level is a parameter.

Plate motion on a grid without a mesh [design]: each plate has a pole (a unit vector, Q30) and a rate (rad/My, Q30). For each cell x and each plate p (about 8 to 12), back-rotate x by p's step to y_p, read the old plate id at the nearest cell to y_p, and accept p if it matches. No candidate: a divergence, so create oceanic crust at age 0 owned by the nearest plate. Two or more: a convergence, resolved by R1410's rules (continental over oceanic; older oceanic over younger; continent on continent starts a collision), the loser's column vanishing from the surface and its crust going into the overriding column's thickness. This is a covering test per destination, so no fast plate can tunnel through another, unlike a boundary-triangle tracker. Nearest-cell resampling gives a position error of up to half a cell a step, a random walk of about 3 cells (36 km) in 100 steps; carry a sub-cell offset if it shows [design]. Pole, rate and Voronoi centres come from the seed through Philox, the only random inputs.

Order in a step: move, resolve covers, thicken (the law above: relative speed to the front, distance to the front with a cubic bump, the thickness of the plunging crust), arc magmatism by the slab distance and the flare-up clock, accretion events, ridge ages, rifting events, then isostasy and a diffusion of the height over 3 to 5 cells. The lithology field is written at each event: basalt at ridges, granitoid in an arc and the batholith behind it, sediment in trenches and shelves, high-grade rock in a collision.

Checks that mean something (not tuned): land fraction near 0.29 (Appendix B), ocean depth 2.5 km at a ridge growing as sqrt(age), ranges 50 to 200 km wide at converging margins with crust 55 to 70 km, ridge-parallel age stripes symmetric to first order, an arc 125 km above the slab behind a trench, rifts producing basins (11 km sediment for alpha 2).

## Open (gaps)

1. The size scaling of plate speed and of plate size on a 2,000 km planet (above). Earth has 7 major plates of about 7e13 m^2; this planet's whole area is 5.0e13 m^2, so 6 to 10 plates are 5e12 to 8e12 m^2 each, the size of Earth's small plates [INFERENCE; Bird 2003 not read].
2. No physical uplift law for collisions and no passive-margin law in R1410 beyond the sqrt(age) and R1411's stretching; flexural width is not resolved; hot spots are absent (R1410 names them as future work).
3. The arc flux and flare-up timing are cited for Cordilleran arcs and not derived from the slab; the flare-up clock is a [design] draw (period 30 to 70 My, length 5 to 20 My) from the seed.
4. Delamination of arc roots (R1415, R1411 sec. 1.1) is not modelled; the dense residue (1 to 3 times the melt) never leaves, so arcs would stay too thick and high.
5. Lower crust and terrane thickness values, plate-model old-age depth and the Coast Mountains numbers are [UNVERIFIED] above.
