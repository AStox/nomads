## What was built

The planet as the chain leaves it (a height in metres above the sea, the atmosphere's sea-level air temperature and precipitation of four seasons) is the baseline; a forcing shifts every temperature; ice builds where solid precipitation beats melt, flows, cuts the bed, lowers the sea and calves into it. Per step, all integer kernels on the tier-0 grid (393,216 cells), in the order the host records them (`wgsl/globe-ice.ts`):

1. `prep`: the bed (initial bed less the rock the ice has cut plus the sediment laid, less the load's depression), the ice's thickness from its volume, the surface `s = bed + h`, the mass balance at the surface (R1630), whether the bed is at the melting point (R1635). A floor under the sea with no ice has no balance (ice would be afloat).
2. the grid's halo exchange of `s` and its least-squares gradient (second order across face edges and cube corners).
3. `coef`: each cell's diffusion number `D dt / (the mean cell's area)` from Glen's law and the Weertman slide (R1633, R1636), held at the cap (R1642), and the cut rate `K_g u_s^l` (R1637); the stiffest cell's log goes to one atomic word, with counts of ice cells and of capped cells.
4. `demand`: the step's mass balance (ablation cannot take more than the stock; the debris of melted ice is laid where it melts), the four links' fluxes with the grid's conservative stencil (a link's number is that of the cell the ice comes from), and each cell's share of its stock that it can give.
5. `carry`: the fluxes as moved (the giver's share of each, and the debris goes with the ice at the giver's concentration), the bed's cut under the ice that lay there, afloat ice calved (its debris laid on the sea floor), the depression relaxed toward the load.

`IceGpu.advance(years)` repeats batches of 8 steps at one `dt` chosen from the batch before so that the stiffest cell sits at a diffusion number of 0.06, never under `dtMin` (2 years), and `runIce` updates the forcing's offset and the sea level every outer step (500 years). A step of the kernels measured 0.9 ms on the M3 Pro with the GPU to itself and 2.3 to 3.9 ms while another agent shared it (all 393,216 cells, nine dispatches); the cycle of 140 kyr at 2 years (70,000 steps with the sea level every 500 years) took 120 to 130 s. `IceGpu` runs on any tier (the host keeps each cell's area weight in flat order, `aw`); `IceTwin` is tier 0 only.

Entry points: `IceGpu` (the kernels on a device), `IceTwin` (every kernel in TypeScript), `runIce` (the forcing and the sea over a timeline), `seaState`, `books` (the ledger, in bigint), `bedsOf`, `troughs`, `landComponents`; the laws in float (`ablationMm`, `balanceMm`, `elaOf`, `diffusivity`, `slidingSpeed`, `erosionRate`, `seaLevelOf`) which are the tests' oracles; the orbit (`orbitOf`, `summerAnomaly`, `lgmForcing`, `forcingTable`).

## Why the explicit step is capped and not solved implicitly

The shallow-ice scheme is a diffusion of the surface with `D = h^4 |grad s|^2 (Gamma h + K_s (rho g)^3)` and the explicit limit is `dt = 0.098 A / D` (A the mean cell's 1.28e8 m2): 52 years for 300 m of ice on a slope of 0.1, 0.4 years for 1.5 km on 0.02, and 0.04 years for the 2 km of an ice sheet with the sliding of temperate ice at slope 0.01 (D about 2e8 m2 a year). Over 100 kyr that is 2.5 million steps for a sheet, some 1.5 hours here. The research note names three ways out; the choice and why:

- **(b) a cap on the flux, taken.** A cell's number is held at 0.09 (the largest the grid's own tests run its stencil at; its two-point bound is 0.098), every step is explicit, positive and exactly conservative (a link's flux is one integer that both cells take with opposite sign; each cell gives at most its stock, so thickness cannot go negative), and the step is chosen so that the cap binds rarely. It costs nothing in code and keeps the integer, bit-for-bit discipline.
- **(a) a semi-implicit step, not taken.** Picard or ADI needs a linear solve on the cube sphere per step. Jacobi sweeps converge as slowly as the explicit steps they replace (a sweep is as dear as a step and the stiff number is the same); conjugate gradients or multigrid need reductions and divisions that are not integer-exact on the GPU; line solves along a face's rows leave the stiff couplings across face edges explicit, which is where the instability would come back. None was built [gap].
- **(c) a lumped ice sheet, not taken**: it drops the valley glaciers that are the point of the unit.

What the cap costs: where D passes `0.09 A / dtMin` (5.7e6 m2 a year at the floor of 2 years, ice over some 700 m thick on a slope of 0.01 or so) the ice moves more slowly than Glen's law says, so it comes out thicker and its sheets respond more slowly. `advance` counts the ice cells the cap held: 13 to 18% of the cells that hold ice over the cycles of the results section. Measured (the `bias` stage: from each seed's glacial maximum, 5 kyr more at its forcing at dtMin 2, 1 and 0.5 years): halving the floor twice takes 11 to 22% off the ice volume, 1.3 km off the thickest ice (6.0 to 6.5 km down to 4.5 to 4.7 km), 3 to 7 m off the sea-level drop and 11 to 16% off the rock cut in those 5 kyr; the glaciated area changes by 2 to 3%. The two decrements fall geometrically (ratios 0.5 to 0.64); extrapolated, dtMin 2's volume is 23 to 40% over the converged one at the three maxima; from no ice, 10 kyr at -6 K on seed 1 gives 5.42e5, 4.21e5, 3.73e5 km3 at dtMin 2, 1, 0.5, about 3.3e5 extrapolated (dtMin 2 is 64% over). **Volumes, thicknesses and sea-level drops in this note are overestimates, by roughly a quarter to a half in the thick-ice regime; areas are within a few percent; the depth of the cut under thick ice is overestimated (its sliding speed comes from the inflated thickness and slope and often sits at the law's 3 m a day cap).**

### Do ice ages form at this planet's temperatures

Mountain and coastal ice forms, and grows and shrinks with the forcing (the area at the maximum is 1.3 to 1.6 times the interglacial's); **lowland ice sheets do not form, and that is a gap.** At the cited glacial cooling the lowland where the equilibrium line reaches the ground is 2 to 5% of the lowland (5e4 to 9e4 km2), and the lowland ice at the maximum (7e4 to 1.6e5 km2) is mostly the coastal domes' spread. Why, from the climate fields along 60 to 72 degrees on all three seeds: the continents' interiors get almost no precipitation from the atmosphere (0 to 300 mm a year, often 0 over the plateaus and over lowland at 70 N in seed 3), while their windward edges get 2,400 to 39,000 mm a year (above the wettest places on Earth, about 12,000); their summer air at sea level is 10 to 14 C on lowland at 70 N and 20 to 33 C reduced to sea level over the 4 to 4.5 km plateaus. With no snow there is nothing for a cooling of 6 K to keep. The parts of the chain behind it, in order: the atmosphere's precipitation, which drops all its water on the first slope (no recycling, no inland moisture transport worth the name) [gap, atmosphere unit]; the forcing's single uniform offset, with no polar amplification and no ice-albedo or elevation feedback on the climate (R1647) [gap]; the continents as broad plateaus 4 to 4.5 km high, which the range work is changing [gap, tectonics]. The sweep shows the lowland ice grows to 4.6e5 to 6.4e5 km2 at -10 K, so the model can glaciate the lowland where snow falls; it is the snow, not the ice, that is missing.

### What resolves at tier 0, and the tier-1 check

A tier-0 cell is 12.3 km across at a face's centre; a fjord is 2 to 10 km wide. At tier 0 what is resolved: the equilibrium line and the mass balance, the extent of caps, domes and outlet lobes, the ice volume and the sea level it takes, where thick temperate ice slides and so where the bed is cut hardest, the lowstand's bared shelf, and troughs two cells wide or more (25 km). What is not: any fjord or valley glacier narrower than a cell, a fjord's sill, its depth profile, the valley network the ice follows (the bed under 12 km is smooth), and the steering of ice into passes that Kessler et al. (R1465) find enough to make fjords. A drowned tier-0 cell says the ice cut below the sea somewhere in those 150 km2; the coast-parallel strips say a terminus stood there.

The window stage must add: the sub-cell relief the ice follows (the window's own river valleys), then the ice over it, from the tier-0 ice surface and balance at the time, cutting by the same law (R1637) at its own resolution for the glaciated part of the timeline, the sea level history taken from tier 0 as a given scalar; the cut volume of a tier-0 cell is the budget its valleys share. At 1.5 km the explicit limit is 64 times tier 0's, so this needs the semi-implicit step (a) or a steady ice surface per epoch rather than the cap.

The tier-1 check (`--stage tier1`, seed 3, R1651): seed 3's glacial maximum carried to tier 1 (6.1 km cells, 1.57 million) and run 10 kyr more at the maximum's forcing, against tier 0 run the same 10 kyr from the same state; the region is 600 km around the deepest cut (55.4 S 179.7 W), a range front on a coastal lowland. What the ice had cut there by the maximum is a sound along the foot of the range, between it and a strip of coastal lowland, open to the sea at its ends. After the 10 kyr: at tier 0 the region's main trough is 263 km long, 26 km wide, reaching 74 km inland, 872 m deep, with a second of 29 km, 17 km wide, 981 m; at tier 1 the same two are 261 km, 23 km wide, 61 km, 803 m and 32 km, 13 km wide, 812 m. So the troughs keep their place and length, narrow by 10 to 25% and lose depth to the interpolation's smoothing; at 13 to 23 km they are still two to four tier-1 cells wide, not fjord-narrow, and 10 kyr is too short to show more (the law's fastest cut is 94 m in that time). A tier-1 step cost about 8 ms here against 2.8 ms at tier 0 in the same run (with the sea level every 500 years), so the full 140 kyr at tier 1 would be some 40 minutes, eight times the five-minute rule; carving a whole glaciation at tier 1 or finer needs the regional window (a patch of tiles with the tier-0 ice at its edge) and the semi-implicit step, neither built [gap].

### Gaps

1. The cap's overestimate of thickness, volume and sea level (above): a semi-implicit step is the fix and was not built.
2. Lowland ice sheets do not form: precipitation, uniform cooling with no feedback, plateau continents (above).
3. The cut under thick ice runs at the 3 m a day ceiling; the depth of the deepest troughs (1.1 to 1.3 km) is that ceiling times the time, not a measured balance.
4. Fjords proper are below the grid: tier 0 gives strips and a few troughs cut 25 to 75 km inland.
5. The sea level has no hydro-isostasy or gravitational fingerprint (R1643); the load's depression is local (R1641).
